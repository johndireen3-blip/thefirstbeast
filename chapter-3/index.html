REVELATION 3:01–06

A reputation for life, but asleep

A reputation for life, but asleep.

01To the angel of the church in Sardis: the one who has the seven spirits of God and the seven stars says this. I know your works: you have the reputation of being alive, but you are dead.

02Wake up and strengthen what remains before it dies, for I have not found your works complete in the sight of my God.

03Remember what you received and heard; keep it, and repent. If you do not keep watch, I will come like a thief, at an hour you do not know.

04Yet a few in Sardis have not soiled their garments. They will walk with me in white, for they are worthy.

05The victor will be dressed in white. I will never erase his name from the book of life, and I will acknowledge his name before my Father and his angels.

06Whoever has ears ought to hear what the Spirit says to the churches.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The last three cities lay along a Roman road through the province of Asia, in what is now western Turkey. Each letter draws on what its readers knew about their own city. Sardis was the old capital of Lydia, built around a citadel thought to be unassailable. Twice in its history, under Cyrus and later Antiochus, it fell by surprise because no one was keeping watch. Christ’s warning, “I will come like a thief,” would have stung.

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 3:07–13

Little strength, an open door

Little strength, an open door.

07To the angel of the church in Philadelphia: the holy one, the true, who holds the key of David, who opens and no one closes, who closes and no one opens, says this.

08I know your works. I have set before you an open door that no one can close. You have little strength, yet you have kept my word and have not denied my name.

09Those who have opposed and slandered you will come and bow at your feet, and they will learn that I love you.

10Because you have kept my message of endurance, I will keep you safe in the hour of trial that is coming upon the whole world.

11I am coming quickly. Hold fast to what you have, so that no one takes your crown.

12The victor I will make a pillar in the temple of my God, never to leave it. I will write on him the name of my God, the name of the new Jerusalem coming down from heaven, and my own new name.

13Whoever has ears ought to hear what the Spirit says to the churches.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Philadelphia (modern Alaşehir) was founded as an “open door” for Greek culture into the interior. Shaken by a great earthquake in A.D. 17 and rebuilt, it knew what it meant to need a lasting pillar. The key of David recalls Isaiah 22:22; the USCCB notes cross-reference the keys given to Peter (Mt 16:19). Verse 9 speaks of “those of the assembly of Satan who claim to be Jews and are not.” As in chapter 2, it reflects a local conflict in the first century. The Church firmly rejects any use of such passages against the Jewish people (Vatican II, Nostra Aetate, no. 4), and this guide follows that teaching.

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 3:14–22

Lukewarm, and invited to the table

Lukewarm, and invited to the table.

14To the angel of the church in Laodicea: the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the source of God’s creation, says this.

15I know your works: you are neither cold nor hot. I wish you were one or the other.

16Because you are lukewarm, I will spit you out of my mouth.

17You say, “I am rich and need nothing,” and do not see that you are wretched, pitiable, poor, blind and naked.

18Buy from me gold refined by fire so that you may be truly rich, white garments to cover your nakedness, and ointment for your eyes so that you may see.

19Those whom I love, I correct and discipline. Be earnest, then, and repent.

20Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears my voice and opens the door, I will come in and dine with him, and he with me.

21To the victor I will give a place with me on my throne, as I conquered and sit with my Father on his throne.

22Whoever has ears ought to hear what the Spirit says to the churches.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Laodicea was wealthy, proud of its banks, its medical school, its eye salve and its fine wool, and it rebuilt itself without imperial help after the earthquake of A.D. 60/61. Its water came by aqueduct and may have arrived tepid, unlike the hot springs of nearby Hierapolis or cold mountain water. Christ answers each civic boast: true gold, white garments, ointment for the eyes.

REVELATION 3:4–5

White garments and the book of life

White is the color of victory, joy and resurrection. Those who have kept their baptismal garments unstained walk with Christ, and he will acknowledge their names before his Father, as he promised in the Gospel (Mt 10:32).

Read Matthew 10
02

REVELATION 3:7

The key of David

Isaiah 22:22 gives the key of the royal household to a faithful steward. Christ holds that key; he alone opens and shuts the way into the kingdom, and he shares that authority with Peter (Mt 16:19).

Read Isaiah 22
03

REVELATION 3:8, 12

The open door and the pillar

A small church with little strength is given an open door to proclaim the faith. In a city often shaken by earthquakes, the victor is promised to be a pillar in God’s temple, never to be moved.

Read Revelation 3
04

REVELATION 3:15–16

Lukewarm water

Neither refreshing like cold water nor healing like hot, lukewarm water is simply useless. Laodicea’s danger was not open sin but self-satisfied indifference: “I am rich and need nothing.”

Read Revelation 3
05

REVELATION 3:20–21

The door and the meal

Christ does not force the door; he knocks and waits. The meal he offers points to the messianic banquet (Isa 25:6; Lk 22:30) and, for Catholics, to the Eucharist, a foretaste of the heavenly throne he promises to share.

Read Luke 22
READ WITH CARE

Verse 20 is often read as an invitation to a first conversion. In context it is addressed to baptized members of a church who have grown complacent. Christ knocks on the door of believers, calling them back to fervor and to his table.

01 / 03

REPUTATION & REALITY

Am I more alive in name than in fact?

Sardis looked healthy from the outside. Where am I coasting on past fervor, and what in me needs strengthening before it fades?

REVELATION 3:1–3

WEAKNESS & OPPORTUNITY

What door has been opened to me?

Philadelphia had little strength and was given an open door anyway. Where is Christ inviting me to witness, even if I feel too small for it?

REVELATION 3:8

COMFORT & FERVOR

What makes me think I need nothing?

Laodicea’s wealth dulled its hunger for God. What comfort keeps me lukewarm, and how will I answer the knock at the door this week?

WHERE IT FITS

The letters to the churches.

Chapter 3 completes the seven letters. Each again opens with a title of Christ from chapter 1 (the seven stars, the key, the faithful witness) and closes with a promise “to the victor.” The last promise, a place on Christ’s throne (3:21), leads straight into chapter 4, where John sees an open door to heaven and is called up to see the throne of God. The door Christ knocks on in 3:20 and the door opened in heaven in 4:1 frame the turn from the churches on earth to the worship of heaven.
