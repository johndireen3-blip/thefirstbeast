REVELATION 9:01–12

The first woe.

The first woe.

01The fifth angel blows his trumpet, and John sees a star that has fallen from heaven to earth. It is given the key to the shaft of the abyss.

02It opens the shaft, and smoke pours out like smoke from a great furnace, darkening the sun and the air.

03Out of the smoke come locusts onto the earth, given power like the power of scorpions.

04They are told not to harm the grass, any plant or any tree, but only those people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads.

05They are not allowed to kill them, only to torment them for five months, with pain like a scorpion’s sting.

06In those days people will seek death and not find it; they will long to die, but death will flee from them.

07The locusts look like horses ready for battle. On their heads are what look like gold crowns, and their faces are like human faces.

08They have hair like women’s hair and teeth like lions’ teeth.

09Their chests are like iron breastplates, and the sound of their wings is like the roar of many chariots rushing into battle.

10They have tails with stingers like scorpions, and in their tails is their power to harm people for five months.

11Their king is the angel of the abyss, whose name in Hebrew is Abaddon and in Greek Apollyon.

12The first woe has passed; two more are still to come.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The eagle of 8:13 warned of three woes. Chapter 9 brings the first two, with the fifth and sixth trumpets. The USCCB notes insist that “the imaginative details are not to be taken literally.” John paints evil in its true colors: ugly, tormenting and ultimately self-destructive. The fifth trumpet draws on the plague of locusts and darkness in Egypt (Exod 10) and especially the locust invasion described by the prophet Joel (Joel 1–2). The abyss, in the notes, is the netherworld where Satan and the fallen angels are kept. The locusts are demonic, but they work under strict limits. They cannot touch those who bear the seal of God (9:4; compare 7:3), and they may torment for only “five months,” about the life span of a locust. Their king is named Abaddon and Apollyon, “Destruction” and “Destroyer”: evil’s only work is to ruin. Christ, by contrast, holds the keys of death and the netherworld (1:18).

REVELATION 9:13–19

The second woe.

The second woe.

13The sixth angel blows his trumpet, and John hears a voice from the horns of the gold altar before God

14telling the sixth angel, “Release the four angels who are bound at the great river Euphrates.”

15The four angels, held ready for this very hour, day, month and year, are released to kill a third of humanity.

16The number of the cavalry is two hundred million; John hears their number.

17In the vision the riders wear breastplates of fiery red, deep blue and sulfur yellow. The horses’ heads are like lions’ heads, and fire, smoke and sulfur pour from their mouths.

18By these three plagues, the fire, smoke and sulfur from their mouths, a third of humanity is killed.

19The horses’ power is in their mouths and in their tails, for their tails are like snakes with heads that do harm.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The Euphrates marked the eastern edge of the Roman world, beyond which lay the feared Parthian cavalry (see 6:2). The USCCB notes see in the sixth trumpet “another diabolical attack” symbolized by such an invasion. The huge number, two hundred million, is meant to overwhelm the imagination, not to be counted. Even this army is released only at God’s appointed “hour, day, month, and year.”

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 9:20–21

They did not turn back.

They did not turn back.

20The rest of humanity, who were not killed by these plagues, still do not repent of the works of their hands. They do not stop worshiping demons and idols of gold, silver, bronze, stone and wood, which cannot see or hear or walk.

21Nor do they repent of their murders, their sorcery, their sexual immorality or their thefts.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The chapter ends not with the monsters but with human hearts. The survivors still cling to idols “which cannot see or hear or walk” (compare Ps 135:15–17) and to murder, sorcery, immorality and theft. Like Pharaoh, they harden their hearts. Revelation shows that suffering alone does not convert anyone; only grace does. God, as St. Peter writes, is patient, “not wishing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance” (2 Pet 3:9).

REVELATION 9:1–2

The fallen star and the key

A star fallen from heaven receives a key, but only for a time and only by permission. Compare 20:1, where an angel holds the key and chains Satan: the abyss is always under God’s control.

Read Revelation 20
02

REVELATION 9:3–10

The locusts

Drawn from the locust swarms of Joel 1–2 and the plagues of Egypt, they picture evil as a devouring, tormenting force. Yet they cannot touch anyone who bears God’s seal.

Read Joel 2
03

REVELATION 9:11

Abaddon and Apollyon

Hebrew and Greek for “Destruction” and “Destroyer.” Evil’s king creates nothing; he can only ruin. The Lamb who was slain is the true King, who makes all things new (21:5).

Read Job 26
04

REVELATION 9:14–19

The Euphrates and the horsemen

The great river was Rome’s frontier with the feared Parthians. Fire, smoke and sulfur recall the destruction of Sodom (Gen 19:28). The vast army stands for destruction let loose, limited to God’s appointed hour.

Read Genesis 19
05

REVELATION 9:20

Idols that cannot see or hear

The survivors still worship gods “made from gold, silver, bronze, stone, and wood.” The Psalms mock such idols (Ps 135:15–17). Anything we put in God’s place, whether money, power or pleasure, cannot save.

Read Psalm 135
READ WITH CARE

Some modern writers have identified the locusts as attack helicopters and the two hundred million horsemen as a particular nation’s army. The USCCB notes state plainly that “the imaginative details are not to be taken literally.” Matching these images to modern weapons misses John’s point: evil destroys, God sets its limits, and the urgent question is whether we will repent.

SEAL & SAFETY

Do I trust God’s protection in the dark?

The locusts cannot harm those who bear God’s seal. When evil seems strong, how can I lean on the grace of my Baptism and Confirmation?

REVELATION 9:4

DESTROYER & CREATOR

What in my life only destroys?

Evil’s king is named Destroyer. Which habits, words or influences tear down rather than build up, and what would it take to let them go?

REVELATION 9:11

IDOLS & REPENTANCE

What am I refusing to give up?

Even after the plagues, people cling to their idols. What “work of my hands” have I put in God’s place, and when will I next go to Confession?

WHERE IT FITS

From woes to witness.

Chapter 9 sounds the fifth and sixth trumpets, the first two of the three woes announced in 8:13. As with the seals, the book now pauses before the seventh: chapters 10–11 bring the little scroll and the two witnesses, a reminder that God’s word is still being proclaimed to a world that will not repent. The seventh trumpet (11:15) finally announces that “the kingdom of the world now belongs to our Lord and to his Anointed.” The abyss reappears in chapter 20, when Satan himself is bound there.
