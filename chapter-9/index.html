The first woe.

The first woe.

01The fifth angel blows his trumpet, and John sees a star that has fallen from heaven to earth. It is given the key to the shaft of the abyss.

02It opens the shaft, and smoke pours out like smoke from a great furnace, darkening the sun and the air.

03Out of the smoke come locusts onto the earth, given power like the power of scorpions.

04They are told not to harm the grass, any plant or any tree, but only those people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads.

05They are not allowed to kill them, only to torment them for five months, with pain like a scorpion’s sting.

06In those days people will seek death and not find it; they will long to die, but death will flee from them.

07The locusts look like horses ready for battle. On their heads are what look like gold crowns, and their faces are like human faces.

08They have hair like women’s hair and teeth like lions’ teeth.

09Their chests are like iron breastplates, and the sound of their wings is like the roar of many chariots rushing into battle.

10They have tails with stingers like scorpions, and in their tails is their power to harm people for five months.

11Their king is the angel of the abyss, whose name in Hebrew is Abaddon and in Greek Apollyon.

12The first woe has passed; two more are still to come.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The eagle of 8:13 warned of three woes. Chapter 9 brings the first two, with the fifth and sixth trumpets. The USCCB notes insist that “the imaginative details are not to be taken literally.” John paints evil in its true colors: ugly, tormenting and ultimately self-destructive. The fifth trumpet draws on the plague of locusts and darkness in Egypt (Exod 10) and especially the locust invasion described by the prophet Joel (Joel 1–2). The abyss, in the notes, is the netherworld where Satan and the fallen angels are kept. The locusts are demonic, but they work under strict limits. They cannot touch those who bear the seal of God (9:4; compare 7:3), and they may torment for only “five months,” about the life span of a locust. Their king is named Abaddon and Apollyon, “Destruction” and “Destroyer”: evil’s only work is to ruin. Christ, by contrast, holds the keys of death and the netherworld (1:18).

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The Euphrates marked the eastern edge of the Roman world, beyond which lay the feared Parthian cavalry (see 6:2). The USCCB notes see in the sixth trumpet “another diabolical attack” symbolized by such an invasion. The huge number, two hundred million, is meant to overwhelm the imagination, not to be counted. Even this army is released only at God’s appointed “hour, day, month, and year.”

REVELATION 9:20–21

They did not turn back.

They did not turn back.

20The rest of humanity, who were not killed by these plagues, still do not repent of the works of their hands. They do not stop worshiping demons and idols of gold, silver, bronze, stone and wood, which cannot see or hear or walk.

21Nor do they repent of their murders, their sorcery, their sexual immorality or their thefts.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The chapter ends not with the monsters but with human hearts. The survivors still cling to idols “which cannot see or hear or walk” (compare Ps 135:15–17) and to murder, sorcery, immorality and theft. Like Pharaoh, they harden their hearts. Revelation shows that suffering alone does not convert anyone; only grace does. God, as St. Peter writes, is patient, “not wishing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance” (2 Pet 3:9).

READ WITH CARE

Some modern writers have identified the locusts as attack helicopters and the two hundred million horsemen as a particular nation’s army. The USCCB notes state plainly that “the imaginative details are not to be taken literally.” Matching these images to modern weapons misses John’s point: evil destroys, God sets its limits, and the urgent question is whether we will repent.

WHERE IT FITS

From woes to witness.

Chapter 9 sounds the fifth and sixth trumpets, the first two of the three woes announced in 8:13. As with the seals, the book now pauses before the seventh: chapters 10–11 bring the little scroll and the two witnesses, a reminder that God’s word is still being proclaimed to a world that will not repent. The seventh trumpet (11:15) finally announces that “the kingdom of the world now belongs to our Lord and to his Anointed.” The abyss reappears in chapter 20, when Satan himself is bound there.

04 / GOING DEEPER

What the Fathers said.

The sections above introduce the chapter. This one is for further study: how the Fathers of the Church and the early commentators read it, verse by verse. Read it now, or go on to the next chapter and come back to it.

The Fathers disagree openly about the fifth and sixth trumpets. Tyconius and the Latin writers who follow him, Primasius, St. Caesarius of Arles and St. Bede, see heresy and persecution attacking the Church from within and from without. St. Andrew of Caesarea sees the demons, bound by Christ at his coming, let loose for a time before the end. Oecumenius places the chapter after the resurrection, as the punishment of sinners carried out by holy angels, and Andrew answers him more than once. All of them draw the same lesson from it: the present life is the time for repentance.

01

REVELATION 9:1

The fifth trumpet: the fallen star and the key

Tyconius: the star is the body of the many who have fallen from heaven by agreeing with the devil. The key to the pit is false teaching, which shuts people in so that they cannot look on the light of truth. The devil fell, received this key, opened his mouth in blasphemy, and taught his followers to do their own will and not the will of God (Commentary on the Apocalypse 9.1).

Primasius: the abyss and the pit are people who are hiding places for error: they hide iniquity in their hearts though they are in the Church. With this key they open the poison of their hearts, and it bursts into the open. There have to be factions, the Apostle says, so that those who are approved may become known (1 Corinthians 11:19). Whenever error arises, the truth is preached all the more, and the falsehood long hidden is uncovered at the right time. Schismatics and heretics often arise from those who have fallen, and of them the Church says in the Song of Songs that her mother’s sons were angry with her (Song of Songs 1:6) (Commentary on the Apocalypse 9.1).

St. Bede: the fifth trumpet shows the growing swarm of the heretics, who are the forerunners of the time of the Antichrist (Explanation of the Apocalypse 8.2).

Oecumenius: up to now the vision has shown how earth and heaven and humankind will be brought to completion or changed. Now the end has come and the resurrection has taken place, and the vision turns to the punishment of sinners. The star is an angel of God, so called because he comes down to the earth in brightness. The judgment of sinners will be on the earth, which the prophet calls the Valley of Jehoshaphat (Joel 4:2). The shaft of the abyss is Gehenna (Commentary on the Apocalypse 9.1–4).

St. Andrew of Caesarea: the star is a holy angel. With God’s permission he leads up from the pit the evil demons whom Christ bound at his incarnation, so that they may do their work before the end and then go to unending torment (Commentary on the Apocalypse 9.1–5).

02

REVELATION 9:2

Smoke from the abyss

Tyconius: the devil opened the shaft when he revealed his own heart and taught people to sin without fear or shame. The smoke rises from the heart of a blaspheming people, which also persecutes the Church. The sins that are constantly committed through the world obscure the sun of faith and righteousness, and in some they produce a blindness to the light of truth. Smoke comes before the fire of a furnace. So this points to the last persecution, when the crowd of hypocrites and unbelievers will be shown for what it is (9.2).

St. Caesarius of Arles: the text says that the sun was darkened. It does not say that it fell. The sins of the evil and the proud, constantly committed throughout the world, obscure the Church and sometimes bring darkness on the saints and the righteous. The evil are so many that the good among them are often hard to see (Exposition on the Apocalypse, Homily 7).

Oecumenius: the smoke means fire and also darkness. He reports an interpretation of the psalm verse on the voice of the Lord and the flame of fire (Psalm 29:7), which in his Greek text reads “divides the flame of fire”: the fire of Gehenna will be divided, so that its burning heat and its darkness remain and its light is taken away. The sun and the air were not themselves darkened. Those cast into the shaft were filled with darkness by their punishment and could no longer see them, as the prophet speaks of the sun going down at midday (Amos 8:9) for people overwhelmed by misfortune (9.1–4).

03

REVELATION 9:3

The locusts

Tyconius: the locusts are hostile spiritual powers, which fly about in the air like locusts to harm humankind (9.3).

Oecumenius: he thinks the locusts are the worms of Isaiah: “Their worm shall not die, their fire shall not be extinguished” (Isaiah 66:24). The worm may be the gnawing of the soul, an unceasing and creeping pain (9.1–4).

Primasius: the locusts come out of the smoke. Like smoke, the heretics want to rise high, and by their teachings they strive for positions of eminence. Like smoke they are scattered. The book of Wisdom has the wicked say that the breath in our nostrils is smoke (Wisdom 2:2). They fight against Christ, who is Wisdom, and against his Church, and harm her with malicious mouths. They are kept from harming the green grass and the trees (9.3).

04

REVELATION 9:4

Those without the seal of God

Primasius: the grass is the little ones, content with the milk of the faith. The green growth is those who have begun to live by the virtues and are moving toward fruitfulness. The trees are those in the Church who already bear fruit. He finds the three in the Psalms: the earth spread on the waters, which he takes as Baptism (Psalm 136:6), the land softened with showers (Psalm 65:11), and the olive tree flourishing in the house of God (Psalm 52:10). The number of the elect is made up from all three, and the bite of the locusts cannot harm them. It harms only those who do not have the sign of God on their foreheads, “for not all have faith” (2 Thessalonians 3:2). By such people the sun is darkened and not put out. Though evildoing increases and the love of many grows cold (Matthew 24:12), “God’s solid foundation stands, bearing this inscription, ‘The Lord knows those who are his’” (2 Timothy 2:19) (9.1–4).

Oecumenius: the earth has by now been made new, as the psalm says: “Send forth your spirit, they are created and you renew the face of the earth” (Psalm 104:30). Nothing that adorns it is to be struck, only people without the seal of God. Those who were perfectly holy have their place with Christ, gazing on the throne of God. He then speaks of a middle group: people who were baptized and bear the sign of Christ on their foreheads, and who have seriously soiled their Baptism by sinful conduct. He places them halfway between virtue and evil and has them remain on the earth without further punishment (9.1–4). See the note on Oecumenius below.

05

REVELATION 9:5

Five months of torment

Oecumenius: certain fathers, he says, drew from this passage the idea of a restoration: sinners are punished for a time and then no longer, having been purified by punishment. Most of the fathers and the received Scriptures say that the torments of that time are everlasting. He declares that he agrees with the teaching of the Church that the punishments of the age to come are everlasting, as the Lord says: “these will go off to eternal punishment” (Matthew 25:46), and as Isaiah says of the worm that does not die (Isaiah 66:24). Then, “as a kind of exercise and not as an outright assertion,” he proposes a middle path. For a time, which the Revelation calls “five months” by a mystical number, sinners will be punished with the greatest severity, as by the sting of a scorpion. After that they will be punished less severely, and they will not be without punishment, so that they seek death and do not find it (9.5–6).

A note on Oecumenius. The editor of the volume notes that Oecumenius has in mind the followers of Origen, who held that in the end all would be purified and restored. The Church teaches that hell exists and is eternal, and that its chief punishment is eternal separation from God (Catechism of the Catholic Church 1035). The purification after death that she does teach, purgatory, is for those who die in God’s grace and friendship and are assured of their salvation, and it is entirely different from the punishment of the damned (CCC 1030–1031). Scripture and the Church do not teach that the pains of hell are lessened after a time, or that there is a middle state on the earth for baptized people who have died in grave sin. Oecumenius offers these ideas as an exercise, and he holds to the Church’s teaching against the Origenists.

Caesarius, following Tyconius: there are two parts in the Church, the good and the evil. One is afflicted so that it may be corrected. The other is given up to its own desires. Some of the good are handed over to humiliation so that they may know the righteousness of God and remember penance: “It was good for me to be afflicted, in order to learn your statutes” (Psalm 119:71). The torment like a scorpion’s sting comes when the devil draws near with the poison of sins (Homily 7).

Andrew: that the locusts sting like scorpions shows the death of the soul, which lies hidden at the end of evil deeds. It comes on those who have not signed their foreheads with the divine seal and with the light of the life-giving cross through the Holy Spirit, so that their light may shine before others (Matthew 5:16). The five months are the shortness of the time, since the Lord said that those days would be shortened (Matthew 24:22). Or they are the five senses through which sin enters, or a fixed period known to God alone (9.1–5).

Bede: the heretics are supported by the secular power and are allowed for a time to attack the good. As the Lord said, they cannot kill the soul (Matthew 10:28). The five months are the time of this age, because of the five senses we use in this life. Another translation has “six months,” for the six ages of the world. A scorpion gives out its poison from its tail. So the impiety of the wicked wounds from behind when, by threats and enticements, it makes temporal things, which are behind us, preferred to eternal blessings, which lie ahead (Explanation of the Apocalypse 9.5).

06

REVELATION 9:6

They will seek death and not find it

Tyconius takes the verse in a good sense. People call death a rest. These seek death in order to rest from evil while their vices die away. They want to die to the world and, as the Apostle says, to live for God (Romans 6:11). They desire to be changed for the better and to rest after the labour of sins. We truly die when we are being freed from the chains of sins (9.6).

Andrew: the words show how great the evils are, for people in severe trouble commonly call on death. It is by God’s judgment that death does not come to them. He holds it good to use the bitterness of affliction to make sin hateful to them, since sin is the mother of their torments (9.6).

07

REVELATION 9:7–8

Locusts like horses ready for battle

Tyconius: the horses show the suddenness of a persecution that runs about to oppress the innocent and bar their way to the heavenly kingdom. The twenty-four elders, a figure of the Church, wore crowns of gold. These offer a figure of the Church only by pretence, and so their crowns are only “like gold,” and their faces only “like” human faces. St. Caesarius says plainly that heretics are meant. The hair is the softness of those of either sex who are given to wantonness. With teeth like lions’ teeth they devour the Church daily (9.7–8).

Primasius: the locusts are those who originate evil teachings. Locusts destroy with their mouths, and these tear apart by their preaching, like the “savage wolves” who will not spare the flock (Acts 20:29). They run about like horses and destroy many whom they lead astray. If the mouth is false doctrine, the hair is the soft morals of the leaders and the easily led souls of their followers, as the Apostle describes those who make their way into homes and make captives of people weighed down by sins (2 Timothy 3:6). History shows this of both sexes. Priscilla and Maximilla gave their support to Montanus, and Lucilla gave hers to Donatus. The mouth of a lion gives off a stench. These run after fleeting fame as preachers, and their opinion is rightly called a stench, against the Apostle’s word that we are the aroma of Christ (2 Corinthians 2:15) (9.7–8).

Andrew: some take the locusts to be angels who carry out God’s punishment, and read the images as their fearsomeness, their swiftness and the destruction they bring. He thinks they are evil demons prepared for war against us. They wear crowns “like gold” because they expect to conquer us. When we give in to them and win an evil victory through pleasure, we think that we are crowned as well. The hair shows their love of luxury and their stirring up of fornication. The lions’ teeth show their murderous and poisonous character (9.7–9).

08

REVELATION 9:9–10

Breastplates, wings and tails

Tyconius: the tails are leaders who have fallen away from the Lord, the Head of the Church, so that the first have become the last. Isaiah says that the elder and the noble are the head, and “the prophet who teaches falsehood is the tail” (Isaiah 9:14). The power of the locusts is the character of false prophets, who never stop attacking the true Church with lying and poisonous doctrines (9.10).

Primasius: the noise of the wings like chariots shows that the few who led as horses have been multiplied in the chariots that follow. It means a growing variety of new opinions, or a growing number of people led astray. They rush into battle because the preachers of error rebel against the truth and fight against unity, as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses (2 Timothy 3:8). Their power is in their tails, as Isaiah says of the false prophet (Isaiah 9:14–15). They want to rule by what every saint and teacher of truth avoids: the glory of the world and a destructive preaching. The five months again mean the present life, where falsehood can be strong, and can capture the useless or torment the spiritual for a short time (9.9–10).

Andrew: the iron breastplates are their hardness of heart. The sound of their wings is like war chariots because of their speed in the air, for the psalm, in his Greek text, says that they make war from on high (Psalm 56:3). The tails like scorpions’ are the result of sins, which is spiritual death: “when sin reaches maturity it gives birth to death” (James 1:15) (9.9–12).

09

REVELATION 9:11–12

The angel of the abyss; the first woe

Tyconius: the angel of the abyss is the devil, who holds his great power among the kings of the world (9.11).

Primasius: his Latin text gives the name in Hebrew as “Armageddon,” in Greek as “Apollion,” and in Latin as “Exterminans,” the destroyer. God is supremely good, and by hidden and just judgments he allows such people to have a ruler who suits them, for a person becomes the servant of the one who conquered him. So the Apostle says of those who did not accept the love of truth that God sends them a deceiving power so that they believe what is false (2 Thessalonians 2:10–12). The work of this king fits his name (9.11).

Andrew: the devil is their king, and he destroys those who obey him (9.9–12).

Bede: three woes were foretold. One has already come in the deceit of heresy. Two remain, and they will come against the perverse in the time of the Antichrist and at the day of judgment (9.12).

10

REVELATION 9:13

The sixth trumpet: a voice from the golden altar

Tyconius: the first woe has passed, and the sixth trumpet announces the final preaching, that of the sixth age (9.13).

Bede: the sixth trumpet shows the appearance of the Antichrist and the war his followers will wage on the Church, and then, after a passage that goes back over the time since the Lord’s coming, the destruction of that enemy. The sixth angel is the preachers of the final struggle, who uncover the lies of the Antichrist as the Gospel warns. The horns of the golden altar are the Gospels, which stand above all else in the Church (Explanation of the Apocalypse 8.2; 9.13).

Caesarius: the altar in the sight of God is the Church. In the time of the last persecution she will despise the words and commands of that most inhuman of kings, and will separate herself from those who have submitted to him (Homily 7).

Oecumenius: the horns of the altar are the foremost of the angels. The altar is called golden because it is precious, holy and wonderful. It stands for all the ministering spirits, because of the spiritual sacrifice they bring to God (9.13–19).

11

REVELATION 9:14–15

The four angels bound at the Euphrates

Tyconius: the four angels are the adversaries whom God will order to be loosed for the testing of his Church. They are the same as the winds held back at the four corners of the earth (7:1). The Euphrates stands for a persecuting people, in whom Satan and his will are bound so that he cannot do all he wants before the time. It is the river of Babylon, and the prophets speak of the Lord’s sword by the Euphrates (Jeremiah 46:10) and in Bozrah and Edom (Isaiah 34:6). These were places of Esau, whose persecution drove his brother Jacob to flee (9.14).

Primasius: the loosing of the four angels is the beginning of the persecution. The hour, day, month and year are four times, by which he understands the three years and six months that it lasts. They are called “prepared” because the devil and his angels are always entangled in hostility toward the Church and want to harm her. Divine power restrains them, so that they cannot do as much damage as they wish (9.15).

Bede: there are four angels because the persecution will rage in the four parts of the world. They are the angels seen at the four corners of the earth, held back until the servants of God were sealed. The wicked spirits desire the death of humankind at every hour and moment. They will be allowed to rage more freely so that the Church may be made stronger, and at the proper time they will be destroyed. He asks what such spirits will do when they are released, when they do so much harm while they are bound (9.15).

Oecumenius: Scripture shows the fallen angels bound in darkness (2 Peter 2:4; Jude 6) or cast into the sea, as in Job. There is no tradition that they are bound at the Euphrates, or that they will be released, or that people will be punished through them. St. Jude’s “eternal chains” exclude it. So he takes the four to be holy angels. They are “bound” to the vision of God, which gladdens the soul and is called a river in Scripture (Isaiah 66:12; Psalm 46:5; John 7:38). They are loosed from it and sent to punish the wicked. Perhaps, he says, they are Michael, Gabriel, Uriel and Raphael. (The name Uriel comes from writings outside the canon of Scripture. The Church venerates by name only Michael, Gabriel and Raphael.) The vast cavalry is the invincible power of the angels of God (9.13–19).

Andrew, probably answering Oecumenius: these are the most evil of the demons, bound at the coming of Christ. At a divine command from the heavenly altar, of which the ancient tabernacle was the image, an angel will loose them to trouble the nations. They will fight against Christians and against each other. So some will be shown as approved and faithful, like ripe wheat, and worthy of the best rewards. Others, the wicked and unrepentant, will be justly punished here like chaff, and will receive a harsher sentence at the judgment. It is not strange that they are bound at the Euphrates, for by God’s ordinance some demons were sent for a time to the abyss, some into the swine, and others to other places. Perhaps the mention of the Euphrates indicates that the Antichrist will come from those regions. There is no reason to doubt the multitude of the demons, since all the saints say that the air is full of them (9.13–16).

12

REVELATION 9:16

The number of the cavalry

Primasius: his Latin text gives the number as eighty thousand, and he reads it as the whole body of the wicked. As sixty stands for the good in the Song of Songs, “Sixty are the queens,” so eighty is used in a bad sense. It is built on four, for the four qualities of the body or the four passions of dread, desire, pain and joy, doubled for the two sexes and multiplied by ten. The verse goes on, “eighty the concubines” (Song of Songs 6:8), who are joined by no lawful union. This is the mark of all heresies. They lay hold of the sacraments of faith for a time and are clothed in the name of Christ. Like concubines who do not remain in the house, they want to profit from that name by gaining influence, advantage, authority or human praise (9.16).

Tyconius comments on another translation, “twice ten thousand times ten thousand.” Twice ten thousand is twenty thousand, the number the Lord sets against the king who has ten thousand (Luke 14:31). The duplicity of the wicked stands against the simplicity of Christians. Caesarius and Bede follow this reading.

13

REVELATION 9:17–19

Horses and riders: fire, smoke and sulfur

Oecumenius: the fire is wrath and punishment. The hyacinth blue shows that those who are sent are from heaven, since the sky is that colour. He takes even the sulfur in a good sense, as a sign that they are pleasing to God. The vision then compares the holy angels to lions, fire, smoke and serpents, and all of it shows how fearsome and irresistible they are (9.13–19).

Primasius: the horses are human beings and their riders are evil spirits. Scripture speaks in a good sense of the Lord mounted on his horses and his victorious chariot (Habakkuk 3:8). Here, in an evil sense, people are joined to wicked spirits. Locusts, horses and lions have little in common, and each suits the heretics. The breastplates signify the punishments of the impious, for those who worship the beast are promised fire and sulfur, and “The smoke of the fire that torments them will rise forever and ever” (Revelation 14:11) (9.17).

Andrew: the horses are people ruled by animal lust, or people who have submitted to demons and are governed by them, as a rider governs a horse. The demons use one another as servants, and they use evil people as instruments against others like them. The breastplates of fire, smoke and sulfur are the airy nature of the demons and their destructive work. The lions’ heads are their murderous and beastly nature. The fire, smoke and sulfur from their mouths, by which a third of humankind is killed, are the sins that consume the fruits of the heart through the poisonous suggestions of the demons. Or they are the devastation of cities by fire and bloodshed at the hands of barbarians, which God allows (9.17–19).

Caesarius: the smoke, fire and sulfur are the blasphemies that come from their mouths against God. The tails are the leaders of the heretics, and the heads are the rulers of the world. Through these the devil does his harm, and without them he cannot do it. Irreligious kings do harm by an evil use of authority, and worldly prelates do harm by teaching badly (Homily 7).

14

REVELATION 9:20–21

The rest did not repent

Oecumenius: the psalm says of those in the world of the dead: “in death there is no remembrance of you. Who praises you in Sheol?” (Psalm 6:6). They are deprived of joy in God and of repentance. Joy comes from remembering God, as he reads in his Greek Psalter (Psalm 77:4). Repentance comes through confession, which is the declaration of faults out of sorrow and a conversion that comes from contrition in the soul. God has set two lives before us. The present life is for choosing prudently, and the future life holds the reward for how one has lived. The present life is not the time of judgment, and the future life is not a time for works by which we might change for the better. So the words “did not repent” are said of those who are still living and have not turned from their evil deeds. He ends with a prayer that all of us may remain free of such things by the grace and goodness of our Lord Jesus Christ (9.20–21).

Andrew: the rest were spared and still did not repent, and so they will suffer the same things. The universal wrath comes from idolatry, murder, fornication, theft and magic. These deceits bring a frenzy on the nations that do not acknowledge the truth and that worship idols and the creature in place of the Creator. They are found also among those who profess to know God and deny him by their works, who wear the form of piety and deny its power. He prays that we may show the purity of our faith in Christ by deeds, so that we never hear the words “I do not know you” (Matthew 25:12) and “Depart from me, all you evildoers!” (Luke 13:27), and may hear the invitation: “Come, you who are blessed by my Father. Inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world” (Matthew 25:34) (9.20–21).

Bede: having described the false Christians and the heretics, John now completes the picture of the whole body of the devil with the error of the pagans. It did not profit them to be spared by these plagues, since they continue in their wickedness. That persecution will not force them to consent, and they will die in their unbelief (9.20).
