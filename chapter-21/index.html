REVELATION 21:01–08

God’s dwelling is with us.

God’s dwelling is with us.

01John sees a new heaven and a new earth. The first heaven and the first earth have passed away, and the sea is no more.

02He sees the holy city, a new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared like a bride adorned for her husband.

03He hears a loud voice from the throne: Look, God’s dwelling is with humanity. He will live with them; they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God.

04He will wipe every tear from their eyes. There will be no more death, no more mourning, crying or pain, for the former things have passed away.

05The one seated on the throne says: See, I am making all things new. And he says: Write this down, for these words are trustworthy and true.

06He tells John: It is done. I am the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end. To the thirsty I will give water freely from the spring of life.

07The one who conquers will inherit these things; I will be his God, and he will be my son.

08But the cowardly, the unfaithful, the depraved, murderers, the sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters and all liars will have their share in the lake burning with fire and sulfur, which is the second death.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The visions of judgment are over, and the book turns to the new creation (Isa 65:17; 66:22). The USCCB notes explain that the sea disappears because it was the home of the dragon and the source of the beast (13:1): nothing hostile to God remains. The holy city is the Bride, which the notes identify with the Church. Verse 3 fulfills the covenant promise repeated through the whole Bible, “I will be their God, and they will be my people” (Lev 26:11–12; Ezek 37:27), and the word for “dwelling” recalls the tabernacle and John 1:14, “the Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us.” Verse 4 takes up Isaiah 25:8. The notes add that God’s reign has already begun: “It is done.” The “cowardly” of verse 8, the notes explain, are those whose faith gives way so that they deny Christ under trial: the new creation is a gift, but it can be refused.

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 21:09–21

The Bride, the wife of the Lamb.

The Bride, the wife of the Lamb.

09One of the seven angels who held the seven bowls of the last plagues comes and says: Come, I will show you the Bride, the wife of the Lamb.

10He carries John in the spirit to a great, high mountain and shows him the holy city Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God,

11shining with the glory of God. Its radiance is like a precious jewel, like jasper, clear as crystal.

12It has a great, high wall with twelve gates, and twelve angels at the gates; on the gates are written the names of the twelve tribes of Israel.

13There are three gates on the east, three on the north, three on the south and three on the west.

14The city wall has twelve foundation stones, and on them are the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb.

15The angel who speaks with John has a golden measuring rod to measure the city, its gates and its wall.

16The city is laid out as a square, its length equal to its width. He measures it with the rod: about fifteen hundred miles; its length, width and height are all equal.

17He measures its wall: one hundred and forty-four cubits, by the measure a human being uses, which the angel also uses.

18The wall is built of jasper, and the city is pure gold, clear as glass.

19The foundations of the city wall are decorated with every kind of precious stone: the first jasper, the second sapphire, the third chalcedony, the fourth emerald,

20the fifth sardonyx, the sixth carnelian, the seventh chrysolite, the eighth beryl, the ninth topaz, the tenth chrysoprase, the eleventh hyacinth, the twelfth amethyst.

21The twelve gates are twelve pearls, each gate made from a single pearl, and the city’s street is pure gold, transparent as glass.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The same angel who showed John the harlot Babylon (17:1) now shows him the Bride: the false city and the true one are deliberately set side by side. The USCCB notes explain that this whole section (21:9–22:5) is symbolic and draws heavily on Ezekiel’s vision of the restored temple and city (Ezek 40–48). The names of Israel’s twelve tribes on the gates and of the twelve apostles on the foundations show one People of God across both covenants; the notes cite Ephesians 2:20, the Church “built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets.” The city is a perfect cube, like the Holy of Holies in Solomon’s temple (1 Kgs 6:20): the whole city has become the place of God’s presence. Its measures are multiples of twelve: 12,000 stadia, 144 cubits. The notes read 12,000 as twelve times a thousand, the immense number of believers, and the gold and jewels as the beauty and excellence of the Church. The twelve stones recall the high priest’s breastplate (Exod 28:17–21).

REVELATION 21:22–27

No temple, no night.

No temple, no night.

22John sees no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God almighty and the Lamb.

23The city needs no sun or moon to shine on it, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb.

24The nations will walk by its light, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it.

25Its gates will never be shut by day, and there will be no night there.

26They will bring into it the glory and honor of the nations.

27Nothing unclean will enter it, nor anyone who does what is detestable or false, but only those whose names are written in the Lamb’s book of life.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

In Ezekiel’s vision the temple was the center of everything. In John’s there is no temple at all, because, the USCCB notes explain, Christ is present throughout the Church and no earthly temple is needed: God and the Lamb are the temple. The Lamb as lamp recalls Jesus’ words, “I am the light of the world” (John 8:12), and Isaiah’s promise that the Lord would be his people’s everlasting light (Isa 60:19–20). The kings who once committed immorality with Babylon now bring their glory to the true city (Isa 60:3, 11). The notes see in this that all people of good will are welcome, while nothing false or unclean can enter. Only those written in the Lamb’s book of life belong there.

THE BRIDE, NOT THE HARLOT
01

REVELATION 21:1

No more sea

In the Bible the sea is the realm of chaos and of the monsters that oppose God (Ps 74:13; Dan 7:2–3). The beast rose from the sea in 13:1. In the new creation nothing threatening remains.

Read Isaiah 65
02

REVELATION 21:3

God’s dwelling with humanity

The tabernacle in the desert, the temple in Jerusalem, and the Word made flesh who “made his dwelling among us” (John 1:14) all point to this: God living with his people, face to face, forever (CCC 1045).

Read Ezekiel 37
03

REVELATION 21:2, 9

The new Jerusalem, the Bride

The holy city and the Bride are the same: the Church in glory. The Catechism describes the Church in heaven as the Bride of the Lamb, without spot or wrinkle (CCC 1045, 1138; Eph 5:27).

Read Isaiah 62
04

REVELATION 21:12–14

Twelve gates, twelve foundations

Israel’s tribes on the gates, the Lamb’s apostles on the foundations: one People of God, Old and New Covenant together, built on the apostles with Christ the cornerstone (Eph 2:19–22).

Read Ephesians 2
05

REVELATION 21:16

A perfect cube

The only cube in Scripture is the Holy of Holies, where God’s glory dwelt (1 Kgs 6:20). The whole city is now the inner sanctuary: no veil, no distance, God’s presence everywhere.

Read 1 Kings 6
READ WITH CARE

Some readers calculate the city’s dimensions, about 1,500 miles on each side, as the literal size of a future space city or a rebuilt Jerusalem, or treat the gold and jewels as a description of heaven’s architecture. The USCCB notes read the measures and materials as symbols: twelve and its multiples for the complete People of God, the cube for perfection, the gold and gems for the Church’s beauty. Nor is the “new earth” an escape from the material world. The Church teaches that God will not discard creation but renew it, and that our bodies will be raised (CCC 1042–1047).

TEARS & HOPE

What tears do I need God to wipe away?

God himself will wipe every tear. What grief am I carrying, for someone I have lost or something I have suffered, that I can entrust to that promise today?

REVELATION 21:4

NEW & RENEWAL

What does God want to make new in me?

“Behold, I make all things new.” Is there a relationship, habit or wound I have given up on? Can I ask God to begin his new creation there?

LIGHT & WELCOME

Does my parish look like the city with open gates?

The city’s gates are never shut, and the nations bring their glory in. Who is on the edge of my parish or family, and how could I help them feel welcome?

WHERE IT FITS

From witness to reign.

Chapter 21 is the destination of the whole book. Every threat has been removed: Babylon (chapters 17–18), the beast and false prophet (chapter 19), the dragon and death itself (chapter 20). What remains is the Bride announced at the wedding feast (19:7–9), now revealed as the holy city. The promises made to the victors in the letters to the churches (chapters 2–3), the new name, the white garments, the pillar in God’s temple, are all fulfilled here. The vision continues into chapter 22 with the river and tree of life, closing the story the Bible began in the garden of Genesis.

04 / GOING DEEPER

What the Fathers said.

The sections above introduce the chapter. This one is for further study: how the Fathers of the Church and the early commentators read it, verse by verse. Read it now, or go on to the next chapter and come back to it.

This is a summary. The Fathers on chapter 21 are set out in full on The Woman and the Ark: The Bride, the Wife of the Lamb and The City, the Temple and the Garden, which gives the full table of the twelve stones.

01

REVELATION 21:1–4

A new heaven and a new earth

Renewed, not replaced. On this the Fathers cited give one answer. Oecumenius reads the verse with St. Paul: creation will be set free from its slavery to corruption (Romans 8:19–21). St. Augustine: what burns away is the corruptible quality of things, so that a world made new may suit bodies made new. Cassiodorus: a man is still himself when he rises, and so are heaven and earth.

The new Jerusalem coming down. Augustine: she comes down from heaven because God makes her by heavenly grace, and her descent began with time itself, as her citizens are born through the bath of rebirth. Apringius of Beja: the city is the multitude of the saints.

No more tears. Tertullian argues from causes: where could sorrow and death come from in the presence of God, with the devil cast out? Primasius adds a point for now: here, the holier a person is, the more he weeps in prayer.

02

REVELATION 21:5–9

“I make all things new”

The Alpha and the Omega. St. Andrew of Caesarea: first by his divinity and last by his humanity, and his care reaches from the first creature to the last.

A gift. Oecumenius asks how the water can be free when the saints reach it through such hardship. It is free because no labour is worth what is given (Romans 8:18).

03

REVELATION 21:10–21

The city, its gates and foundations

The wall and the gates. The wall is Christ, all say. The gates are the apostles, through whom we came to the faith; Apringius adds that Christ alone is the door (John 10:9). Three gates on each side: the four quarters of the world have received faith in the Trinity.

Twelve foundations, one foundation. Primasius explains how there can be twelve when St. Paul says there is one (1 Corinthians 3:11): the apostles are foundations in Christ, as they are the light of the world in him.

The stones and the pearls. Andrew assigns each stone to an apostle; St. Bede reads each as a grace of the Christian life. Each gate is a single pearl, and the one pearl is Christ, from whom the apostles have their brightness.

04

REVELATION 21:22–27

No temple, no night

No temple. Apringius: a temple was given so that people might reach by faith what cannot be seen. Where God shows himself openly, none is wanted. Oecumenius: we know now as in a mirror, then face to face (1 Corinthians 13:12).

Nothing unclean. Apringius: no one enters who refuses to be cleansed of sin, or who, once cleansed and soiled again, refuses the washing of humility and tears.
