REVELATION 12:01–06

A woman in glory, a dragon in wait

A great sign appears in the sky.

01A great sign appears in the sky: a woman clothed with the sun, the moon beneath her feet, and a crown of twelve stars on her head.

02She is with child and cries out in the pain of giving birth.

03A second sign appears: a huge red dragon with seven heads, ten horns, and seven crowns.

04Its tail sweeps a third of the stars to earth. It stands before the woman, ready to devour her child at birth.

05She gives birth to a son destined to rule the nations with an iron rod. Her child is caught up to God and his throne.

06The woman flees into the desert, to a place God has prepared, where she is cared for 1,260 days.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The USCCB notes read the woman as God’s people of both Testaments: Israel, who gives birth to the Messiah, and the Church, persecuted after him. Her sun, moon and twelve stars recall Joseph’s dream (Gen 37:9). Catholic tradition also sees in her Mary, the Mother of the Messiah, the new Eve of Genesis 3:15. The Church reads 11:19 and 12:1 together on the Solemnity of the Assumption: the ark of the covenant appears in God’s temple, and then the Woman clothed with the sun. Catholic tradition sees in Mary the new Ark, who carried the Word made flesh (see Chapter 11).

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 12:07–12

The accuser is thrown down

War breaks out in heaven.

07War breaks out in heaven: Michael and his angels fight the dragon and its angels.

08The dragon loses, and there is no longer any place for it in heaven.

09The great dragon, the ancient serpent called the Devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world, is thrown down to earth with its angels.

10A loud voice in heaven proclaims that salvation, power and the kingdom of God have come, because the accuser who accused our brothers day and night has been cast out.

11They conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony; love for life did not keep them from death.

12Heaven rejoices, but woe to earth and sea: the Devil has come down in great fury, knowing his time is short.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Michael, whose name means “Who is like God?”, is Israel’s protector in Daniel 12:1. The victory belongs to the Lamb’s blood and the martyrs’ testimony: the dragon is defeated before the beasts of chapter 13 ever appear. ‘They conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony’ (12:11). This is the Lamb ‘that seemed to have been slain’ (5:6), in whose blood the great multitude washed their robes white (7:14).

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 12:13–18

The woman protected, her children targeted

The dragon pursues the woman.

13Thrown down to earth, the dragon pursues the woman who gave birth to the male child.

14She is given the two wings of the great eagle to fly to her place in the desert, where she is cared for “a time, times, and half a time,” far from the serpent.

15The serpent spews a torrent of water after her to sweep her away.

16The earth helps the woman: it opens its mouth and swallows the flood.

17Enraged, the dragon goes off to wage war on the rest of her offspring, those who keep God’s commandments and bear witness to Jesus.

18It takes its stand on the sand of the sea, where the next chapter’s beast is about to rise.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

God carried Israel “on eagles’ wings” out of Egypt (Exod 19:4), and the earth swallowed the pursuers (Exod 15:12). The Church is protected through her trial, not spared from it, for a limited season. Her 1,260 days are the same span as the two witnesses’ prophecy (11:3) and the beast’s forty-two months (13:5): a limited season of trial in which God both protects his people and sends them out to bear witness.

01

REVELATION 12:1–2

The woman clothed with the sun

Sun, moon and twelve stars recall Joseph’s dream of Israel (Gen 37:9). She is God’s people, Israel and the Church, and the Church sees in her Mary, Mother of the Messiah.

Read Genesis 37
02

REVELATION 12:3–4, 9

The red dragon

“The ancient serpent” of Genesis 3 and the sea monster Leviathan (Isa 27:1). Its seven diadems claim rule over the world, a counterfeit of Christ’s many diadems (19:12).

Read Genesis 3
03

REVELATION 12:5

The child caught up to God

The Messiah of Psalm 2, who rules the nations with an iron rod. “Caught up to God” points to the Ascension: the dragon cannot touch him.

Read Psalm 2
04

REVELATION 12:7–12

Michael and the war in heaven

Michael, whose name means “Who is like God?”, is Israel’s protector in Daniel (Dan 12:1). The victory belongs to the Lamb’s blood and the martyrs’ testimony (12:11).

Read Daniel 12
05

REVELATION 12:6, 14–16

Eagle’s wings and the desert

God carried Israel “on eagles’ wings” out of Egypt (Exod 19:4), and the earth swallowed the pursuers (Exod 15:12). The Church is protected for a limited time: 1,260 days.

Forty-Two Months
READ WITH CARE

The USCCB notes read the woman as God’s people, Israel and the Church; Catholic tradition also sees Mary. Benedict XVI summed it up: she represents “at the same time Our Lady and the Church.” This guide presents both, as the Church does, and does not use the chapter’s signs to predict events.

04 / GOING DEEPER

What the Fathers said.

The sections above introduce the chapter. This one is for further study: how the Fathers of the Church and the early commentators read it, verse by verse. Read it now, or come back to it.

This is a summary. Chapter 12 is the subject of the sister site, The Woman and the Ark, where the Fathers are set out page by page, beginning with A Woman Clothed with the Sun and Who Is the Woman?.

01

REVELATION 12:1–2

The Woman

The Church. This is the reading of most of the Fathers. St. Methodius: our mother the Church, clothed with the brightness of the Word, in labour to bring forth natural people as spiritual. Tyconius: the Church, who in Baptism puts on Christ, the sun of righteousness (Galatians 3:27). St. Victorinus: the ancient Church of the patriarchs, prophets and apostles.

Mary. Two name her. Oecumenius says plainly that the vision speaks of the mother of our Saviour. St. Quodvultdeus of Carthage, about 430: “that woman signifies the Virgin Mary,” and he adds at once that she is a figure of the Church.

The birth pangs. Tyconius: the Church is with child “not in her womb but in her mind,” and never stops being in labour, as St. Paul was with the Galatians (Galatians 4:19). Oecumenius, reading Mary, notes that Isaiah says she gave birth before she was in labour (Isaiah 66:7), and takes the pain of the vision in another sense.

02

REVELATION 12:3–6

The dragon and the child

The dragon. Oecumenius: called a dragon for his deceit and red for his thirst for blood. The heads and horns are kings and kingdoms (Tyconius) or powers of wickedness set against the seven gifts of the Spirit (St. Andrew of Caesarea). The third of the stars are the angels who fell with him, or members of the Church dragged down by false teachers.

The male child. Oecumenius reads Christ alone, whom Herod sought to kill. Tyconius and Primasius read Christ and his body: the Church is always bringing forth Christ in her members, and the devil is always seeking to devour whoever is born to God.

03

REVELATION 12:7–12

War in heaven

When is the war? The Fathers give three answers. At the beginning, when Satan fell (Oecumenius, with Luke 10:18). At the Cross, when “the ruler of this world” was condemned (Andrew, John 16:11). Now, in the Church, where each of the faithful contends with the powers of evil (Primasius, following Tyconius, Ephesians 6:12).

The accuser cast out. Oecumenius marvels that the angels call human beings their brothers. St. Caesarius of Arles: the saints are the heaven from which the devil is cast out. “His time is short”: short beside the unending ages that follow.

04

REVELATION 12:13–17

The Woman in the desert

Read of Mary. St. Epiphanius of Salamis is the first known to raise the question, in defending her perpetual virginity. Oecumenius reads the eagle’s wings of the flight into Egypt.

Read of the Church. The two wings are the two Testaments (Primasius, St. Bede), and the desert is our present exile, while we are away from the Lord (2 Corinthians 5:6). “A time, times and half a time” is the whole time of the Church.

The rest of her offspring. Oecumenius: the faithful are the brothers of the Lord, and so her children. Bede: to keep the commandments of God by faith in Jesus Christ is already to be at war with the devil.

WHERE IT FITS

From the ark to the beast.

Chapter 12 opens just as the seventh trumpet ends chapter 11 with the ark of the covenant revealed in heaven (11:19), and it closes with the dragon on the seashore, waiting for its ally (12:18). In chapter 13 the dragon gives its power to the beast from the sea, and the war on “the rest of her offspring” (12:17) becomes the persecution of the holy ones (13:7). The dragon’s “short time” (12:12) is the same limited season as the beast’s forty-two months.
