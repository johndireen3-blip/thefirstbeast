REVELATION 14:01–05

Marked with his name.

Marked with his name.

01John looks and sees the Lamb standing on Mount Zion, and with him the 144,000, who carry the Lamb’s name and his Father’s name on their foreheads.

02A sound comes from heaven like a roaring flood and rolling thunder, yet also like harpists playing their harps.

03Before the throne, the four living creatures and the elders, they sing a new song that no one can learn except the 144,000 redeemed from the earth.

04They have kept themselves pure, like virgins; they follow the Lamb wherever he goes. They were redeemed as the first fruits of humanity for God and the Lamb.

05No lie is found on their lips; they stand without blemish.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Chapter 14 answers chapter 13. The beast marked its followers on the hand or forehead; here the Lamb’s people bear his name and the Father’s. The USCCB notes explain that Mount Zion was where the faithful remnant was expected to gather in the messianic age, and that the 144,000, as in chapter 7, stand for the whole Christian people. “Virgins” is a metaphor: in the prophets, idolatry is called adultery, so these are the ones who refused to worship the beast. “First fruits” is a harvest word, and it prepares for the harvest at the end of the chapter.

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 14:06–13

Fear God and give him glory.

Fear God and give him glory.

06Another angel flies high overhead, carrying an everlasting gospel to proclaim to every nation, tribe, language and people on earth.

07He cries out: Fear God and give him glory, for the hour of his judgment has come. Worship the one who made heaven and earth, the sea and the springs of water.

08A second angel follows: Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great, who made all the nations drink the wine of her passionate immorality.

09A third angel follows, crying out: Anyone who worships the beast and its image, or takes its mark on forehead or hand,

10will drink the wine of God’s fury, poured undiluted into the cup of his anger, and will be tormented with fire and sulfur before the holy angels and the Lamb.

11The smoke of their torment rises forever; those who worship the beast and its image, or take the mark of its name, have no rest day or night.

12Here is the call for the endurance of the holy ones, who keep God’s commandments and their faith in Jesus.

13John hears a voice from heaven: Write this down: blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on. The Spirit agrees: they will rest from their labors, for their works go with them.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Three angels deliver God’s last appeal before the final judgments. The first offers an “everlasting gospel”: the good news, the USCCB notes say, that God’s eternal reign is about to begin, with a call to worship the Creator. The second announces the fall of Babylon, the book’s name for Rome (Isa 21:9), before chapters 17–18 describe it. The third warns that loyalty to the beast ends in God’s wrath. The language of fire and smoke comes from the prophets (Isa 34:9–10). Then comes the second of Revelation’s seven beatitudes. The notes stress that alongside faith in Jesus, John insists on good works: in Jewish thought a person’s deeds follow them as witnesses before God.

REVELATION 14:14–20

The earth is ripe.

The earth is ripe.

14John looks and sees a white cloud, and seated on it one like a son of man, with a golden crown on his head and a sharp sickle in his hand.

15Another angel comes out of the temple and calls to him: Put in your sickle and reap, for the hour has come and the earth’s harvest is ripe.

16The one on the cloud swings his sickle over the earth, and the earth is harvested.

17Another angel comes out of the temple in heaven, he too with a sharp sickle.

18Then the angel who has charge of the fire comes from the altar and calls to him: Put in your sharp sickle and gather the clusters from the vine of the earth, for its grapes are ripe.

19The angel swings his sickle, gathers the earth’s vintage, and throws it into the great wine press of God’s fury.

20The wine press is trodden outside the city, and blood flows from it as high as a horse’s bridle for some two hundred miles.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Two harvests close the chapter. The USCCB notes read the grain harvest, reaped by “one like a son of man” (Dan 7:13; Rev 1:13), as the gathering of the elect at the final judgment, as in Jesus’ parables (Matt 13:30, 39). The grape harvest pictures the judgment of the ungodly, drawn from Joel 4:13 and Isaiah 63:1–6, and it is carried out in chapter 19 when the rider treads the wine press. The “two hundred miles” is literally 1,600 stadia, four squared times a hundred: a symbolic number for the whole land. The point is not the volume of blood but the completeness of God’s justice.

HIS NAME, NOT THE BEAST’S
01

REVELATION 14:1

Mount Zion

Zion is the mountain of God’s city and the place of his chosen king (Ps 2:6). Joel promised that on Mount Zion there would be survivors (Joel 3:5). Here the Lamb stands there with his people, safe in the middle of the beast’s war.

Read Psalm 2
02

REVELATION 14:1

The name on the forehead

The beast’s mark in chapter 13 is a parody of this seal. To bear the Lamb’s name is to belong to him. The Church sees in it the seal of Baptism, which marks the Christian as Christ’s own (CCC 1274).

Read our page on the mark of the beast
03

REVELATION 14:3

The new song

A new song in the Psalms celebrates God’s fresh act of salvation (Ps 96:1; 98:1). Only the redeemed can learn this one, because it is learned by living it: following the Lamb wherever he goes.

Read Psalm 98
04

REVELATION 14:4

First fruits

Under the Law, the first and best of the harvest belonged to God (Exod 23:19). The redeemed are offered to God as the beginning of a much greater harvest, the whole of humanity he wills to save.

Read James 1
05

REVELATION 14:14–20

The sickle and the wine press

Joel saw the nations gathered for judgment as a ripe harvest and a full wine press (Joel 4:13). Jesus spoke of the harvest at the end of the age (Matt 13:39). Both images say that history is ripening toward God’s judgment.

Read Joel 4
READ WITH CARE

Some groups teach that only 144,000 people will be saved, or that the 144,000 are a special class set apart from other believers. The USCCB notes read them, as in chapter 7, as a symbol of the whole Christian people, twelve times twelve times a thousand. The “river of blood” of verse 20 is likewise symbolic: it is a picture of complete judgment, not a forecast of a battlefield.

WHERE IT FITS

From witness to reign.

Chapter 14 is God’s answer to chapter 13. Against the beast and its marked followers stand the Lamb and those who bear his name. Its three angels preview the rest of the book: the call to worship the Creator, the fall of Babylon (chapters 17–18), and the judgment of the beast’s followers (chapters 19–20). Its two harvests close the visions of the conflict that began in chapter 12, and the next chapter opens the final series of seven, the bowls.

04 / GOING DEEPER

What the Fathers said.

The sections above introduce the chapter. This one is for further study: how the Fathers of the Church and the early commentators read it, verse by verse. Read it now, or go on to the next chapter and come back to it.

This is a summary. The Fathers on chapter 14 are set out in full on The Woman and the Ark: The Lamb on Mount Zion, Three Angels and The Harvest and the Winepress.

01

REVELATION 14:1–5

The Lamb on Mount Zion

Who stands with the Lamb? St. Victorinus and Oecumenius think of Israel coming to faith at the end (Romans 11:25–26). St. Andrew of Caesarea: the mountain is “not the ancient city, but the new city of the living God.” St. Bede reads the Church, and marks the contrast: the beast stands on the sand of the sea, the Lamb on Mount Zion.

Who are the “virgins”? St. Methodius, St. Cyprian, St. Jerome and St. Augustine read consecrated virgins. St. Caesarius of Arles reads the whole Church that holds to the pure faith (2 Corinthians 11:2). Augustine will not let anyone conclude that the Lamb’s company is closed: the Lord’s garden holds “the lilies of virgins, and the ivy of married people and the violets of widows.”

02

REVELATION 14:6–7

The eternal gospel

The angel in mid-heaven. Victorinus: Elijah. Primasius: the Church’s preachers, one messenger because the Church is one. Oecumenius: an angel of high rank.

Whom to fear. Andrew: fear God, and not the Antichrist, whose power lasts only a short time and cannot kill the soul.

03

REVELATION 14:8–13

Babylon fallen; the blessed dead

What “Babylon” means. Tyconius: the city and people of the devil, one city made of all the nations that error has made drunk. Oecumenius: the confusion of this present life. Andrew widens the wine to all sin.

Why “fallen,” when she still stands? Tyconius: Scripture often speaks of what is to come as already past, because what it foretells is certain.

04

REVELATION 14:14–20

The harvest and the winepress

The one on the white cloud is Christ, for all the Fathers cited. The cloud is his mother, on whom the Lord rode when he took flesh (Oecumenius); or his own flesh (Primasius, Bede); or his body the Church, white after persecution (Caesarius).

One harvest or two? Oecumenius: two, the grain for the just and sinners together, the vintage for the gravest transgressors. Caesarius and Bede: one, told twice, since the Church is both the Lord’s field and his vineyard.

The blood and the sixteen hundred stadia. The Fathers do not take these as measurements.
