Full text & notes on USCCB
REVELATION 16:01–09

You are just, O Holy One.

You are just, O Holy One.

01A loud voice from the temple tells the seven angels: Go, pour out the seven bowls of God’s wrath on the earth.

02The first angel pours his bowl on the land, and painful, ugly sores break out on everyone who bears the beast’s mark and worships its image.

03The second pours his bowl into the sea; it turns to blood like that of a dead body, and every living thing in the sea dies.

04The third pours his bowl into the rivers and springs, and they too become blood.

05John hears the angel of the waters say: You are just, Holy One, who are and who were, in judging this way.

06They poured out the blood of the holy ones and the prophets, and you have given them blood to drink; they have earned it.

07And he hears the altar answer: Yes, Lord God almighty, your judgments are true and just.

08The fourth angel pours his bowl on the sun, and it is allowed to scorch people with fire.

09People are seared by the heat and curse the name of God, who has power over these plagues, but they do not repent and give him glory.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The bowls are the last of Revelation’s three series of seven. The USCCB notes explain that, like the seals and trumpets, they bring a succession of disasters modeled in part on the plagues of Egypt (Exod 7–12): sores like the sixth plague, water turned to blood like the first. The trumpets struck a third of creation; the bowls strike the whole, because this is the final warning. In the middle of the plagues comes a hymn: the angel of the waters and the altar itself confirm that God’s judgments are just. The altar is where the martyrs cried out “How long?” (6:9–10). Their prayer is being answered.

REVELATION 16:10–16

Blessed is the one who watches.

Blessed is the one who watches.

10The fifth angel pours his bowl on the throne of the beast, and its kingdom is plunged into darkness. People gnaw their tongues in pain

11and curse the God of heaven for their pains and sores, but they do not turn from what they have done.

12The sixth angel pours his bowl on the great river Euphrates, and its water dries up to open a road for the kings from the east.

13John sees three unclean spirits like frogs come out of the mouths of the dragon, the beast and the false prophet.

14They are demonic spirits that work signs, and they go out to the kings of the whole world to gather them for battle on the great day of God the almighty.

15(Look, I am coming like a thief. Blessed is the one who stays awake and keeps his clothes on, so that he does not go about naked and his shame is seen.)

16And they gather the kings at the place called in Hebrew Armageddon.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The fifth bowl strikes the throne of the beast, which the USCCB notes call a symbol of the forces of evil, with darkness like the ninth plague of Egypt. The dried Euphrates opens the way for “the kings of the East,” the Parthians, Rome’s feared enemies beyond the river. The frogs recall the second plague (Exod 8), and the “false prophet” is the second beast of chapter 13. Then, in the middle of the gathering storm, Christ himself breaks in with a beatitude: stay awake. The notes link it to his warnings in the Gospels (Matt 24:42–44) and to 1 Thessalonians 5:2. Armageddon means “Mountain of Megiddo.” Megiddo saw many decisive battles in Israel’s history (Judg 5:19; 2 Kgs 23:29), and so, the notes explain, it became the symbol of the final rout of the forces of evil.

REVELATION 16:17–21

It is done.

It is done.

17The seventh angel pours his bowl into the air, and a loud voice comes from the temple, from the throne: It is done.

18There are flashes of lightning, rumblings and thunder, and an earthquake so violent that nothing like it has happened since people have lived on the earth.

19The great city splits into three parts and the cities of the nations fall. God remembers Babylon the great and gives her the cup of the wine of his furious wrath.

20Every island flees and the mountains disappear.

21Enormous hailstones, each weighing about a hundred pounds, fall from the sky on people, and they curse God for the plague of hail, so terrible is it.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The seventh bowl is poured into the air, the realm the ancients associated with evil powers, and the voice from the throne declares “It is done.” Readers of John’s Gospel hear an echo of Jesus’ last word from the Cross, “It is finished” (John 19:30): the judgment completes what the Cross already won. The earthquake, the fleeing islands and the hail repeat the signs that closed the seals (6:12–14) and the trumpets (11:19), with the hail like the seventh plague of Egypt. The USCCB notes identify “the great city” with Rome and its empire, whose fall is described in detail in chapters 17–18. The tragedy of the chapter is repeated three times: people curse God and do not repent.

REVELATION 16:2–21

The plagues of Egypt

Sores, blood, darkness, frogs and hail: the bowls retell the Exodus plagues (Exod 7–12). Pharaoh hardened his heart; so do those who curse God here. The point is the same: God frees his people and exposes the false powers that enslave them.

Read Exodus 7
02

REVELATION 16:5–7

The angel of the waters and the altar

Creation and the sanctuary together affirm that God is just. The altar, where the martyrs’ souls rest (6:9), answers “Yes.” God’s judgments are not arbitrary; they answer the blood of the innocent.

Read Chapter 6
03

REVELATION 16:13

Three unclean spirits like frogs

The unholy trinity, the dragon, the beast and the false prophet, speak and their words become spirits that deceive the nations. Lies are the enemy’s weapon, from the serpent in Eden (Gen 3) to propaganda in every age.

Read our page on the second beast
04

REVELATION 16:15

Like a thief

Jesus’ own image for his unexpected return (Matt 24:43; Rev 3:3). Keeping one’s clothes on recalls the white garments of the faithful (3:18): stay ready, clothed in faith and good works.

Read Matthew 24
05

REVELATION 16:16

Armageddon

Har Megiddo, the Mountain of Megiddo. Kings fell at Megiddo, including good King Josiah (2 Kgs 23:29). In Revelation it is a symbol of the final defeat of evil, not a map reference: the battle is announced here and fought in 19:19–21.

Read 2 Kings 23
READ WITH CARE

Armageddon has become a byword for a literal world war fought in the valley of Megiddo in northern Israel, and some popular teachers have linked it to whatever conflict fills the news. The USCCB notes read it as a symbol: because so many decisive battles were fought at Megiddo, the name stands for the final rout of the forces of evil. Notice, too, that no battle is actually described here. The kings gather, and the outcome is told in chapter 19, where they are defeated by the word of Christ.

WHERE IT FITS

From witness to reign.

Chapter 16 pours out the bowls prepared in chapter 15 and completes Revelation’s three series of seven. Its plagues echo the trumpets of chapters 8–9 but now strike the whole world. Two threads are left hanging for later chapters: the great city Babylon, whose judgment is shown in chapters 17–18, and the kings gathered at Armageddon, whose defeat comes in chapter 19. The voice from the throne, “It is done,” will be heard again when God makes all things new (21:6).
