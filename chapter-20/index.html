REVELATION 20:01–06

A thousand years.

A thousand years.

01John sees an angel coming down from heaven, holding the key to the abyss and a great chain in his hand.

02He seizes the dragon, the ancient serpent, who is the Devil and Satan, and binds him for a thousand years.

03He throws him into the abyss, locks it and seals it over him, so that he can no longer lead the nations astray until the thousand years are over. After that he must be let loose for a short time.

04John sees thrones, and those seated on them are given authority to judge. He sees the souls of those beheaded for their witness to Jesus and for the word of God, who had not worshiped the beast or its image or taken its mark on forehead or hand. They come to life and reign with Christ for a thousand years.

05The rest of the dead do not come to life until the thousand years are over. This is the first resurrection.

06Blessed and holy is anyone who shares in the first resurrection. Over them the second death has no power; they will be priests of God and of Christ and will reign with him for the thousand years.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

This passage is the source of the long debate over the “millennium.” The USCCB notes are clear: like the other numbers in the book, the thousand years are not to be taken literally. They symbolize the long period between the chaining of Satan, a symbol of Christ’s resurrection-victory over death and evil, and the end of the world. During this time, the notes explain, God’s people share in God’s reign by virtue of their baptismal victory over sin and death (Rom 6:1–8; John 5:24–25; Eph 2:1–6). This is the reading of St. Augustine (City of God, Book 20), which the Church has followed: the “first resurrection” is new life in Christ, begun in Baptism; the “second death” is final separation from God. Verse 6 is the fifth beatitude of the book, and calls the faithful priests, as in 1:6 and 5:10.

REVELATION 20:07–10

Fire from heaven.

Fire from heaven.

07When the thousand years are over, Satan will be released from his prison.

08He will go out to deceive the nations at the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them for battle; they are as many as the sand of the sea.

09They march across the breadth of the earth and surround the camp of the holy ones and the beloved city, but fire comes down from heaven and devours them.

10The Devil who deceived them is thrown into the lake of fire and sulfur, where the beast and the false prophet are, and they will be tormented day and night forever and ever.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The USCCB notes describe this as the symbolic battle at the end of time, when Satan is released. “Gog and Magog,” they explain, stand for all pagan nations; the names come from Ezekiel 38–39, where a great northern army attacks God’s people and is destroyed from heaven. The “beloved city” is Jerusalem, the community of the faithful. As at Armageddon, there is no real battle: fire from heaven ends it at once. The Catechism teaches that before Christ’s return the Church will pass through a final trial (CCC 675–677), and that the kingdom will be fulfilled not by the Church’s historical triumph but by God’s victory over the final unleashing of evil.

REVELATION 20:11–15

The books were opened.

The books were opened.

11Then John sees a great white throne and the one seated on it. Earth and sky flee from his presence, and no place is found for them.

12He sees the dead, great and small, standing before the throne, and books are opened. Another book is opened, the book of life. The dead are judged by what is written in the books, according to their deeds.

13The sea gives up its dead, and Death and Hades give up their dead, and each one is judged according to their deeds.

14Then Death and Hades are thrown into the lake of fire. This lake of fire is the second death.

15Anyone whose name is not found written in the book of life is thrown into the lake of fire.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

This is the description of the final judgment. The USCCB notes explain that after the intermediate reign of Christ, all the dead are raised and judged, inaugurating the new age. Two kinds of books are opened: the books of deeds and the book of life (see 3:5). The Church holds both truths together. We are judged by what we have done (Matt 25:31–46; Rom 2:6), and our names are written in the book of life by God’s grace in Christ. The last enemy to be destroyed is death (1 Cor 15:26): here Death and Hades themselves are thrown into the fire. The Catechism calls the last judgment the moment when “the truth of each man’s relationship with God will be laid bare” (CCC 1039).

REVELATION 20:1–3

The key and the chain

Jesus spoke of tying up the strong man to plunder his house (Mark 3:27) and saw Satan fall like lightning (Luke 10:18). The binding began with Christ’s death and resurrection: the Devil still prowls, but on a chain.

REVELATION 20:2–7

A thousand years

A thousand means immensity. The USCCB notes read the thousand years as the whole time between Christ’s victory and his return: the age of the Church, in which we now live.

REVELATION 20:5–6

The first resurrection

Paul says the baptized have already been raised with Christ (Col 3:1; Rom 6:4). The first resurrection is the new life of grace; the second, the resurrection of the body at the end (John 5:25–29).

Read Romans 6
04

REVELATION 20:8

Gog and Magog

From Ezekiel 38–39: a vast army from the ends of the earth attacks God’s people and is destroyed by God alone. In Revelation they stand for all the nations that oppose God, not for any one modern country.

Read Ezekiel 38
05

REVELATION 20:12

The books and the book of life

Daniel saw books opened before the Ancient of Days (Dan 7:10). We are judged by our deeds, and saved by having our names written in the Lamb’s book of life (Phil 4:3; Rev 21:27).

Read Daniel 7
READ WITH CARE

Many Christians read Revelation 20 as a prediction of a literal thousand-year earthly kingdom, ruled by Christ from Jerusalem, either before or after his Second Coming (premillennialism and postmillennialism), often combined with a “rapture.” The Catholic Church does not hold these views. The Catechism rejects “even modified forms” of the idea of a millennial kingdom within history (CCC 676). Following St. Augustine and the USCCB notes, the Church reads the thousand years symbolically, as the present age in which Christ already reigns and his people share in that reign through Baptism. Gog and Magog, likewise, are symbols of all the nations hostile to God, not codes for particular modern states.

WHERE IT FITS

From witness to reign.

Chapter 20 completes the defeat of the three enemies introduced in chapters 12–13. The beast and false prophet were captured in chapter 19; now the dragon himself is bound, released for a short time, and cast into the lake of fire. The chapter also answers the cry of the martyrs under the altar in 6:9–11: they are given thrones and reign with Christ. With death itself destroyed, the way is clear for the new creation of chapters 21–22, where there will be no more death.
