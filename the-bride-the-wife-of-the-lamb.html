Revelation 21:1–9

The Bride, the Wife of the Lamb

The book that showed a Woman in labor, pursued into the desert, ends by showing a Bride. The same angel who showed John the harlot now says, Come, I will show you the wife of the Lamb.

The text
"Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth. The former heaven and the former earth had passed away, and the sea was no more. I also saw the holy city, a new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, 'Behold, God's dwelling is with the human race. He will dwell with them and they will be his people and God himself will always be with them [as their God]. He will wipe every tear from their eyes, and there shall be no more death or mourning, wailing or pain, [for] the old order has passed away'" (Revelation 21:1–4).
In one sentence
The Bride is the Church made perfect, the whole people of the redeemed; and the Church sees in Mary, already glorified, the image and beginning of what the Bride will be.
Authority
Scripture

The Woman answered

This study began with a sign in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, in labor, with a dragon before her (12:1–4). It has followed her into the desert and followed her enemy to his end. Here is where her story arrives.

The Woman (chapter 12) The Bride (chapter 21)
John sees "A great sign… in the sky" "The holy city… coming down out of heaven"
Her light Clothed with the sun, the moon under her feet "It gleamed with the splendor of God" (21:11); no need of sun or moon (21:23)
The number twelve A crown of twelve stars Twelve gates, twelve foundations (21:12, 14)
Her condition "She wailed aloud in pain" (12:2) "No more… mourning, wailing or pain" (21:4)
Her place The desert, "a place prepared by God" (12:6) The city "prepared as a bride" (21:2)
Her enemy The dragon, standing before her Gone; "the sea was no more" (21:1)
Her relation to Christ Mother of the male child Wife of the Lamb

The word "prepared" is the same in both. God prepared a place to keep her through the trial, and through the trial he was preparing her.

The words

  • A new heaven and a new earth. Isaiah's promise: "See, I am creating new heavens and a new earth" (Isaiah 65:17). The word for "new" means new in kind, renewed, not merely another.
  • The sea was no more. "Because as home of the dragon it was doomed to disappear," say the notes of the New American Bible (note on 21:1).
  • Coming down out of heaven from God. The city is not built up from the earth. She is given.
  • A bride adorned for her husband. "Symbol of the church," say the notes, citing St. Paul: "the Jerusalem above is freeborn, and she is our mother" (Galatians 4:26).
  • God's dwelling is with the human race. "Dwelling" is the word for tent, the tabernacle. "He will dwell with them" is the same word John's Gospel uses of the Incarnation: the Word "made his dwelling among us" (John 1:14).
  • He will wipe every tear. From Isaiah 25:8, and promised already to the great multitude (7:17). There an elder said it of God. Here the voice from the throne says it.
  • "Behold, I make all things new." The first words spoken by the one on the throne since the opening chapter.
  • The bride, the wife of the Lamb. Both words together: the freshness of a bride, and the permanence of a wife.

Scripture

The Old Testament behind it

The prophets spoke of the covenant as a marriage. "I will betroth you to me forever: I will betroth you to me with justice and with judgment, with loyalty and with compassion" (Hosea 2:21). "As a bridegroom rejoices in his bride so shall your God rejoice in you" (Isaiah 62:5). The notes of the New American Bible: "Marriage is one of the biblical metaphors used to describe the covenant relationship between God and his people… Hence, idolatry and apostasy are viewed as adultery and harlotry" (note on 19:7).

The covenant formula itself, "they will be his people and God himself will always be with them," comes from Ezekiel 37:27. It was spoken at Sinai, repeated by the prophets, and is here said for the last time, because here it is complete.

The New Testament names the bridegroom. John the Baptist called himself the friend of the bridegroom (John 3:29). St. Paul wrote that Christ "loved the church and handed himself over for her… that he might present to himself the church in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or any such thing" (Ephesians 5:25–27). Scripture

Two women, one angel

Compare the two invitations.

  • "Then one of the seven angels who were holding the seven bowls came and said to me, 'Come here. I will show you the judgment on the great harlot'" (17:1). He carries John away into a desert (17:3).
  • "One of the seven angels who held the seven bowls… came and said to me, 'Come here. I will show you the bride, the wife of the Lamb.'" He takes John "to a great, high mountain" (21:9–10).

The wording is deliberately the same. John wants the two set side by side: a woman who is a city, Babylon, clothed in purple and scarlet and seated on the beast; and a woman who is a city, Jerusalem, clothed in the glory of God and given to the Lamb.

With the Woman of chapter 12 there are three women in the second half of the book. The Woman is the people of God in labor and in flight. The harlot is the city that gave itself to the beast. The Bride is the Woman at the end of her road. The harlot is what the Woman refused to become.

What the Church teaches

The Bride is the Church. "The Church… is described as the spotless spouse of the spotless lamb. It is she whom Christ 'loved and for whom he delivered himself up that he might sanctify her'" (CCC 757). At the end "all those he has redeemed… will be gathered together as the one People of God, the 'Bride of the Lamb,' 'the holy city Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God'" (CCC 865).

She is not yet what she will be. "Those who are united with Christ will form the community of the redeemed, 'the holy city' of God, 'the Bride, the wife of the Lamb.' She will not be wounded any longer by sin, stains, self-love, that destroy or wound the earthly community" (CCC 1045). The Church on earth is wounded by these. John says the city is "prepared." She is being made ready now.

Scripture begins and ends with a wedding. "Sacred Scripture begins with the creation of man and woman in the image and likeness of God and concludes with a vision of 'the wedding-feast of the Lamb'" (CCC 1602).

The new creation is real. "The universe itself will be renewed" (CCC 1042). "In this new universe, the heavenly Jerusalem, God will have his dwelling among men" (CCC 1044). "We know neither the moment of the consummation of the earth and of man, nor the way in which the universe will be transformed" (CCC 1048).

Church teaching

Read with the Woman

Here the readings of the Woman as Mary and as the Church come closest together, and the Catechism joins them in a single line. Listing those who share in the worship of heaven, it names "the all-holy Mother of God (the Woman), the Bride of the Lamb" (CCC 1138).

Mary is not the Bride in place of the Church. She is the first in whom the Bride is finished. "Mary goes before us all in the holiness that is the Church's mystery as 'the bride without spot or wrinkle'" (CCC 773). "In her we contemplate what the Church already is in her mystery on her own 'pilgrimage of faith,' and what she will be in the homeland at the end of her journey… The Mother of Jesus, in the glory which she possesses in body and soul in heaven, is the image and beginning of the Church as it is to be perfected in the world to come" (CCC 972). Church teaching

So the sign of chapter 12 and the vision of chapter 21 answer each other in her. What John sees coming down at the end, the Church believes has already been given to one of her members. The Assumption is the Bride's future, shown in advance.

Read as Israel, the Woman is here too: the city's gates bear "the names of the twelve tribes of the Israelites" (21:12). Read as the Church: its foundations bear "the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb" (21:14). The three readings that ran side by side through chapter 12 enter the same city. The victor and the coward

The voice from the throne ends with a promise and a warning (21:7–8). "The victor will inherit these gifts, and I shall be his God, and he will be my son." Then a list of those whose lot is the second death, and the first word in it is "cowards."

The notes of the New American Bible explain: "their conviction is so weak that they deny Christ in time of trial and become traitors." This is the choice of chapter 13 once more. The mark was taken, for the most part, out of fear. See Two marks on the forehead.

Where readers differ

  • A new world or this one renewed. "Passed away" could suggest replacement. The Church speaks of renewal and transformation: the universe "will be perfectly re-established in Christ" (CCC 1042). How, she says, we do not know (CCC 1048).
  • Present or future. The notes of the New American Bible read the new Jerusalem as "the church," and comment on "They are accomplished" that "God's reign has already begun." The Catechism reads these verses of the consummation. Both are held: the Bride exists now, and is not yet perfected. Disputed
  • Mary and the Bride. Scripture's Bride is the Church. Calling Mary the Bride's image and beginning is the Church's teaching (CCC 773, 972); identifying her simply as the Bride of this chapter would go beyond it.

What it asks of us

To know what we are being prepared for. The word for the Church's present life is "prepared": a bride being made ready. Everything in the Christian life that purifies, including what is painful, belongs to that.

And to take the promise personally. The voice does not say that suffering will be explained. It says that God himself "will wipe every tear from their eyes." The one who sat on the throne through every vision of this book rises, at the end, to do that.
