What this site is

The Woman and the Ark is an intermediate and advanced study of one story in the Book of Revelation: the Woman and the Dragon. It begins where the story begins, with the ark of the covenant seen in heaven (Revelation 11:19) and the great sign of the Woman (12:1), and follows it to its end in the Bride of the Lamb (21:9).

Its sources are Scripture, the liturgy, the Catechism and other teaching of the Church, the Fathers and Doctors, Church-approved apparitions (as aids, never additions to the faith), and the saints. Every claim is labeled by its level of authority.

What it is not

It is not speculative. It names no person, living or dead, as any figure of the Apocalypse, and it sets no dates.

It is also a different site from apocalypse12.org, whose address is one letter away. That site is Fr. John's own writing. This one is a study of what has been revealed.

Fr. John

Fr. John is a Catholic diocesan priest. He founded and directs The Ephesus Challenge, a family of websites on the Book of Revelation and Christian hope. This site is researched and written with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, under his direction, and he reviews its pages. He is not an academic scholar of Scripture.

Patrons

The Ephesus Challenge is dedicated to Our Lady, Queen of Heaven and Earth, the Woman clothed with the sun (Revelation 12:1), under the patronage of St. Carlo Acutis, St. Bernard of Clairvaux, St. Teresa Benedicta of the Cross, St. Rose of Lima and Pope St. John Paul II.

The emblem

The emblem shows the ark of the covenant within a crown of twelve stars: the last thing John sees in chapter 11 and the first sign of chapter 12, seen together as the liturgy reads them.
