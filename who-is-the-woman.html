Revelation 12:1–6

Who Is the Woman?

The Church reads her as Mary, as Israel and as the Church. This page sets out what in the text supports each reading, who has held it, and how they are one.

The text
A woman clothed with the sun is with child and cries out in labor. A dragon waits to devour the child. "She gave birth to a son, a male child, destined to rule all the nations with an iron rod. Her child was caught up to God and his throne. The woman herself fled into the desert where she had a place prepared by God, that there she might be taken care of for twelve hundred and sixty days" (Revelation 12:1–6).
In one sentence
She is the mother of the Messiah: Mary in person, Israel as the people he came from, and the Church as the mother of all who belong to him.
Authority
Scripture

What the text itself gives us

Any answer has to fit six things the passage says of her:

  1. She appears in heaven, clothed with the sun, the moon under her feet, crowned with twelve stars (12:1).
  2. She is with child and cries out in the pain of labor (12:2).
  3. Her son is the Messiah: he will "rule all the nations with an iron rod" (12:5; Psalm 2:9).
  4. Her son is "caught up to God and his throne" (12:5).
  5. She flees to the desert and is cared for there for 1,260 days (12:6, 14).
  6. She has other children: "the rest of her offspring, those who keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus" (12:17).

No single reading takes all six in their plainest sense. Points 3 and 4 point most directly to one mother, Mary. Points 1, 2 and 5 are drawn from the Old Testament's pictures of Israel. Points 5 and 6 describe the Church through her history. That is why the Church has kept all three readings.

There is also the serpent. The dragon is "the ancient serpent" (12:9), and the scene of a woman, her offspring and the serpent is the scene of Genesis 3:15: "I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers." Whoever the Woman is, she is the woman of that first promise. Scripture

Mary

The argument from the text. The child is a real person, Jesus Christ, born, enthroned and ruling. It is natural that his mother in the vision is also a real person, the one who bore him.

St. John Henry Newman, a Doctor of the Church, put it this way. He granted the older reading at once: "I do not deny of course, that under the image of the Woman, the Church is signified." But he held that John "would not have spoken of the Church under this particular image, unless there had existed a blessed Virgin Mary, who was exalted on high" and "the object of veneration to all the faithful." The mother and the child, he argued, "are not personifications but Persons." And since a serpent stands in the vision as in Genesis: "if the first woman is Eve, why is not the second Mary?" (Letter to Pusey, 1866, pp. 58–59). A Father or Doctor

The Church's use. The liturgy of the Assumption, Pope St. Pius X, Pius XII, Pope St. Paul VI and Pope St. John Paul II all read the sign of Mary; their words are given on A woman clothed with the sun. The Catechism calls her "the Woman" among those who worship in heaven (CCC 1138). Church teaching

The difficulty. The earliest commentators did not read her so. Newman met the objection directly: he admitted that "such an interpretation is but poorly supported by the Fathers," and answered that "Christians have never gone to Scripture for proof of their doctrines, till there was actual need." Other features, the labor pains and the flight to the desert, are harder to say of Mary herself. The labor pains have a page of their own.

Israel

The argument from the text. The sun, moon and twelve stars are Jacob's family in Joseph's dream (Genesis 37:9). The prophets pictured Zion as a mother in labor: "Writhe, go into labor, O daughter Zion, like a woman giving birth" (Micah 4:10); "Before she is in labor, she gives birth; before her pangs come upon her, she delivers a male child" (Isaiah 66:7). The Messiah was born of this people, "from them, according to the flesh" (Romans 9:5).

Who has held it. St. Victorinus of Pettau, who died a martyr about 304 and wrote the earliest Latin commentary on Revelation, reads the Woman as "the ancient Church of fathers, and prophets, and saints, and apostles," which groaned with longing until Christ took flesh from its own people (Commentary on the Apocalypse, on 12:1). A Father or Doctor The notes of the New American Bible read her as "God's people in the Old and the New Testament" (note on 12:1–6). A theologian

The difficulty. Israel as a people did not flee the dragon after the Ascension, and "those who bear witness to Jesus" are her offspring only if Israel continues in the Church. So this reading always passes over into the next.

The Church

The argument from the text. After the child is taken up, the Woman remains on earth, hunted and protected, for the whole measured time; and her other children are the faithful (12:6, 13–17). St. Paul had already called "the Jerusalem above" "our mother" (Galatians 4:26).

Who has held it.

  • St. Hippolytus (about 200): "By the woman then clothed with the sun, he meant most manifestly the Church" (On Christ and Antichrist 61). A Father or Doctor
  • St. Methodius of Olympus (d. about 311): the Woman "is certainly, according to the accurate interpretation, our mother," the Church, who stands upon the baptismal font "bringing forth those who are washed in it" (The Banquet VIII.5–6). A Father or Doctor
  • The Second Vatican Council describes the Church in the same terms: "By her preaching she brings forth to a new and immortal life the sons who are born to her in baptism" (Lumen Gentium 64; CCC 507). Church teaching

The difficulty. The Church did not give birth to Christ; Christ gave birth to the Church. Hippolytus and Methodius therefore read the birth as the Church bringing forth Christ in believers, which is true, but it is not the plain sense of a child who is "caught up to God and his throne."

How they are one

Each reading is strong exactly where another is weak. The Church has not chosen among them, and her teaching explains why she does not need to.

  • Mary is Israel in one person. She is "the daughter of Zion in person" (CCC 2676), the one woman of the chosen people in whom its whole vocation, to bring forth the Messiah, was accomplished.
  • Mary is what the Church is. "At once virgin and mother, Mary is the symbol and the most perfect realization of the Church" (CCC 507). Church teaching
  • Benedict XVI joined all three in a few sentences: "This Woman represents Mary, the Mother of the Redeemer, but at the same time she also represents the whole Church, the People of God of all times, the Church which in all ages, with great suffering, brings forth Christ ever anew" (General Audience, August 23, 2006). Church teaching
  • Pope St. John Paul II spoke of Mary's "ecclesial identification as the 'woman clothed with the sun'" (Redemptoris Mater 47). Church teaching

This study follows the liturgy and reads the Woman first as Mary. On each page it then asks what the same verse says of Israel and of the Church, because the text itself moves from one to the other: from the mother of one Son (12:5) to the mother of many (12:17).

Where readers differ

  • The order. The ancient commentators begin from the Church or Israel and say little of Mary; the modern liturgy and the popes begin from Mary. The Church has defined none of this. Disputed
  • Whether the Fathers read her as Mary at all. Several later writers, among them St. Epiphanius, Quodvultdeus, Oecumenius and St. Andrew of Caesarea, are reported to connect the Woman with the Mother of God, some firmly and some with hesitation. Their own texts have not yet been consulted for this study, and nothing is claimed for them here until they have been; see The Commentators.

What it asks of us

To find the Woman is to find our own place in the chapter. If she is Mary, we are the children given to her at the Cross (John 19:26–27). If she is the Church, we are the offspring the dragon makes war on. If she is Israel, we are the nations who have come to share her hope. In each case the text says the same thing of her children: they "keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus" (12:17).
