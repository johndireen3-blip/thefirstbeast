Revelation 20:1–3

The Dragon Bound

The dragon of chapter 12 is named again by all four of his names, chained and shut in the abyss. When this happens, and what it means that he is bound and still dangerous.

Home › The Study › The dragon bound

Draft · awaiting Fr. John's review

The text
"Then I saw an angel come down from heaven, holding in his hand the key to the abyss and a heavy chain. He seized the dragon, the ancient serpent, which is the Devil or Satan, and tied it up for a thousand years and threw it into the abyss, which he locked over it and sealed, so that it could no longer lead the nations astray until the thousand years are completed. After this, it is to be released for a short time" (Revelation 20:1–3).
In one sentence
The Church's common reading, from St. Augustine on, is that the dragon was bound by Christ's death and resurrection and that the thousand years are the present age of the Church; she has rejected the expectation of an earthly kingdom before the end.
Authority
Scripture

The same dragon

John names him exactly as he did in chapter 12.

Revelation 12:9 Revelation 20:2
"The huge dragon, the ancient serpent, who is called the Devil and Satan" "The dragon, the ancient serpent, which is the Devil or Satan"
"who deceived the whole world" "so that it could no longer lead the nations astray"
"was thrown down to earth" "threw it into the abyss"

The four names appear together only in these two places. John means the reader to set the passages side by side. See The great red dragon and War in heaven.

The dragon's story in this book is a descent in three steps: from heaven to earth (12:9), from earth to the abyss (20:3), and from the abyss to the pool of fire (20:10). He never rises.

The words

  • An angel. Not Christ himself, and not even Michael by name. One angel with a key and a chain is enough. Scripture never treats the devil as God's rival. "He is only a creature" (CCC 395).
  • The key to the abyss. At 9:1 the key was given so that the abyss could be opened. The risen Christ said at the beginning of the book, "I hold the keys to death and the netherworld" (1:18).
  • Seized, tied, threw, locked, sealed. Five verbs, and the dragon does nothing in any of them.
  • Sealed. The word used for God's servants in chapter 7. God's seal protects his own, and God's seal confines their enemy.
  • So that it could no longer lead the nations astray. The text itself says what the binding is for. It is not said that he can do nothing. It is said that he can no longer deceive the nations.
  • A thousand years. The notes of the New American Bible: "Like the other numerical values in this book, the thousand years are not to be taken literally; they symbolize the long period of time between the chaining up of Satan (a symbol for Christ's resurrection-victory over death and the forces of evil) and the end of the world" (note on 20:1–6).
  • A short time. The same phrase as 12:12: the devil "knows he has but a short time."

Scripture

When was he bound?

The Lord himself used this image, and of his own ministry. Accused of casting out demons by the prince of demons, he answered: "How can anyone enter a strong man's house and steal his property, unless he first ties up the strong man? Then he can plunder his house" (Matthew 12:29). The verb "ties up" is the verb of Revelation 20:2.

He spoke the same way elsewhere. "I have observed Satan fall like lightning from the sky" (Luke 10:18). And on the eve of his Passion: "Now is the time of judgment on this world; now the ruler of this world will be driven out" (John 12:31).

The Catechism gathers these: "The coming of God's kingdom means the defeat of Satan's… Jesus' exorcisms free some individuals from the domination of demons. They anticipate Jesus' great victory over 'the ruler of this world.' The kingdom of God will be definitively established through Christ's cross" (CCC 550). And: "Victory over the 'prince of this world' was won once for all at the Hour when Jesus freely gave himself up to death to give us his life" (CCC 2853). Church teaching

This is also what chapter 12 said in its own way. The dragon was thrown down when "salvation and power have come," and the brothers conquered him "by the blood of the Lamb" (12:10–11). Chapter 20 tells the same victory a second time under another image.

Bound, and still prowling

St. Peter writes to Christians living after the resurrection: "Your opponent the devil is prowling around like a roaring lion looking for [someone] to devour" (1 Peter 5:8). How can he be bound and prowling?

St. Augustine, in the twentieth book of The City of God, answered from the text. The devil is bound in one respect: he can no longer hold the nations in deception as he did before Christ. Before the Gospel, the knowledge of the true God was nearly confined to one people. Since Pentecost it has gone to every nation, and he has not been able to stop it. He can still tempt, and he can persecute. He cannot prevent the Church from being gathered. A Father or Doctor

The Catechism says the same in its own words: "The power of Satan is, nonetheless, not infinite… He cannot prevent the building up of God's reign," though his action "may cause grave injuries" and "is permitted by divine providence" (CCC 395). Church teaching

A chained animal is the old comparison. It is dangerous to anyone who goes within its reach, and it cannot go where it pleases.

The thousand years

This study does not repeat the full treatment of the millennium. For that, see the topic pages on The Lamb's Victory. Three points belong here.

The reign is now. Those who reign with Christ for the thousand years are the martyrs and all who refused the beast (20:4). The notes of the New American Bible: "During this time God's people share in the glorious reign of God that is present to them by virtue of their baptismal victory over death and sin."

Some early Fathers read it otherwise. In the second and third centuries several respected writers, St. Justin and St. Irenaeus among them, expected a literal thousand-year reign of Christ and the saints on a renewed earth after his return. This view is called chiliasm or millenarianism. From the time of St. Augustine the reading given above became the common one in the Church. Fathers in agreement

The Church has judged the question. "The Antichrist's deception already begins to take shape in the world every time the claim is made to realize within history that messianic hope which can only be realized beyond history through the eschatological judgment. The Church has rejected even modified forms of this falsification of the kingdom to come under the name of millenarianism, especially the 'intrinsically perverse' political form of a secular messianism" (CCC 676). Church teaching

The practical meaning is plain. Any promise of a perfected world inside history, whether religious or political, is to be refused. The kingdom comes fully only after the judgment.

Released for a short time

"After this, it is to be released for a short time." John does not say why. He says "it is to be," literally "it must be": the word used throughout the book for what stands in God's plan.

The Catechism speaks of this as the Church's last trial: "Before Christ's second coming the Church must pass through a final trial that will shake the faith of many believers" (CCC 675). She "will enter the glory of the kingdom only through this final Passover, when she will follow her Lord in his death and Resurrection" (CCC 677). As the Lord's own life ended with an hour when darkness was given power, so will the life of his Body. And as with him, it is short, and it is not the end. See Gog and Magog, and the dragon's end.

Read with the Woman

In chapter 12 the dragon stood before the Woman to devour her child, pursued her into the desert, and poured out a river to sweep her away. Each time he failed. The Catechism reads these verses of Mary: "'He pursued the woman' but had no hold on her: the new Eve, 'full of grace' of the Holy Spirit, is preserved from sin and the corruption of death" (CCC 2853).

The promise in Eden was that the serpent's head would be struck by the woman's offspring (Genesis 3:15). The binding is that blow. What remains for him is what Genesis also foretold: to strike at the heel. Church teaching

Where readers differ

  • The sequence. Read straight through, chapter 20 seems to follow chapter 19 in time. The common Catholic reading takes it as a new vision that goes back to the beginning, as chapter 12 did, and tells the whole age of the Church from another side. Revelation often retells in this way. Disputed
  • A future binding. Some Christians, mostly outside the Catholic Church, expect the binding and the thousand years after Christ's return. The Church's rejection of millenarianism is given above.
  • How long "a short time" is. The text does not say, and the Church sets no dates (CCC 673).

What it asks of us

Not to fear him more than he deserves. One angel binds him. He acts only by permission. The Catechism quotes St. Ambrose: "One who entrusts himself to God does not dread the devil" (CCC 2852).

And not to go within reach of the chain. He is bound for the nations, and still a danger to the one who draws near. "Resist him, steadfast in faith" (1 Peter 5:9).
