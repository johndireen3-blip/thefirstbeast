## Three answers to chapter 13

Chapter 13 made three claims on the world: worship the beast (13:4, 8, 15); the power behind him cannot be fought (13:4); and take his mark or you cannot buy or sell (13:16–17). The three angels answer them in order. Their messages are public, spoken "in a loud voice" to "every nation, tribe, tongue, and people," the same four words used for the beast's dominion (13:7). Whatever the beast has reached, the Gospel reaches.

## The first angel: worship the Creator

"Fear God and give him glory… Worship him who made heaven and earth and sea and springs of water."

- **Everlasting good news.** The Greek is *euangelion aiōnion*, an eternal gospel. It is called good news even though it speaks of judgment, because for those oppressed by the beast the coming of the true Judge is the best news there is. The New American Bible's note: "that God's eternal reign is about to begin."
- **It is the first truth, and the simplest.** The angel does not begin with the Cross but with creation: there is one God, he made everything, and he alone is to be adored. St. Paul preached the same to pagans at Lystra: "turn from these idols to the living God, 'who made heaven and earth and sea and all that is in them'" (Acts 14:15).
- **To every nation.** "This gospel of the kingdom will be preached throughout the world as a witness to all nations, and then the end will come" (Matthew 24:14).

The Catechism: "Adoration is the first act of the virtue of religion. To adore God is to acknowledge him as God, as the Creator and Savior, the Lord and Master of everything that exists" (CCC 2096). And of its opposite: "Man commits idolatry whenever he honors and reveres a creature in place of God, whether this be gods or demons… power, pleasure, race, ancestors, the state, money… Many martyrs died for not adoring 'the Beast,' refusing even to simulate such worship" (CCC 2113). [[auth:church]]

## The second angel: Babylon is fallen

"Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great, that made all the nations drink the wine of her licentious passion."

The words are Isaiah's, spoken of the old Babylon: "Fallen, fallen is Babylon! All the images of her gods are smashed to the ground!" (Isaiah 21:9). Jeremiah supplies the cup: "Babylon was a golden cup in the hand of the LORD making the whole earth drunk; the nations drank its wine, thus they have gone mad. Babylon suddenly falls" (Jeremiah 51:7–8).

This is the first time Babylon is named in Revelation. Her fall is announced before she has even been described; the description comes in chapters 17 and 18. The notes of the New American Bible say the verse "anticipates the lengthy dirge over Babylon (Rome) in Rev 18:1–19:4." [[auth:theologian]]

The angel speaks in the past tense of something still to come. In prophecy this is the tense of certainty. To those who live inside the great city and cannot imagine the world without it, heaven says: it is already over.

Babylon is studied on the partner site, [The First Beast](https://thefirstbeast.com/the-harlot-and-the-scarlet-beast), and on The Lamb's Victory under [Babylon](https://thelambsvictory.org/topics/babylon).

## The third angel: the cost of the mark

"Anyone who worships the beast or its image, or accepts its mark on forehead or hand, will also drink the wine of God's fury, poured full strength into the cup of his wrath, and will be tormented in burning sulfur before the holy angels and before the Lamb."

This is the most severe warning in the book. Four things should be said about it.

**1. It is a warning, given so that it need not happen.** It is spoken aloud, in advance, to everyone. The Catechism reads all such passages this way: "The affirmations of Sacred Scripture and the teachings of the Church on the subject of hell are a call to the responsibility incumbent upon man to make use of his freedom in view of his eternal destiny. They are at the same time an urgent call to conversion" (CCC 1036). [[auth:church]]

**2. It answers the beast's threat with a greater one.** The beast says: worship, or be killed (13:15); take the mark, or starve (13:17). The angel says that there is something worse than death. The Lord said it first: "Do not be afraid of those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul; rather, be afraid of the one who can destroy both soul and body in Gehenna" (Matthew 10:28).

**3. The two cups.** Babylon gave the nations her wine (14:8); those who drank it will drink another (14:10). The image is the prophets' "cup of wrath" (Isaiah 51:17; Jeremiah 25:15). "Full strength" means unmixed: in the ancient world wine was drunk diluted, and this cup is not.

**4. What the Church teaches about hell.** "The teaching of the Church affirms the existence of hell and its eternity… The chief punishment of hell is eternal separation from God" (CCC 1035). And at the same time: "God predestines no one to go to hell; for this, a willful turning away from God (a mortal sin) is necessary, and persistence in it until the end" (CCC 1037). The images of fire and sulfur come from Sodom (Genesis 19:24); the reality they point to is the loss of God, freely chosen and held to. [[auth:church]]

The warning is aimed at one act: worship of the beast and acceptance of his mark. What the mark is, and how it stands against the seal of God, is the subject of Part 3 of this study. Here it is enough to notice that in every verse the mark is joined to worship (13:15–17; 14:9, 11). It is not something a person could receive by accident.

## Here is what sustains the holy ones

"Here is what sustains the holy ones who keep God's commandments and their faith in Jesus" (14:12).

The Greek word is *hypomonē*: endurance, the strength to remain under a weight. John has said this once already, at the worst moment of chapter 13: "Such is the faithfulness and endurance of the holy ones" (13:10).

The description of the holy ones is the same as that of the Woman's offspring: "those who keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus" (12:17). The New American Bible's cross-reference for this verse is 12:17. These are her children, and this is what is asked of them: not to defeat the beast, but to outlast him without giving in. ## Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord

"I heard a voice from heaven say, 'Write this: Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on.' 'Yes,' said the Spirit, 'let them find rest from their labors, for their works accompany them.'"

This is the second of the seven beatitudes of Revelation, and here the Spirit himself answers, as he does once more at the very end of the book (22:17).

- **It reverses the third angel exactly.** Those who take the mark have "no relief day or night" (14:11). Those who die in the Lord "find rest." The same Greek root is used for both.
- **"From now on."** From the death and resurrection of Christ, death is changed for those who belong to him. The Catechism: "Because of Christ, Christian death has a positive meaning" (CCC 1010).
- **"Their works accompany them."** Not ahead of them, as a payment, but with them. The note in the New American Bible observes that John "insists upon the necessity and value of works," beside faith in Jesus (note on 14:12). The Catechism speaks of "the joy of heaven, as God's eternal reward for the good works accomplished with the grace of Christ" (CCC 1821). [[auth:church]]
- **Rest.** "Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). "A sabbath rest still remains for the people of God" (Hebrews 4:9).

The Church reads this verse at funerals. It was written first for people who might be killed for refusing the mark, to tell them that the beast's worst is, for them, a blessing.

## Where readers differ

- **Who the angels are.** John calls them angels and gives no more. Some commentators have seen in them the preachers of the Gospel in the last days, or particular saints or movements in the Church's history. The Church has identified none of them, and several later groups have claimed the "three angels' messages" for themselves. This study keeps to the text. [[auth:disputed]]
- **Babylon.** Rome for John's first readers; for later readers, every city and system built on the same idolatry of wealth and power; for some, a particular power still to come.
- **The fire.** The Church teaches the reality and eternity of hell and has not defined the nature of its fire.
- **The Fathers** on this passage will be added from their own texts; see [The Commentators](/the-commentators).

## What it asks of us

The three messages are a short catechism for a time of pressure. Adore God alone, who made you. Do not be impressed by Babylon; her fall is already spoken. And count the real cost: what the beast can take from you is less than what he asks you to give up.

Then the passage turns from warning to promise, and that is where it rests. "The Church encourages us to prepare ourselves for the hour of our death" (CCC 1014). To die in the Lord is to have lived in him. The beatitude is for those who endure, and it is the last word heaven speaks before the harvest.
