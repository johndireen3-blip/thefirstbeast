## The words

- **A great sign.** The Greek is *sēmeion mega*. A sign points beyond itself. It is the first of three "signs" in this part of the book: the Woman (12:1), the dragon (12:3), and the seven angels with the last plagues (15:1). Only the Woman's is called simply "great" at its first appearing.
- **Appeared.** The same verb as in the verse before, where the ark "could be seen" (11:19). See [The ark in heaven](/the-ark-in-heaven).
- **Clothed with the sun.** She does not shine by her own light. She is wrapped in it, as one puts on a garment.
- **A crown.** The word is *stephanos*, the wreath given to a victor. The dragon wears *diadēmata*, the diadems of kings (12:3). Her crown is won; his are seized.

## The Old Testament behind it

- **Joseph's dream.** "The sun and the moon and eleven stars were bowing down to me" (Genesis 37:9). The sun, moon and stars are Jacob, his wife and his sons: the family of Israel. Twelve stars are the twelve tribes complete.
- **The bride of the Song.** "Who is this that comes forth like the dawn, beautiful as the white moon, pure as the blazing sun, fearsome as celestial visions?" (Song of Songs 6:10).
- **God's own garment.** The psalmist says of the LORD, "You are clothed with majesty and splendor, robed in light as with a cloak" (Psalm 104:1–2). The Woman wears what belongs to God.
- **The sign of the virgin.** Isaiah told King Ahaz, "Ask for a sign from the LORD, your God; let it be deep as Sheol, or high as the sky!" When Ahaz refused, the prophet said, "The Lord himself will give you a sign; the young woman, pregnant and about to bear a son, shall name him Emmanuel" (Isaiah 7:11, 14). John sees a sign, high as the sky: a woman with child (Revelation 12:2).

## Read first as Mary

The child the Woman bears is the one "destined to rule all the nations with an iron rod" (Revelation 12:5; Psalm 2:9). He is the Messiah, and the mother of the Messiah is Mary. The Church's prayer and teaching read the sign of her:

- **The liturgy.** Pope St. Paul VI begins his letter on Mary with these words: "The great sign which the Apostle John saw in heaven, 'a woman clothed with the sun,' is interpreted by the sacred Liturgy, not without foundation, as referring to the most blessed Mary, the mother of all men by the grace of Christ the Redeemer" ([*Signum Magnum*](https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/apost_exhortations/documents/hf_p-vi_exh_19670513_signum-magnum.html), 1967). The verse is the entrance antiphon and part of the first reading of the Mass of the Assumption. [[auth:church]]
- **Pope St. Pius X.** "Everyone knows that this woman signified the Virgin Mary, the stainless one who brought forth our Head" ([*Ad Diem Illum*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-x/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-x_enc_02021904_ad-diem-illum-laetissimum.html) 24, 1904). [[auth:church]]
- **Pius XII.** "The scholastic Doctors have recognized the Assumption of the Virgin Mother of God as something signified, not only in various figures of the Old Testament, but also in that woman clothed with the sun whom John the Apostle contemplated on the Island of Patmos" ([*Munificentissimus Deus*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/apost_constitutions/documents/hf_p-xii_apc_19501101_munificentissimus-deus.html) 27, 1950). [[auth:church]]
- **Pope St. John Paul II.** The enmity foretold in Genesis 3:15 "is confirmed in the Apocalypse," where "there recurs the sign of the 'woman,' this time 'clothed with the sun' (Rev. 12:1). Mary, Mother of the Incarnate Word, is placed at the very center of that enmity" ([*Redemptoris Mater*](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_25031987_redemptoris-mater.html) 11, 1987). [[auth:church]]
- **The Catechism** names among those who take part in the liturgy of heaven "the all-holy Mother of God (the Woman)" (CCC 1138), and says of the dragon: "'He pursued the woman' but had no hold on her: the new Eve, 'full of grace' of the Holy Spirit, is preserved from sin and the corruption of death" (CCC 2853). [[auth:church]]

## Israel beside her

The images are Israel's. The sun, moon and stars come from Joseph's dream, and the twelve stars are the twelve tribes. The prophets spoke of Zion as a mother in labor (Isaiah 66:7–9; Micah 4:10). The notes of the New American Bible read the Woman this way: she "symbolizes God's people in the Old and the New Testament. The Israel of old gave birth to the Messiah" ([note on 12:1–6](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/12)). [[auth:theologian]]

Mary does not replace this reading. She is the daughter of Zion in whom it comes true: the one woman of Israel who in fact gave birth to the Messiah.

## The Church beside her

The earliest commentary we have reads the Woman as the Church. St. Hippolytus of Rome, about the year 200:

> "By the woman then clothed with the sun, he meant most manifestly the Church, endued with the Father's word, whose brightness is above the sun. And by the 'moon under her feet' he referred to her being adorned, like the moon, with heavenly glory. And the words, 'upon her head a crown of twelve stars,' refer to the twelve apostles by whom the Church was founded" ([*On Christ and Antichrist* 61](https://bkv.unifr.ch/en/works/cpg-1872/versions/treatise-on-christ-and-antichrist/divisions/62)). [[auth:father]]

The rest of the chapter supports him. The Woman flees into the wilderness and is nourished there (12:6, 14), and the dragon makes war on "the rest of her offspring, those who keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus" (12:17). Those are things said of the Church through her history.

## How the three belong together

Pope St. John Paul II holds them in one sentence. By Mary's "ecclesial identification as the 'woman clothed with the sun' (Rev. 12:1), it can be said that 'in the Most Holy Virgin the Church has already reached that perfection whereby she exists without spot or wrinkle'" (*Redemptoris Mater* 47). [[auth:church]]

The Catechism gives the principle: in Mary "we contemplate what the Church already is in her mystery on her own 'pilgrimage of faith,' and what she will be in the homeland at the end of her journey" (CCC 972). [[auth:church]]

So the sign is one. Israel is the people from whom the Messiah came. Mary is the daughter of Israel who bore him. The Church is the mother of all who belong to him. What is said of the Woman is said first of Mary, and through her of the people she comes from and the people she is mother to.

## Where readers differ

- **Which reading comes first.** Hippolytus reads the Church first; the modern liturgy and the popes quoted above read Mary first; many Scripture scholars begin with Israel. The Church has not ruled between them, and she uses all three. Paul VI's words are careful: the liturgy applies the sign to Mary "not without foundation." [[auth:disputed]]
- **The birth pangs.** Verse 2 says the Woman "wailed aloud in pain as she labored to give birth." Those who read the Church or Israel first find this easy; those who read Mary first must explain it, as Pius X did when he read the pangs as her care for the children still to be born to God. This has a page of its own, to come.
- **The Fathers after Hippolytus.** Several later commentators, in the East especially, read the Woman directly as the Mother of God. Their texts are still to be consulted for this study; see [The Commentators](/the-commentators).

## An approved apparition

The image of Our Lady of Guadalupe (1531) shows a woman who stands before the sun, with the moon beneath her feet and stars on her mantle, and who is with child. The Church has approved the devotion and keeps her feast on December 12. An apparition adds nothing to the faith (CCC 67), but this one has taught generations to see Mary in John's sign. A later page takes up the Woman in the approved apparitions. [[auth:apparition]]

## What it asks of us

A sign is given to be read. John is shown the Woman before he is shown the dragon, and clothed in God's light before any attack begins. The order is a mercy: we are meant to see who she is, and whose she is, before we see what rises against her. Every child of the Church is among "the rest of her offspring" (12:17). To look at the Woman is to learn where we come from and what we are meant to become.
