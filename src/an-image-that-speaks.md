## What the text shows

The second beast deceives the inhabitants of the earth, "telling them to make an image for the beast who had been wounded by the sword and revived. It was then permitted to breathe life into the beast's image, so that the beast's image could speak and [could] have anyone who did not worship it put to death" (Revelation 13:14–15).

The image is made by the people at the false prophet's urging. It is made *for* the first beast and stands for him. The false prophet is "permitted" to give it breath. And it does two things no idol had done before: it **speaks**, and it **condemns** those who will not worship it. The image (*eikōn*) is not the same as the mark (*charagma*) of 13:16, but the two go together from here to the end: "the beast and its image" and "its mark" (14:9–11; 16:2; 19:20; 20:4).

## Idols that could not speak

Israel's prophets mocked the idols for their silence: "They have mouths but do not speak, eyes but do not see… they produce no sound from their throats. Their makers will be like them, and anyone who trusts in them" (Psalm 115:5–8). "Ah! you who say to wood, 'Awake!' to silent stone, 'Arise!' Can any such thing give oracles? … there is no breath in it at all" (Habakkuk 2:19). The image of the beast answers the prophets' mockery: an idol that seems to have what the idols never had, a voice and breath.

## A counterfeit of God's breath

God formed man from the dust and "blew into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living being" (Genesis 2:7). The false prophet is "permitted to breathe life" into a made thing. It is the beast's version of creation: not a living soul made in God's image, but an image of the beast made to seem alive.

## The golden statue of Daniel 3

The NABRE cross-reference for Revelation 13:15 points to Daniel 3: a king's golden statue, the whole world commanded to bow, and the furnace for those who refuse ([mark 6](/worship-demanded)). Revelation adds the voice. The statue no longer needs a herald to cry the command; the image speaks it.

## First fulfillment

In John's world, statues of the emperor stood in temples and public squares, and offering incense before the emperor's image was the test put to Christians (Pliny, *Letters* 10.96). Ancient writers describe wonder-workers who made statues seem to speak or move by hidden devices, and modern commentators often read the speaking image against that background [[auth:theologian]].

## The Fathers

St. Irenaeus reads the verse plainly of the end: the false prophet "shall order an image of the beast to be made, and he shall give breath to the image, so that the image shall speak, and he shall cause those to be slain who will not adore it" (*Against Heresies* V.28.2) [[auth:father]]. The Fathers agree that the image is part of the last deception and that the faithful will refuse it, as the three young men refused the statue [[auth:fathers]].

## The Church [[auth:church]]

Idolatry is honoring and revering a creature in place of God (CCC 2113). Writing on artificial intelligence, the Holy See has warned that "the presumption of substituting God for an artifact of human making is idolatry," and that in the end "it is not AI that is ultimately deified and worshipped, but humanity itself," which "becomes enslaved to its own work" (Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith and Dicastery for Culture and Education, *Antiqua et Nova* 105, 2025). Pope Leo XIV adds that "technology is never neutral, because it takes on the characteristics of those who devise, finance, regulate and use it" (*Magnifica Humanitas* 9, 2026).

## Where interpreters differ [[auth:disputed]]

- **The emperor's statues and the tricks of the cult:** the first meaning for John's readers.
- **A literal image of the Antichrist at the end, made to speak:** St. Irenaeus and the Fathers.
- **Media and propaganda:** the ruler's image multiplied on screens and made to speak everywhere at once.
- **Artificial intelligence:** a made thing that speaks, answers and seems to think, which some modern readers see as the image, or as the means by which an image could be given a voice and power over life.

The Church has not decided.

## The response asked of us

Those who "won the victory over the beast and its image" stand on the sea of glass and sing (Revelation 15:2); those who "had not worshiped the beast or its image" reign with Christ (20:4). Do not give to any made thing, however wise it sounds, the trust and honor that belong to God. "You shall not make for yourself an idol… you shall not bow down before them or serve them" (Exodus 20:4–5).
