## What the text shows

The beast rises with blasphemous names on its heads (Revelation 13:1). It "was given a mouth uttering proud boasts and blasphemies," and it opened that mouth "to utter blasphemies against God, blaspheming his name and his dwelling and those who dwell in heaven" (Revelation 13:5–6).

The mouth is "given," like everything else the beast has. And the words move in a line: first proud boasts about itself, then blasphemy against God, then against his name, his dwelling, and the saints and angels who dwell with him. Self-exaltation is where it begins; contempt for God is where it ends.

## The mouth of Daniel's little horn

John is drawing on Daniel. Among the ten horns of Daniel's fourth beast a little horn springs up, with eyes like human eyes and "a mouth that spoke arrogantly" (Daniel 7:8, 20). It speaks against the Most High and wears down his holy ones (Daniel 7:25), and Daniel watches "from the first of the arrogant words" until the beast is slain (Daniel 7:11). St. Paul describes the man of lawlessness the same way: he opposes and exalts himself above every so-called god and object of worship (2 Thess 2:4).

## First fulfillment

The NABRE note identifies Daniel's little horn as Antiochus IV, the Seleucid king who took the surname *Epiphanes*, "God made manifest," entered the Temple and spoke with great arrogance (1 Maccabees 1:21–24; Daniel 11:36). For John's first readers, the blasphemous names on the beast's heads are commonly read as the divine titles the Roman emperors took or were given, such as *divus* (divine) and, under Domitian, *dominus et deus*, "lord and god" [[auth:theologian]].

## The Fathers

St. Irenaeus reads Daniel's arrogant horn and Paul's man of lawlessness as one figure: the one to come will set aside all idols and present himself as the only one to be worshiped as God (*Against Heresies* V.25.1–3) [[auth:father]]. St. Jerome, commenting on Daniel 7:8, insists that the horn is a man, not the devil or a demon, though Satan will dwell in him wholly (*Commentary on Daniel*, on 7:8) [[auth:father]]. The Fathers agree that the boasting of the last enemy ends in a claim to divine honor [[auth:fathers]].

## The Church [[auth:church]]

Blasphemy is uttering against God, inwardly or outwardly, words of hatred, reproach or defiance; it extends to words against the Church, the saints and sacred things (CCC 2148). The Catechism names the final deception as a pseudo-messianism by which man glorifies himself in place of God and of his Messiah come in the flesh (CCC 675). The beast's mouth is that self-glorification spoken aloud.

## Where interpreters differ [[auth:disputed]]

- **The words of one man at the end:** St. Irenaeus, St. Jerome, and most of the Fathers.
- **The proud claims of the empire and its cult:** the first meaning for John's readers, emphasized in modern commentary.
- **A spirit present in every age:** the "many antichrists" whose words deny the Father and the Son (1 John 2:18, 22).

These readings can stand together. The Church has not decided which the text intends at the end.

## The response asked of us

Against a mouth that boasts and blasphemes, the holy ones bless the name of God (Psalm 113:2–3) and keep the second commandment: "You shall not invoke the name of the LORD, your God, in vain" (Exodus 20:7; CCC 2142–2149). Daniel's horn speaks "until the beast was slain" (Daniel 7:11). Its words have an end; the praise of God does not.
