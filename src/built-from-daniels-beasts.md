## What the text shows

The beast rises from the sea with ten horns and seven heads, crowns on its horns and blasphemous names on its heads (Revelation 13:1). It looks like a leopard, with a bear's feet and a lion's mouth (Revelation 13:2).

## Daniel's four beasts in one

Daniel saw four beasts come up from the churning sea: a lion, a bear, a leopard with four heads, and a fourth beast with ten horns (Daniel 7:3–7). Count their heads and you have seven; their horns, ten. John's beast gathers all four into one body. He names them in reverse, leopard, bear, lion, as if looking back from the last kingdom to the first.

## What Daniel's beasts are

The angel explains: the four beasts are four kings, and the fourth beast is a fourth kingdom (Daniel 7:17, 23). So the beast from the sea is first a power over nations, the sum of the empires that went before it. The sea it rises from is the restless world of the peoples; Revelation itself says the waters are peoples, multitudes, nations and tongues (Revelation 17:15).

## First fulfillment, then further fulfillment

Daniel's visions were first fulfilled in the empires of his own horizon and in Antiochus IV (1 Maccabees 1). For John's readers, the NABRE note reads the composite beast as the Roman Empire and its seven heads as emperors (see Revelation 17:9–10). A further fulfillment at the end is read on top of this first one, not instead of it.

## The Fathers

St. Irenaeus writes that the fourth kingdom will be divided among ten kings, and that the one to come will rise among them and bring down three (*Against Heresies* V.25.3; V.26.1). St. Hippolytus (*On Christ and Antichrist* 25–28) and St. Cyril of Jerusalem (*Catechetical Lecture* 15.12–13) agree [[auth:fathers]]. St. Augustine reads the beast instead as the city of the ungodly, the whole body of the wicked (*City of God* XX.9) [[auth:father]].

## The Church [[auth:church]]

The Church has not defined who or what the beast is. She does teach that the final deception can take political form: whenever someone claims to fulfil within history the hope that can only be fulfilled beyond it, including in a secular messianism (CCC 676).

## Where interpreters differ [[auth:disputed]]

- **The sea beast is the Antichrist himself:** St. Irenaeus.
- **It is his kingdom:** St. Hippolytus, who reads the land beast as the Antichrist's kingdom.
- **It is the ungodly city, the whole body of the wicked:** St. Augustine.
- **It is Rome first, and the end later:** modern Catholic commentary, including the NABRE notes.

The Church has not decided.

<div class="response" markdown>

## The response asked of us

Every beast in Daniel 7 passes away. The kingdom is given to the holy ones of the Most High, and it does not pass (Daniel 7:27). Empires are real, and they are temporary.
