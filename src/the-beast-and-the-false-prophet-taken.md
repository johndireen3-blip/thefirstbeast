## Where the vision stands

In chapter 13 the world asked, "Who can compare with the beast or who can fight against it?" (13:4). The beast "was also allowed to wage war against the holy ones and conquer them" (13:7). The second beast, whom chapter 16 first calls "the false prophet," worked signs, made the image and imposed the mark.

Here is the answer to the world's question. For the two beasts themselves, see [The dragon on the shore](/the-dragon-on-the-shore) and the partner site [The First Beast](https://thefirstbeast.com/).

## The words

- **An angel standing on the sun.** Where all can see and hear. The victory is announced before the enemy has even assembled. The notes of the New American Bible: "The certainty of Christ's victory is proclaimed by an angel, followed by a reference to the mustering of enemy forces" ([note on 19:17–21](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/19)).
- **Gathered to fight.** This is the gathering prepared at 16:14–16, where three unclean spirits assembled the kings "for the battle on the great day of God the almighty."
- **Was caught.** The Greek word means seized, arrested. It is the word used when men tried to arrest Jesus. Nothing else is said. No blow is described.
- **The false prophet who had performed… the signs.** He is identified to the last by what he did: he deceived. The mark and the image are named once more, and then never again in the book except of those who refused them (20:4).
- **Thrown alive.** The two do not die as their followers do. They go directly to their end.
- **The fiery pool burning with sulfur.** The notes: a "symbol of God's punishment… different from the abyss." The abyss is a prison (20:3). This is final.
- **The rest were killed by the sword that came out of the mouth.** That is, by his word.

## The Old Testament behind it

The summons to the birds is taken from Ezekiel's oracle against Gog: "Say to birds of every kind and to every wild beast: 'Assemble! Come from all sides for the sacrifice I am making for you… You shall eat the flesh of warriors and drink the blood of the princes of the earth'" (Ezekiel 39:17–18). The notes of the New American Bible say plainly that "the gruesome imagery is borrowed" from this chapter.

John uses Ezekiel's Gog twice: the feast of the birds here, and the name itself at 20:8. See [Gog and Magog, and the dragon's end](/gog-and-magog-and-the-dragons-end). [[auth:scripture]]

It is prophetic language for total defeat, and it should be read as such. The list at 19:18, "free and slave, small and great," repeats the list of those who took the mark at 13:16. The power that claimed everyone protects no one.

## Two suppers

Chapter 19 contains two meals, a few verses apart.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Text | 19:7–9 | 19:17–18 |
| The call | "Blessed are those who have been called" | "Come here. Gather" |
| The guests | The Bride, in clean white linen | The birds of the air |
| What it is | Communion with the Lamb | The end of those who fought him |

Everyone comes to one table or the other. The Lord's parable of the wedding feast makes the same division (Matthew 22:1–14). The Church hears the first invitation at every Mass, in words taken from this chapter: "Blessed are those called to the supper of the Lamb." [[auth:church]]

## No battle

This is the most striking thing in the passage, and it is easy to miss. The armies gather. The next sentence is, "The beast was caught."

Christian tradition has taken this seriously. The beast's power was always a permitted power: "it was given," "it was allowed," chapter 13 says again and again. When the permission ends, the power ends. St. Paul says the same of the lawless one, "whom the Lord [Jesus] will kill with the breath of his mouth and render powerless by the manifestation of his coming" (2 Thessalonians 2:8). His appearing is enough.

Notice too who goes first. The deceiver and the one he served are removed, and only then "the rest." The lie falls before the armies do.

## What the Church teaches

The Catechism describes the last trial in terms that match chapter 13: "a religious deception offering men an apparent solution to their problems at the price of apostasy from the truth. The supreme religious deception is that of the Antichrist, a pseudo-messianism by which man glorifies himself in place of God and of his Messiah come in the flesh" (CCC 675).

And it describes the end of that trial in terms that match this passage: "The kingdom will be fulfilled, then, not by a historic triumph of the Church through a progressive ascendancy, but only by God's victory over the final unleashing of evil, which will cause his Bride to come down from heaven" (CCC 677). [[auth:church]]

The Church does not defeat the beast. In chapter 13 she is conquered by it. She is asked for "the faithful endurance that is part of the life of the holy ones" (13:10), and the victory is God's.

## Read with the Woman

In chapter 12 the dragon, unable to reach the Woman, "went off to wage war against the rest of her offspring" (12:17), and at once took his stand on the shore to call up the beast. The beast was his instrument against her children. This passage is where that instrument is taken from his hand. He will be dealt with next, and alone.

## Where readers differ

- **Persons or powers.** Whether the beast and the false prophet are, at the end, two individual men, or the persecuting state and its religious servant in every age, or both: a pattern that recurs and comes to a head. The Catechism speaks of "the Antichrist" and also of a deception that "already begins to take shape in the world" (CCC 675–676). This study names no one. The Catechism gives the pattern to watch for, and [How to test a reading of Revelation](/how-to-test-a-reading) gives a way to weigh what you see. [[auth:disputed]]
- **A battle on a field.** Some expect a literal military gathering at a place. The notes of the New American Bible read the imagery as symbolic. The text gives a place-name only at 16:16, and describes no fighting.
- **The first-century reading.** Rome and its emperor-worship did fall, and did not have the last word over the Church. That is a true fulfillment. The passage also looks beyond it, since the pool of fire is no earthly defeat.

## What it asks of us

Not to be impressed. The whole force of chapter 13 was that the beast looked permanent: all nations, all commerce, signs from heaven, and no one able to fight it. John shows its end in two sentences so that Christians living under such a power will measure it rightly.

And not to try to win by the beast's means. The saints are given endurance and faithful witness. The sword belongs to the rider, and it is his word.
