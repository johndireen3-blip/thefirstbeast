## First, what an apparition is

Everything on this page stands under one paragraph of the Catechism:

> "Throughout the ages, there have been so-called 'private' revelations, some of which have been recognized by the authority of the Church. They do not belong, however, to the deposit of faith. It is not their role to improve or complete Christ's definitive Revelation, but to help live more fully by it in a certain period of history" (CCC 67). [[auth:church]]

Three things follow for a study of Scripture.

- **An apparition proves nothing about what a verse means.** The meaning of Revelation 12 is settled by Scripture, Tradition and the Church's teaching, set out on the other pages of this study.
- **An apparition can show.** When the Church receives one, she is saying that nothing in it is contrary to the faith and that the faithful may draw help from it. When its images are those of Revelation 12, it becomes a kind of picture of the text, placed before people who may never read a commentary.
- **No one is bound to believe it.** A Catholic may accept an approved apparition with prudent devotion and is free not to.

The fuller account of each apparition, with the Church's judgment and what Our Lady asked, is on [A Great Sign](https://agreatsign.si/apparitions). This page takes only what bears on Revelation 12.

## Guadalupe, 1531

**What the Church has received.** There is no single decree of approval of the modern kind. The Church received Guadalupe by stages: a canonical inquiry in Mexico in 1665–1666; Benedict XIV's bull of 1754 naming her patroness of New Spain, with a Mass and Office for December 12; the canonical coronation of the image in 1895; and the canonization of St. Juan Diego by Pope St. John Paul II in 2002. Her feast is kept on December 12. [[auth:church]]

**The echo.** The image on Juan Diego's cloak shows a woman with the sun's rays behind her, the moon beneath her feet and stars on her mantle, and the dark sash of a woman with child.

| Revelation 12 | The image of Guadalupe |
| --- | --- |
| "Clothed with the sun" (12:1) | Rays of the sun surround her |
| "The moon under her feet" (12:1) | She stands on a crescent moon |
| "A crown of twelve stars" (12:1) | Stars cover her mantle |
| "She was with child" (12:2) | The sash of a woman expecting a child |

It is the nearest thing the Church has to a portrait of John's sign. And she comes as the Woman of verse 5 above all: carrying a Son. In the years that followed, the peoples of Mexico came to the faith in great numbers. Pope St. John Paul II saw in her face "a perfectly inculturated evangelization" ([*Ecclesia in America* 11](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_exhortations/documents/hf_jp-ii_exh_22011999_ecclesia-in-america.html)). [[auth:apparition]]

## The Miraculous Medal, 1830

**What the Church has received.** St. Catherine Labouré, a novice of the Daughters of Charity in Paris, saw Our Lady on November 27, 1830, and was asked to have a medal made. The Archbishop of Paris, finding "nothing in it at all contrary to faith," allowed it to be struck in 1832 and ordered an inquiry in 1836. Leo XIII approved a feast of the Miraculous Medal in 1894. Catherine was canonized by Pius XII in 1947. [[auth:church]]

**The echo.** The medal puts two verses side by side.

- **On the front,** Mary stands on a globe with a serpent under her feet. This is Genesis 3:15: "I will put enmity between you and the woman." Revelation names the dragon "the ancient serpent" (12:9), joining the last book of the Bible to the first. The victory itself belongs to her Son; she shares in it. See [The dragon pursues the woman](/the-dragon-pursues-the-woman).
- **On the back,** twelve stars encircle a cross and the letter M, with the hearts of Jesus and Mary beneath. "On her head a crown of twelve stars" (12:1). The cross above the M recalls Calvary, where she was given as mother to the disciple (John 19:26–27).
- **The prayer** around the image, "O Mary, conceived without sin, pray for us who have recourse to thee," is the prayer of "the rest of her offspring" (12:17).

The medal is Revelation 12 small enough to wear. [[auth:apparition]]

More: [Rue du Bac on A Great Sign](https://agreatsign.si/rue-du-bac).

## Lourdes, 1858

**What the Church has received.** The Bishop of Tarbes recognized the apparitions to St. Bernadette Soubirous in a pastoral letter of January 18, 1862, judging that the apparition "possesses all the characteristics of truth and that the faithful may believe in it with certainty." The feast of Our Lady of Lourdes, February 11, was extended to the whole Church by Pope St. Pius X in 1907. [[auth:church]]

**The echo.** Lourdes has none of the imagery of sun, moon and stars. Its link to Revelation 12 is in what the Lady said of herself: "I am the Immaculate Conception," four years after the dogma was defined. That is the truth the Catechism finds in verse 13: "'He pursued the woman' but had no hold on her: the new Eve, 'full of grace' of the Holy Spirit, is preserved from sin" (CCC 2853). The Woman at enmity with the serpent from the first moment of her life is the Woman he could not sweep away. [[auth:apparition]]

More: [Lourdes on A Great Sign](https://agreatsign.si/lourdes).

## Fatima, 1917

**What the Church has received.** The Bishop of Leiria declared the apparitions worthy of belief on October 13, 1930. The feast of Our Lady of Fatima, May 13, is in the universal calendar. Sts. Francisco and Jacinta Marto were canonized in 2017. The Holy See published the third part of the secret in 2000, with a commentary by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger. [[auth:church]]

**The echo.**

- **A Lady "more brilliant than the sun."** So Sr. Lúcia described her. On October 13, 1917, before a great crowd, many witnesses reported that the sun seemed to dance and fall toward the earth. The woman "clothed with the sun" (12:1) has often been recalled in this connection.
- **The martyrs.** In the third part of the secret the children saw a persecuted Church, and angels gathering the blood of the martyrs. Revelation 12 says of them: "They conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" (12:11). See [The accuser cast out](/the-accuser-cast-out).
- **The war on her children.** What the message foretold of the Church's suffering is what 12:17 describes in every age.
- **The end.** "In the end, my Immaculate Heart will triumph." Like the chapter itself, Fatima ends in victory.

**How the Church reads it.** Cardinal Ratzinger's commentary is the model for reading any such vision beside Scripture. "The future is not in fact unchangeably set, and the image which the children saw is in no way a film preview of a future in which nothing can be changed." Its purpose is "to mobilize the forces of change in the right direction." And of its particular images: "Insofar as individual events are described, they belong to the past" ([*The Message of Fatima*](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20000626_message-fatima_en.html), 2000). Ten years later, as Pope, he added at Fatima: "We would be mistaken to think that Fatima's prophetic mission is complete" (homily, May 13, 2010). [[auth:church]]

Fatima sets no dates. What Our Lady asked was prayer, the Rosary, penance, and consecration to her Immaculate Heart.

More: [Fatima on A Great Sign](https://agreatsign.si/fatima).

## What the four have in common

Set beside one another, the four show the chapter almost verse by verse.

| Apparition | The part of Revelation 12 it echoes |
| --- | --- |
| Guadalupe | The Woman clothed with the sun, with child (12:1–2, 5) |
| The Miraculous Medal | The Woman and the serpent; the crown of twelve stars; her children's prayer (12:1, 9, 17; Genesis 3:15) |
| Lourdes | The Woman on whom the serpent has no hold (12:13–16) |
| Fatima | The martyrs, the war on her offspring, and the victory (12:11, 17) |

In each, the Woman of the Apocalypse is Mary; none of them shows her as a symbol only. That agrees with the way the liturgy reads the chapter and with the way this study reads it first. It adds nothing to that reading's authority. It shows that the Church's prayer and the Church's experience have moved in the same direction.

Each also asks for the same things, and they are the things the chapter asks: to turn to God, to pray, to do penance, and to stay close to her Son.

## Read with care

- **An echo is not a fulfillment.** No approved apparition says, "this is the woman of Revelation 12 now come to pass." The sign John saw is not used up by any of them.
- **No dates.** None of these apparitions gives a timetable for the end, and the Church forbids making one (CCC 673). Counting years from an apparition is not a use the Church has made of any of them.
- **Stay with what is approved.** Many claims gather around famous apparitions: details said to be hidden in the image of Guadalupe, a supposed unpublished part of the secret of Fatima, dates for chastisements. The Church has made no judgment in favor of these, and her reception of the apparitions does not rest on them.
- **Other apparitions.** Many other apparitions of Mary are reported, and some speak in the language of Revelation. This study uses only those the Church has received, and only as helps.

## What it asks of us

To let the picture do what a picture is for. A reader can study Revelation 12 for years and keep it at arm's length. A mother standing on the hill of Tepeyac, or on the medal a grandmother wore, is harder to keep at a distance.

The apparitions also correct a temptation that goes with studying the Apocalypse: to look for the dragon and forget the Woman. In every one of them she comes first, as she does in the text. And in every one of them she does what she did at Cana, and says: "Do whatever he tells you" (John 2:5).
