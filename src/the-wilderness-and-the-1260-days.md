## One event told twice

Verse 6 and verse 14 describe the same flight. Between them John tells the war in heaven (12:7–12), and then returns to the Woman. Verse 14 adds what verse 6 left out: how she gets there (the eagle's wings) and from whom she is kept (the serpent). The two numbers are the same length of time said two ways.

## The words

- **A place prepared by God.** The desert is not where she is driven. It is where she is expected. The Lord uses the same word of heaven: "I am going to prepare a place for you" (John 14:2).
- **Taken care of.** The Greek verb is *trephō*, "to feed, to nourish." Older English Bibles say "nourished." She is not merely hidden; she is fed.
- **The great eagle.** With the article: *the* great eagle, the one a reader of the Law already knows.

[[auth:scripture]]

## The Old Testament behind it

**The Exodus.** At Sinai God said to Israel: "You have seen how I treated the Egyptians and how I bore you up on eagles' wings and brought you to myself" (Exodus 19:4). Moses sang it again: "He found them in a wilderness, a wasteland of howling desert. He shielded them, cared for them, guarded them as the apple of his eye. As an eagle incites its nestlings, hovering over its young, so he spread his wings, took them, bore them upon his pinions" (Deuteronomy 32:10–11). In the desert "the Israelites ate the manna for forty years" (Exodus 16:35). The dragon had been Pharaoh (Ezekiel 29:3); the Woman's flight is a new exodus.

**Elijah.** When Ahab and Jezebel sought his life, "the word of the LORD came to Elijah: Leave here, go east and hide in the Wadi Cherith... I have commanded ravens to feed you there" (1 Kings 17:2–4). The drought of those days lasted "three and a half years" (Luke 4:25). Later an angel fed him in the desert, and "strengthened by that food, he walked forty days and forty nights to the mountain of God" (1 Kings 19:8).

**The bride.** The desert is also where God wins his people back: "I will allure her now; I will lead her into the wilderness and speak persuasively to her" (Hosea 2:16).

So for a reader of the Old Testament the desert means three things at once: safety from the tyrant, bread from God's hand, and nearness to God. [[auth:scripture]]

## The number

Revelation gives this period in three forms, and they are equal:

| Form | Where | Said of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,260 days | 11:3; 12:6 | The two witnesses' prophecy; the Woman's care in the desert |
| Forty-two months | 11:2; 13:5 | The trampling of the holy city; the beast's authority |
| "A year, two years, and a half-year" | 12:14 | The Woman's care in the desert |

Each is three and a half years, half of seven. The phrase comes from Daniel, where God's people are handed over to the persecutor "for a time, two times, and half a time" (Daniel 7:25; 12:7).

Two things follow from the table.

- **It is the same time seen from two sides.** The beast's forty-two months and the Woman's 1,260 days are one period. For exactly as long as the persecutor is allowed to act, God feeds his people. Not a day of the trial is without its bread.
- **It is a broken seven.** Seven is fullness in Revelation. Three and a half is a time cut short: real, painful, and limited.

The notes of the New American Bible explain the origin: the number is "the duration of the vicious persecution of the Jews by Antiochus IV Epiphanes," which "became the prototype of periods of trial for God's people" ([note on 11:2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/11)). [[auth:theologian]]

It is not a date to be computed: "it is not for you to know times or seasons" (Acts 1:7, quoted in CCC 673). For a fuller account of the number through the whole book, see The Lamb's Victory on [the forty-two months](https://thelambsvictory.org/topics/forty-two-months).

## How the Fathers read it

- **St. Hippolytus** reads the Church under persecution: the 1,260 days are the time "during which the tyrant is to reign and persecute the Church, which flees from city to city, and seeks concealment in the wilderness among the mountains." Her only defence is "the two wings of the great eagle, that is to say, the faith of Jesus Christ, who, in stretching forth His holy hands on the holy tree, unfolded two wings, the right and the left, and called to Him all who believed upon Him" ([*On Christ and Antichrist* 61](https://bkv.unifr.ch/en/works/cpg-1872/versions/treatise-on-christ-and-antichrist/divisions/62)). The wings are the arms of the Crucified. [[auth:father]]
- **St. Victorinus** reads the wings as two prophets, Elijah and the one who comes with him, and the flight as still to come: the faithful will be sustained "for three years and six months from the presence of the devil" ([*Commentary on the Apocalypse*](https://www.intratext.com/IXT/ENG1016/_PC.HTM), on chapter 12). He notes that the flight follows the child's birth and is not at the same time. [[auth:father]]
- **St. Methodius** reads the Church of every age, and especially those who live for God alone: she comes into the wilderness "flying on the heavenward wings of virginity" ([*The Banquet* VIII.12](https://www.intratext.com/IXT/ENG1159/_P1V.HTM)). [[auth:father]]

The three differ on the wings and agree on the Woman: in the desert she is the Church.

## Read of the Church, of Israel, of Mary

**The Church.** This is the passage where the Woman is most plainly the Church. The notes of the New American Bible say simply: "God protects the persecuted church in the desert, the traditional Old Testament place of refuge for the afflicted, according to the typology of the Exodus" ([note on 12:6](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/12)). Many readers take the measured time as the whole age between the Ascension (12:5) and the Lord's return, an age in which the Church is always in some desert and always fed. The bread God gives her there is not hard to name: "your ancestors ate the manna in the desert... I am the living bread that came down from heaven" (John 6:49–51). [[auth:theologian]]

**Israel.** The whole picture is Israel's own story, the Exodus, told again. The Catechism calls this way of reading "typological," because it "reveals the newness of Christ on the basis of the 'figures' (types) which announce him in the deeds, words, and symbols of the first covenant" (CCC 1094). [[auth:church]]

**Mary.** Two things in the Gospels answer to the verse. After the child's birth, when Herod sought his life, "Joseph rose and took the child and his mother by night and departed for Egypt" (Matthew 2:14): the mother of the Messiah did flee from the one who would devour her son. And the "place prepared by God" has been read of her Assumption, since the Lord's promise to prepare a place (John 14:2–3) was kept first in her. These are readings by likeness, and the Church has not taught either as the meaning of the verse. [[auth:disputed]]

## Where readers differ

- **Past, present or future.** Hippolytus and Victorinus place the flight in the last persecution. Many later readers take the 1,260 days as the Church's whole pilgrimage. The first-century reading sees the persecutions under Rome. These can be held together, as a pattern that recurs and will have a last and worst instance: "Before Christ's second coming the Church must pass through a final trial that will shake the faith of many believers" (CCC 675). [[auth:disputed]]
- **Literal or symbolic days.** The Church has not defined that the number is a literal three and a half years, and the end cannot be computed from it (CCC 673).
- **The wings.** The Cross, two prophets, virginity, or simply "the power and swiftness of divine help" (NABRE, note on 12:14).

## What it asks of us

The desert is not the promised land. The Woman is safe there, and fed, and not yet home. That is an exact description of the Church's life now, and of any Christian's life in a hard season. The verse asks for the trust Israel was asked for: to take today's bread from God's hand, and to believe that the time is measured by him and not by the dragon. "They that hope in the LORD will renew their strength, they will soar on eagles' wings" (Isaiah 40:31).
