## What the text shows

The beast "was given a mouth uttering proud boasts and blasphemies, and it was given authority to act for forty-two months" (Revelation 13:5). Like its power, its mouth and its war, its time is *given*. It does not choose how long it lasts.

## One span, three ways of counting it

Revelation names the same span three ways:

- **Forty-two months:** the nations trample the holy city (Revelation 11:2), and the beast acts (13:5).
- **1,260 days:** the two witnesses prophesy (11:3), and the woman is cared for in the desert (12:6).
- **"A year, two years, and a half-year":** the woman is kept safe from the serpent (12:14), echoing Daniel's "a time, two times, and half a time" (Daniel 7:25; 12:7).

The same measure governs the beast's power, the Church's witness and the woman's protection. The trial and the care are the same length: God does not let the one outlast the other.

## Half of seven

The NABRE note on Revelation 11:2 traces the number to the persecution by Antiochus IV, which lasted about three and a half years: "half of seven, counted as 1260 days," it "became the prototype of periods of trial for God's people." The note on Daniel 7:25 adds, "As seven is the Jewish 'perfect' number, half of it signifies great imperfection." The beast's time is a broken seven: real, but never complete.

## Short, because the dragon knows it

The dragon goes down to the earth "in great fury, for he knows he has but a short time" (Revelation 12:12). Christ promised that for the sake of the elect the days of the great tribulation would be shortened (Matthew 24:22). The fury of the last enemy is the fury of one whose time is running out.

## Numbers that cannot be dated

Daniel adds two longer counts, 1,290 days and 1,335 days (Daniel 12:11–12). The NABRE note says these "represent attempts to calculate the precise duration of the three and a half years," and that "the contradictory numbers were allowed to stand in the text; this is a reminder that it is not possible to calculate a precise date for God's judgment" (compare Mark 13:32). Scripture itself keeps the reader from making a calendar.

## The Fathers

St. Irenaeus joins Daniel's half-week to the reign of the one to come: "Now three years and six months constitute the half-week" (*Against Heresies* V.25.4), and says that "he will reign for three years and six months… and then the Lord will come from heaven in the clouds" (V.30.4) [[auth:father]]. St. Augustine says that anyone reading Daniel, "even half asleep," sees that the kingdom of the Antichrist "shall fiercely, though for a short time, assail the Church" (*City of God* XX.23) [[auth:father]]. The Fathers agree that the last persecution is short and measured by God; none of them built a calendar on it [[auth:fathers]].

## The Church [[auth:church]]

"Since the Ascension Christ's coming in glory has been imminent, even though 'it is not for you to know times or seasons which the Father has fixed by his own authority'" (CCC 673). "Of that day and hour no one knows, neither the angels of heaven, nor the Son, but the Father alone" (Matthew 24:36). The Church has never taught a seven-year tribulation, and she expects to pass through the final trial, not to be taken out of it (CCC 675).

## Where interpreters differ [[auth:disputed]]

- **Symbolic:** the broken seven, the Church's whole time of trial between Christ's two comings, measured and limited by God. Closest to the Church's reading, and it cannot become a date.
- **Literal, for the last enemy:** three and a half years of the Antichrist's reign at the end: St. Irenaeus, St. Hippolytus, St. Jerome and many later writers.
- **First fulfillment:** the persecution under Antiochus IV, and then under Rome (NABRE notes).
- **A seven-year frame:** the last week of Daniel 9:27 read as a future seven years, held by dispensational Protestants; not Catholic teaching.

<div class="response" markdown>

## The response asked of us

The number says one thing above all: the trial is measured, and God holds the measure. What sustains the holy ones is not a date but faithful endurance (Revelation 13:10; 14:12). The woman is cared for in the desert for the whole of the beast's time (Revelation 12:6, 14). Keep watch without fear, and leave the calendar to the Father.
