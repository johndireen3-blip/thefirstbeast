## The short answer

The first beast is the beast that rises from the sea in Revelation 13:1–10. The dragon, Satan, gives it "its own power and throne, along with great authority" (Revelation 13:2), the world follows it in amazement, and it makes war on the holy ones for a measured time before it is captured and thrown into the pool of fire (Revelation 13:5–7; 19:20).

Scripture does not say who it is. It describes the beast by what it does, so that the faithful can recognize it and stand firm. This site takes the same approach: it sets out the marks and names no one. That leaves the watching to you, and [How to test a reading of Revelation](/how-to-test-a-reading) gives eleven questions for doing it well.

## What Scripture reveals

- **A power over the nations.** The beast is built from the four beasts of Daniel 7: leopard, bear and lion, with ten horns (Revelation 13:1–2). Daniel's angel explains that those beasts are kingdoms (Daniel 7:17), and Revelation later says its seven heads are also seven kings (Revelation 17:9–10). [[auth:scripture]]
- **Authority on loan.** Its power is the dragon's, and worship of the beast is worship of the dragon (Revelation 13:2, 4). [[auth:scripture]]
- **A counterfeit of Christ.** One of its heads seemed mortally wounded and was healed, and "the whole world" followed it (Revelation 13:3). [[auth:scripture]]
- **A boasting mouth and a war on the faithful.** It speaks blasphemies against God, his name and his dwelling, and makes war on the holy ones (Revelation 13:5–7). [[auth:scripture]]
- **A limited time.** Its authority lasts forty-two months, a measured season, not forever (Revelation 13:5). [[auth:scripture]]
- **A servant.** A second beast, from the earth, makes the world worship the first (Revelation 13:11–17). Revelation later calls it the false prophet (Revelation 16:13; 19:20). [[auth:scripture]]

Each of these is set out with its texts on [the marks](/marks) and [the beast from the sea](/the-beast-from-the-sea).

## How the Church reads it

The Catechism does not name the beast. It teaches that before Christ's return the Church must pass through a final trial, a "religious deception offering men an apparent solution to their problems at the price of apostasy from the truth," and that the supreme deception is that of the Antichrist, "a pseudo-messianism by which man glorifies himself in place of God and of his Messiah come in the flesh" (CCC 675). [[auth:church]] It also warns against setting dates for the end (CCC 673). [[auth:church]]

The note to Revelation 13:1 in the Douay-Rheims Bible reads the beast as "probably the whole company of infidels, enemies and persecutors of the people of God, from the beginning to the end of the world," its heads as the great empires that oppressed God's people, and the last of them as "the great Antichrist and his empire." [[auth:theologian]]

## Why no name is given

St. Irenaeus of Lyons, writing in the second century and the first to comment at length on the number 666, refused to name the beast. He judged it "more certain, and less hazardous, to await the fulfilment of the prophecy, than to be making surmises, and casting about for any names that may present themselves" (*Against Heresies* V.30.3). [[auth:father]]

Many have been named over the centuries, from the persecuting emperors of Rome to rulers of every age, and every confident identification has so far failed. Interpreters still differ on how much of the chapter was fulfilled in the Roman Empire, how much recurs in every age, and how much awaits the end of time. [[auth:disputed]]

## What is asked of us

Revelation 13 interrupts its own vision to speak to the reader: "Whoever has ears ought to hear these words... Such is the faithfulness and endurance of the holy ones" (Revelation 13:9–10). [[auth:scripture]] The question the chapter asks is not only "who is the beast?" but "will you refuse to worship it?" That is [the response asked of the faithful](/the-response-of-the-faithful), and it is the measure of every reading.
