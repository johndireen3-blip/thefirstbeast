## The scene

Revelation 12 ends with the dragon, defeated in heaven and enraged at the woman, going off to make war on the rest of her offspring. He takes his stand on the sand of the sea (Revelation 12:17–18). Revelation 13 opens with what he calls up from it: a beast with ten horns and seven heads (Revelation 13:1). The two chapters are one scene. The dragon cannot reach the woman or her child, so he works through the beast against those who keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus.

Revelation 13 then shows a second beast, from the earth, who serves the first (Revelation 13:11–18). Revelation 16 and 19 call him the false prophet (Revelation 16:13; 19:20).

## Taken together

The beast from the sea is a power over the nations (Daniel 7:17), with authority that is not its own (Revelation 13:2), a healing that imitates the Resurrection (Revelation 13:3), and a world that follows it in fascination. Its time is measured (Revelation 13:5), and its end is told: it is captured and thrown into the pool of fire (Revelation 19:20).
