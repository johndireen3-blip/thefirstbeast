## A sentence divided by a chapter number

Our Bibles end chapter 11 with the ark and begin chapter 12 with the Woman. John wrote no chapter numbers; they were added in the Middle Ages. In his text one sight follows the other without a break: the temple is opened, the ark is seen, and "a great sign appeared in the sky, a woman clothed with the sun" (Revelation 12:1).

The liturgy reads them together. At Mass on the Solemnity of the Assumption, the first reading begins with Revelation 11:19 and passes straight to 12:1. [[auth:church]]

## The words

- **The temple.** The Greek word is *naos*, the sanctuary itself, the inner house where God dwells. It is not the word for the whole temple precinct with its courts. What is opened is the Holy of Holies.
- **Could be seen.** The verb is *ōphthē*, "was seen" or "appeared." John uses the same word in the next two sights: a great sign "appeared" (12:1), and another sign "appeared" (12:3). Three things are shown one after another: the ark, the Woman, the dragon.
- **The ark of his covenant.** Only here in Revelation, and only one other time in the whole New Testament (Hebrews 9:4).
- **Lightning, thunder, earthquake, hail.** These are the signs of Sinai, where the covenant was given (Exodus 19:16–18). They mark the end of each series of seven in Revelation, and here they close the seventh trumpet.

## The ark that was lost

The ark was the gold-covered chest that held the tablets of the covenant. Above it, between the cherubim, God had promised to meet his people (Exodus 25:10–22). When the tabernacle was finished, "the cloud covered the tent of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle" (Exodus 40:34).

When Babylon destroyed Jerusalem in 587 B.C., the ark disappeared. It was never in the second temple. Two traditions remained about it:

- **Jeremiah** foretold a day when it would not be missed: "They will in those days no longer say, 'The ark of the covenant of the LORD!' They will no longer think of it, or remember it, or miss it, or make another one" (Jeremiah 3:16).
- **The Second Book of Maccabees** records that Jeremiah hid "the tent, the ark, and the altar of incense" in a cave on the mountain Moses had climbed, and said: "The place is to remain unknown until God gathers his people together again and shows them mercy. Then the Lord will disclose these things, and the glory of the Lord and the cloud will be seen" (2 Maccabees 2:5–8).

So a Jewish reader of John's day knew that the ark was lost, and that its return would mean God had gathered his people and shown them mercy. In Revelation 11:19 it is shown again, not in a cave, but in heaven. [[auth:scripture]]

## The ark and Mary in the Gospel of Luke

Long before Revelation was written, St. Luke had told the story of Mary's visit to Elizabeth in words that echo the story of David bringing the ark to Jerusalem. Scripture scholars set the two side by side:

| The ark (2 Samuel 6; Exodus 40) | Mary (Luke 1) |
| --- | --- |
| The cloud "settled down upon" the tent, and the glory of the LORD filled it (Exodus 40:35) | "The power of the Most High will overshadow you" (1:35). The Greek verb is the same one the Greek Old Testament uses of the cloud |
| David and the people set out for the hill country of Judah to bring up the ark (6:2) | "Mary set out and traveled to the hill country in haste to a town of Judah" (1:39) |
| "How can the ark of the LORD come to me?" (6:9) | "How does this happen to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?" (1:43) |
| David comes "jumping and dancing before the LORD" (6:16) | "The infant in my womb leaped for joy" (1:44) |
| The ark is brought up "with shouts of joy" (6:15) | Elizabeth "cried out in a loud voice" (1:42) |
| "The ark of the LORD remained in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite for three months" (6:11) | "Mary remained with her about three months" (1:56) |

The old ark held the tablets of the Law, the word of God written on stone. Mary carried the Word made flesh. [[auth:theologian]]

## How the Church reads it

- **The Catechism.** "Mary, in whom the Lord himself has just made his dwelling, is the daughter of Zion in person, the ark of the covenant, the place where the glory of the Lord dwells. She is 'the dwelling of God . . . with men'" (CCC 2676). The last words are from Revelation 21:3. [[auth:church]]
- **The Litany of Loreto** invokes her as "Ark of the Covenant." [[auth:church]]
- **Pius XII**, defining the Assumption, recalled that theologians and preachers had applied to Mary the words of the psalm, "Arise, O Lord, into your resting place: you and the ark, which you have sanctified" (Psalm 132:8), and "have looked upon the Ark of the Covenant, built of incorruptible wood and placed in the Lord's temple, as a type of the most pure body of the Virgin Mary, preserved and exempt from all the corruption of the tomb and raised up to such glory in heaven" ([*Munificentissimus Deus*](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/apost_constitutions/documents/hf_p-xii_apc_19501101_munificentissimus-deus.html) 26). He presents this as the way they explained their belief, not as a proof. [[auth:church]]

Read this way, Revelation 11:19 and 12:1 show the same mystery twice: the ark in God's temple, and the Woman in heaven. This is why the Church reads them together on the day she celebrates Mary taken up, body and soul.

## Where readers differ

The Church has not defined the meaning of this verse. [[auth:disputed]]

- **The ark as Mary.** The reading above rests on Luke's Gospel, the Catechism and the liturgy. It is the reading this study follows first.
- **The ark as God's covenant kept.** Many commentators read the verse simply as the close of the seventh trumpet: the sanctuary opens, and the ark shows that God has been faithful to his covenant and now dwells openly with his people. The notes of the New American Bible take the passage this way: "The seventh trumpet proclaims the coming of God's reign after the victory over diabolical powers" ([note on 11:15–19](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/11)).

The two readings do not exclude each other. The ark was always the sign of God dwelling with his people, and the Church sees that dwelling made complete when the Word took flesh in Mary.

How the earliest commentators on Revelation read this verse will be added here from their own texts; see [The Commentators](/the-commentators).

## What it asks of us

The ark was the place where God met his people. John sees it at the moment the kingdom is proclaimed (Revelation 11:15), and the last vision of the book gives its meaning in plain words: "Behold, God's dwelling is with the human race. He will dwell with them and they will be his people" (Revelation 21:3). The verse asks us to believe that God has not withdrawn. What was hidden is kept safe with him, and what he has promised he shows in his own time.
