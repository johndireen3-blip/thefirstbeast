## Why this belongs to the Woman's story

Chapter 13 ends with a mark on the forehead or the hand, without which no one can buy or sell (13:16–17). Chapter 14 opens with a name on the forehead (14:1). A reader may think the beast invented the idea. He did not. Six chapters earlier, before the beast is ever mentioned, God has already marked his people. The beast's mark is a copy.

This part of the study reads the two together. This page takes the seal; the next takes the people who bear it; the third sets seal and mark side by side.

## Where the vision stands

Six seals of the scroll have been opened, and the sixth ended with a question: "the great day of their wrath has come and who can withstand it?" (6:17). Chapter 7 is the answer, given before the seventh seal is broken. The notes of the New American Bible call it an interlude in which "the elect receive the seal of the living God as protection against the coming cataclysm" ([note on 7:1–17](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/7)).

The order is the point. Everything waits. Four angels hold back the winds of destruction, and are forbidden to release them "until we put the seal on the foreheads of the servants of our God."

## The words

- **The seal.** The Greek is *sphragis*. In the ancient world a seal was the impression of a signet ring. The notes of the New American Bible: "whatever was marked by the impression of one's signet ring belonged to that person and was under his protection" (note on 7:2). A seal says two things: this is mine, and no one is to touch it.
- **Of the living God.** Against the idols, who are not alive, and against the beast, whose image must be given breath by another (13:15).
- **From the East.** Literally "from the rising of the sun": the place of light, and of paradise (Genesis 2:8).
- **On the foreheads.** Where it can be seen. Chapter 14 tells us what is written: the Lamb's "name and his Father's name" (14:1).
- **The servants of our God.** They are his servants before they are sealed. The seal does not make them his; it declares it.

## The Old Testament behind it

**Ezekiel's mark.** This is the vision John is following. Before Jerusalem is destroyed, the Lord says to a man with a scribe's case: "Pass through the city, through the midst of Jerusalem, and mark an X on the foreheads of those who grieve and lament over all the abominations practiced within it." To the destroyers he says: "do not touch anyone marked with the X. Begin at my sanctuary" (Ezekiel 9:4–6).

The note in the New American Bible explains the mark: "lit., the Hebrew letter taw." Taw is the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet, and in the old script it was written as a cross. Christians noticed. From the earliest centuries the Fathers saw in Ezekiel's mark a figure of the cross traced on the forehead. [[auth:father]]

**The blood of the Passover.** "The blood will mark the houses where you are. Seeing the blood, I will pass over you; thereby, when I strike the land of Egypt, no destructive blow will come upon you" (Exodus 12:13). The New American Bible's cross-references for Revelation 7:3 are these two passages: Exodus 12 and Ezekiel 9.

**The high priest's forehead.** Aaron wore on his turban a plate of pure gold engraved "as on a seal engraving, 'Sacred to the LORD,'" resting "over Aaron's forehead" (Exodus 28:36–38). What one man bore in the old covenant, a whole people bears in the new.

**The name written.** Isaiah foresaw a day when "this one shall write on his hand, 'The LORD's'" (Isaiah 44:5). [[auth:scripture]]

In every one of these, the mark means belonging, and belonging means protection when judgment passes through.

## What the seal protects from

Not from suffering. The same chapter shows the sealed as those "who have survived the time of great distress" and "washed their robes… in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14). In chapter 12 the Woman's children are attacked; in chapter 13 the holy ones are conquered (13:7).

The seal protects from two things. From the judgments of God that fall on the world: when the locusts of the fifth trumpet are loosed, they may harm "only those people who did not have the seal of God on their foreheads" (9:4). And from being lost: those sealed in chapter 7 are found standing with the Lamb in chapter 14, all of them. The seal does not keep a Christian from the trial. It keeps him through it.

## How the Church says we are sealed

St. Paul uses John's word for what has already happened to every Christian. "In him you also, who have heard the word of truth… were sealed with the promised holy Spirit" (Ephesians 1:13). "Do not grieve the holy Spirit of God, with which you were sealed for the day of redemption" (4:30). God "has also put his seal upon us and given the Spirit in our hearts as a first installment" (2 Corinthians 1:22). The New American Bible's note on Revelation 7 cites all three.

The Catechism teaches this of the sacraments.

- **Baptism.** "Baptism seals the Christian with the indelible spiritual mark (character) of his belonging to Christ. No sin can erase this mark" (CCC 1272). "The Holy Spirit has marked us with the seal of the Lord… 'for the day of redemption'" (CCC 1274). At the beginning of the rite, "the sign of the cross… marks with the imprint of Christ the one who is going to belong to him" (CCC 1235). It is traced on the forehead.
- **Confirmation.** "By this anointing the confirmand receives the 'mark,' the seal of the Holy Spirit. A seal is a symbol of a person, a sign of personal authority, or ownership… Hence soldiers were marked with their leader's seal and slaves with their master's" (CCC 1295). The bishop anoints the forehead with chrism and says, "Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit."
- **And its purpose in the trial.** "This seal of the Holy Spirit marks our total belonging to Christ, our enrollment in his service for ever, as well as the promise of divine protection in the great eschatological trial" (CCC 1296). The Catechism's footnote to that sentence cites this very passage, Revelation 7:2–3, with 9:4 and Ezekiel 9:4–6.

[[auth:church]]

So the Church reads John's vision of every baptized and confirmed Christian. The angel with the seal is not only a figure of the last days. He describes what was done to us at the font and under the bishop's hand.

## Kept until the end

The seal of Baptism cannot be erased, and it does not by itself bring a person to heaven. "No sin can erase this mark, even if sin prevents Baptism from bearing the fruits of salvation" (CCC 1272). The Catechism speaks of the Christian who has "'kept the seal' until the end, remaining faithful to the demands of his Baptism," and so departs this life "marked with the sign of faith" (CCC 1274). [[auth:church]]

This is why the seal appears again in chapter 14 with a description of those who bear it: they follow the Lamb, and no lie is found in them (14:4–5). The seal is given; it must also be kept.

## Read with the Woman

In chapter 12 the dragon makes war on "the rest of her offspring" (12:17), and John does not say there how they will stand. Chapter 7 has already said it. They were sealed before the war began.

The first of the sealed, in the order of grace, is the Woman herself read as Mary: claimed for God from the first moment of her life, "full of grace," on whom the dragon "had no hold" (CCC 2853). What was given to her at her conception is given to her children at the font. [[auth:theologian]]

## Where readers differ

- **A visible mark?** The seal is not a physical sign on the skin. The sacramental character is a "spiritual mark" (CCC 1272). The cross traced on the forehead is its outward sign.
- **Only for the last days?** Some read the sealing as a special act for the final generation. The Catechism applies it to all who are confirmed, while keeping its reference to "the great eschatological trial." Both hold: every Christian is sealed, and the seal will matter most when the trial is greatest. [[auth:disputed]]
- **Who the 144,000 are.** That is the next page.
- **Why the tribe of Dan is missing.** The note in the New American Bible records "a later tradition that the antichrist would arise from it." St. Irenaeus gives this reason; other explanations have been offered, and the Church has taught none.

## What it asks of us

To remember that we have been marked. Most Christians were sealed as infants and confirmed as children, and think of neither. John puts the sealing before the storm so that those in the storm will know they were claimed first.

And to bear it where it can be seen. It is put on the forehead. "The Christian begins his day, his prayers, and his activities with the Sign of the Cross" (CCC 2157). Each time, the hand goes to the place where the seal was set.
