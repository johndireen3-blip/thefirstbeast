Mark 11 · The beast from the earth

Control of Buying and Selling

The beast's mark is pressed on everyone, small and great, and tied to daily bread: no one can buy or sell without it. It imitates God's own seal, and it is the one point in the chapter where the reader must choose.

Scripture

What the text shows

The second beast "forced all the people, small and great, rich and poor, free and slave, to be given a stamped image on their right hands or their foreheads, so that no one could buy or sell except one who had the stamped image of the beast's name or the number that stood for its name" (Revelation 13:16–17).

The mark is universal: no class is exempt. It is forced: pressed on people, not invented by them. It is visible: on the right hand or the forehead. It bears the beast's name or number (mark 13). And it is tied to daily life: without it, no one can buy or sell.

A stamp, not an image

The Greek word is charagma: a stamp, an engraving, the impress on a coin or a seal, the brand on a slave or a soldier. It is a different word from the eikōn, the image of the beast that speaks (mark 10). Revelation keeps the two together, "the beast or its image, or… its mark" (14:9), but they are not the same thing.

The seal it imitates

Before the trial begins, God's servants are sealed: "Do not damage the land or the sea or the trees until we put the seal on the foreheads of the servants of our God" (Revelation 7:3). The Lamb's followers bear "his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads" (14:1), and in the end "his name will be on their foreheads" (22:4). Ezekiel saw the faithful in Jerusalem marked on the forehead before judgment (Ezekiel 9:4), and Israel was told to bind God's words "on your arm as a sign" and "as a pendant on your forehead" (Deuteronomy 6:8). The beast's mark counterfeits all of these. The question Revelation puts is not whether we will be marked, but whose name we will bear.

The first stamp on us was God's. St. Peter Chrysologus, Doctor of the Church, says of creation: “The second Adam stamped his image on the first Adam when he created him,” and took Adam's name “in order that he might not lose what he had made in his own image.” The beast's stamp is pressed over an image already there. The remedy he gives is to wear the true image fully: “let us put on the complete image of our Creator so as to be wholly like him, not in the glory that he alone possesses, but in innocence, simplicity, gentleness, patience, humility, mercy, harmony” (Sermon 117; Office of Readings, Saturday of the Twenty-ninth Week in Ordinary Time) A Father or Doctor.

Worship and the mark

The mark is never morally neutral in Revelation. The third angel joins it to worship in the gravest warning of the book: "Anyone who worships the beast or its image, or accepts its mark on forehead or hand, will also drink the wine of God's fury" (14:9–10). Those who reign with Christ are those who "had not worshiped the beast or its image nor had accepted its mark" (20:4). The mark is the outward sign of an inward surrender.

First fulfillment

In the cities of Asia, trade was carried on through guilds with patron gods and through markets under the emperor's protection, and coins bore the emperor's image and titles. To buy and sell freely often meant taking part, at least outwardly, in honoring the gods and the emperor; to refuse could mean being shut out of the guild and the market A theologian. Christ's question about the tribute coin, "Whose image and inscription is this?" (Mark 12:16), stands behind the mark.

The Fathers

St. Irenaeus takes the mark of the beast and its number literally, as belonging to the one to come (Against Heresies V.28.2; V.30) A Father or Doctor. St. Augustine reads the forehead as what we profess and the hand as what we do: to bear the mark is to belong to the beast in confession and in action (City of God XX.9) A Father or Doctor. The Fathers agree that the mark is received by consent, and that it is refused by the faithful at any cost Fathers in agreement.

The Church Church teaching

Many martyrs died rather than worship "the Beast," refusing even to pretend to (CCC 2113). The Church teaches that an economy which makes profit "the exclusive norm and ultimate end" is morally unacceptable (CCC 2424), and that the person may never be reduced to a means. Pope Leo XIV warns that technology "takes on the characteristics of those who devise, finance, regulate and use it" (Magnifica Humanitas 9).

Where interpreters differ Disputed

  • Exclusion from the market of the empire and its gods: the first meaning for John's readers.
  • A literal mark at the end: St. Irenaeus and many later writers.
  • Allegiance in confession and conduct: St. Augustine; the forehead and the hand.
  • A technology of identity and payment: many modern readers see the conditions of the mark in systems that identify every person and control every transaction. No technology is the mark by itself; it becomes the mark only when joined to the worship of the beast (14:9).

The Church has not decided what the mark will be made of.

The response asked of us

We were sealed already, at Baptism and Confirmation (CCC 1272–1274, 1295–1296). Bear that seal openly. No one receives the beast's mark by accident: God does not trick his children, and the mark is joined to worship (14:9). Prepare now to refuse it, even at the cost of buying and selling, and do not be afraid: "Here is what sustains the holy ones who keep God's commandments and their faith in Jesus" (14:12).
