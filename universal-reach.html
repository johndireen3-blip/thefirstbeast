Mark 8 · The beast from the sea

Universal Reach

The beast is granted authority over every tribe, people, tongue and nation: the very kingdoms the devil once offered to Christ, and Christ refused.

What the text shows

The beast "was granted authority over every tribe, people, tongue, and nation," and "all the inhabitants of the earth will worship it," except those whose names are written in the Lamb's book of life (Revelation 13:7–8). The authority is granted, like everything the beast has. Its reach is the whole world; its limit is the book of life.

Revelation 17 fills in how the reach is gained. Ten kings "are of one mind and will give their power and authority to the beast" (Revelation 17:13). The waters the harlot sits on "represent large numbers of peoples, nations, and tongues" (Revelation 17:15). Even this serves God: "God has put it into their minds to carry out his purpose" until his words are fulfilled (Revelation 17:17).

The kingdoms the devil offered

The same four words, tribe, people, tongue and nation, describe those the Lamb ransomed by his blood (Revelation 5:9). The beast claims what the Lamb has bought.

The devil once showed Christ "all the kingdoms of the world in a single instant" and said, "I shall give to you all this power and their glory; for it has been handed over to me." The price was worship: "All this will be yours, if you worship me." Christ answered, "You shall worship the Lord, your God, and him alone shall you serve" (Luke 4:5–8). The beast accepts the offer Christ refused, and its universal reach and the worship of mark 6 come together.

Daniel's fourth kingdom

Daniel's fourth beast is a fourth kingdom that will "devour the whole earth, trample it down and crush it" (Daniel 7:23). John's beast inherits that appetite (mark 2).

First fulfillment

For John's first readers, the beast's reach was Rome's, which called its empire the inhabited world: Caesar Augustus decreed "that the whole world should be enrolled" (Luke 2:1). The great city that "has sovereignty over the kings of the earth" (Revelation 17:18) is commonly read in modern commentary as Rome A theologian.

The Fathers

St. Irenaeus reads the ten kings as the kingdoms into which the empire of his day will be divided. The one to come will slay three of them and "subject the remainder to his power," and they "shall give their kingdom to the beast" (Against Heresies V.26.1) A Father or Doctor. The Fathers agree that the last enemy will hold a rule wider than any before him, and that it will be short Fathers in agreement.

The Church Church teaching

The Church does not condemn the unity of nations. Popes have called for a true world political authority to serve the common good, bound by law and by the moral order (St. John XXIII, Pacem in Terris 137; Benedict XVI, Caritas in Veritate 67). What she condemns is power that recognizes nothing above itself: societies that reject God's vision of man "arrogate to themselves an explicit or implicit totalitarian power over man and his destiny" (CCC 2244). The beast's reach is not wrong for being wide but for being given by the dragon and turned to worship.

Where interpreters differ Disputed

  • Rome's empire: the first meaning for John's readers.
  • One man's rule over the whole earth at the end: St. Irenaeus and the Fathers.
  • A world state or global system: some modern readers, who see it in a single world government or in economic and technological control.
  • A leading power whose reach touches every nation: others, who read "every tribe, people, tongue and nation" as influence, not one government.

The Church has not decided which the text intends at the end.

The response asked of us

The beast's reach stops at the book of life (Revelation 13:8). The Lamb's people are already drawn "from every tribe and tongue, people and nation" (Revelation 5:9), and the eternal gospel is proclaimed to the same peoples (Revelation 14:6). Against a power that claims the nations, the Church keeps the nations for Christ, and prays, "Thy kingdom come."
