REVELATION 17:01–06

Babylon the great.

Babylon the great.

01One of the seven angels who held the seven bowls comes and says to John: Come, I will show you the judgment of the great harlot who sits on many waters.

02The kings of the earth have committed immorality with her, and the earth’s inhabitants have become drunk on the wine of her unfaithfulness.

03The angel carries John in the spirit into a desert, and he sees a woman seated on a scarlet beast covered with blasphemous names, with seven heads and ten horns.

04She is dressed in purple and scarlet and glitters with gold, jewels and pearls. In her hand is a golden cup full of abominations and the filth of her immorality.

05On her forehead a name is written, a mystery: Babylon the great, mother of harlots and of the earth’s abominations.

06John sees that the woman is drunk with the blood of the holy ones and the blood of the witnesses to Jesus.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Chapter 17 begins the long description of Babylon’s fall that runs to 19:10. The USCCB notes say the punishment of Babylon is now told as a past event, under the image of a harlot who leads people into idolatry, and that “Babylon” is the symbolic name of Rome. The first Babylon destroyed Jerusalem and its temple in 587 BC; Rome did the same in AD 70, and early Christians used the name for Rome (1 Pet 5:13). In the prophets, unfaithfulness to God is called harlotry, and the notes explain that the kings’ “intercourse” with her means adopting the cult of the emperor. Her purple, gold and pearls are the wealth of empire. Contrast her with the Woman of chapter 12, clothed with the sun and persecuted in the desert: one woman is the faithful People of God, the other the seductive city that persecutes them.

REVELATION 17:06–18

The Lamb will conquer.

The Lamb will conquer.

07When John sees her he is greatly amazed. The angel says: Why are you amazed? I will tell you the mystery of the woman and of the beast with seven heads and ten horns that carries her.

08The beast you saw once was and now is not; it will come up from the abyss and go to destruction. Those whose names are not written in the book of life will marvel when they see the beast that was, is not, and is to come.

09This calls for a mind with wisdom. The seven heads are seven hills on which the woman sits. They are also seven kings:

10five have fallen, one is living, and the other has not yet come; when he comes he must stay only a little while.

11The beast that was and is not is itself an eighth king, yet it belongs to the seven, and it is going to destruction.

12The ten horns are ten kings who have not yet received a kingdom; they will receive authority as kings with the beast for one hour.

13They are of one mind and hand over their power and authority to the beast.

14They will make war on the Lamb, but the Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings, and those with him are called, chosen and faithful.

15The angel goes on: The waters you saw, where the harlot sits, are peoples and crowds, nations and languages.

16The ten horns and the beast will come to hate the harlot; they will strip her and leave her desolate, devour her flesh and burn her up with fire.

17For God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose, to agree together and give their kingdom to the beast until God’s words are fulfilled.

18The woman you saw is the great city that rules over the kings of the earth.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The angel’s explanation points clearly to Rome. The USCCB notes say the seven hills are those of Rome, and that the seven kings are emperors, though there is little agreement on which; the number seven suggests that all the emperors are meant. The beast that “was and is not” and will return alludes, the notes explain, to the popular belief that the dead Nero would come back to power (see 13:3). The ten kings may be Parthian rulers expected to march on Rome with the returning Nero. The chapter ends with a dark irony: evil devours itself. The beast and its allies turn on the city that rode on them, and God uses even their hatred to accomplish his purpose (compare Ezek 16:37–41). Over all of it stands the promise of verse 14: the Lamb will conquer.

TWO WOMEN, TWO CITIES
01

REVELATION 17:1–5

The great harlot

The prophets called idolatrous cities harlots: Tyre (Isa 23:16–17), Nineveh (Nah 3:4), even unfaithful Jerusalem (Ezek 16). Babylon is the opposite of the Woman of chapter 12 and of the Bride of chapter 21: a city of seduction, not of faithfulness.

Read Ezekiel 16
02

REVELATION 17:4

The golden cup

Jeremiah called Babylon a golden cup in the Lord’s hand that made the whole earth drunk (Jer 51:7). Its gold is real; what fills it is corruption. Glitter on the outside, emptiness within.

Read Jeremiah 51
03

REVELATION 17:3, 9

The scarlet beast and seven hills

The beast of chapter 13 returns, now carrying the city. Rome was famous as the city of seven hills. The political power that props up the city’s wealth and worship is the same power that persecutes the faithful.

Read our page on the first beast
04

REVELATION 17:8, 11

The beast that was and is not

A mocking parody of God, “who is and who was and who is to come” (1:8). The beast imitates resurrection but goes only “to destruction.” Evil always borrows the shape of God’s glory and never has its substance.

Read Chapter 1
05

REVELATION 17:14

Lord of lords and King of kings

The title belongs to God in Deuteronomy 10:17 and is given to the Lamb here and in 19:16. The kings make war; the Lamb, with his called, chosen and faithful companions, conquers. This verse is the heart of the whole book.

Read Deuteronomy 10
READ WITH CARE

Over the centuries, the harlot Babylon has been identified with the papacy and the Catholic Church, especially in Reformation-era and some modern polemics, with the seven hills taken as proof. The USCCB notes, following most scholars, identify Babylon with imperial Rome of John’s day, the persecuting power that demanded the worship of the emperor and was drunk with the blood of the martyrs, including Peter and Paul. The harlot is the city that killed the apostles, not the Church they founded. In every age, Babylon stands for any society that makes wealth, power or the state an idol.

CUP & SEDUCTION

What glitters in my life?

Babylon’s cup is gold on the outside. What attractive thing in my life, money, status, approval, quietly draws me away from God?

REVELATION 17:4

CALLED & FAITHFUL

Am I among the faithful?

Those with the Lamb are called, chosen and faithful. God has called and chosen me. Where is he asking me, this week, simply to be faithful?

REVELATION 17:14

POWER & TRUST

Where do I place my security?

The beast turns on the city that trusted it. Am I relying on any power, political, financial or personal, more than I rely on God?

WHERE IT FITS

From witness to reign.

Chapter 17 opens the section on the fall of Babylon (17:1–19:10), filling out the brief announcements of 14:8 and 16:19. The beast of chapter 13 reappears, now carrying the city, and the false Woman of this chapter stands in deliberate contrast to the Woman clothed with the sun in chapter 12 and the Bride of the Lamb in chapters 19 and 21. The war of the kings against the Lamb (17:14) is shown in 19:11–21. Chapter 18 gives the lament over the fallen city.
