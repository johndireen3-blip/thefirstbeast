Full text & notes on USCCB
REVELATION 22:01–05

They will see his face.

They will see his face.

01The angel shows John the river of the water of life, sparkling like crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb

02down the middle of the city’s street. On either side of the river grows the tree of life, bearing twelve kinds of fruit, a crop each month, and its leaves serve to heal the nations.

03Nothing accursed will be there any more. The throne of God and of the Lamb will be in the city, and his servants will worship him.

04They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads.

05There will be no more night; they will need no lamp or sunlight, because the Lord God will shine on them, and they will reign forever and ever.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The Bible ends where it began, in a garden with a river and a tree of life (Gen 2:9–10). What was lost when humanity was driven from Eden (Gen 3:22–24) is given back, and more: the garden is now a city, and the curse is lifted. The river comes from Ezekiel’s vision of water flowing from the temple and turning the Dead Sea fresh, with trees whose fruit is food and whose leaves are medicine (Ezek 47:1–12); the USCCB notes cross-reference it here. The Fathers saw in the living water the gift of the Spirit (John 7:37–39) and the waters of Baptism. Verse 4 is the summit of Revelation: “They will see his face.” Moses could not see God’s face and live (Exod 33:20); now the servants of God see him. The Church calls this the beatific vision (CCC 1023–1028).

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 22:06–15

Blessed are those who wash their robes.

Blessed are those who wash their robes.

06The angel says to John: These words are trustworthy and true. The Lord, the God of the spirits of the prophets, has sent his angel to show his servants what must happen soon.

07Look, I am coming soon. Blessed is the one who keeps the prophetic words of this book.

08I, John, am the one who heard and saw these things. When I had heard and seen them, I fell down to worship at the feet of the angel who showed them to me.

09But he said to me: Do not do that! I am a fellow servant with you, your brothers the prophets, and those who keep the words of this book. Worship God.

10Then he told me: Do not seal up the prophetic words of this book, for the time is near.

11Let the evildoer still do evil and the filthy still be filthy; let the righteous still do right and the holy still be holy.

12Look, I am coming soon, and my reward is with me, to repay each one according to their deeds.

13I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end.

14Blessed are those who wash their robes, so that they may have the right to the tree of life and may enter the city through its gates.

15Outside are the dogs, the sorcerers, the sexually immoral, murderers, idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The book closes as it opened, with a blessing on those who keep its words (1:3) and the promise “I am coming soon.” The last two of Revelation’s seven beatitudes are here (22:7, 14). Daniel was told to seal up his vision until the end (Dan 12:4); John is told the opposite, because the time is near: Revelation is meant to be read and lived now. Christ applies to himself the title God used in 1:8 and 21:6, “the Alpha and the Omega.” Verse 11 is not a counsel of despair but a sober warning: the direction a life is taking will become its final shape. Those who “wash their robes,” made white in the blood of the Lamb (7:14), regain the tree of life that Adam lost.

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 22:16–21

The Spirit and the bride say, Come.

The Spirit and the bride say, Come.

16I, Jesus, have sent my angel to give you this testimony for the churches. I am the root and offspring of David, the bright morning star.

17The Spirit and the bride say: Come. Let everyone who hears say: Come. Let the one who is thirsty come; let anyone who wishes take the water of life as a gift.

18I warn everyone who hears the prophetic words of this book: if anyone adds to them, God will add to that person the plagues described in this book;

19and if anyone takes away from the words of this prophetic book, God will take away that person’s share in the tree of life and in the holy city described in this book.

20The one who gives this testimony says: Yes, I am coming soon. Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!

21The grace of the Lord Jesus be with all.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Jesus speaks in his own name for the last time in the Bible. He is the root and offspring of David, the Messiah promised in Isaiah 11:1, 10, and the morning star (Num 24:17) promised to the faithful in 2:28. Then the Church answers. “The Spirit and the bride” is the Church moved by the Holy Spirit, and her word is “Come”: both a prayer that Christ will come and an invitation to the world to come to him. The warning against adding or removing words echoes Deuteronomy 4:2 and protects the integrity of the prophecy. The USCCB notes explain that “Come, Lord Jesus” was a liturgical refrain, like the Aramaic Marana tha, “Our Lord, come!” (1 Cor 16:22), a prayer for Christ’s glorious coming. The Church still prays it at every Mass: “as we await the blessed hope and the coming of our Savior, Jesus Christ.” Revelation ends not with a beast or a battle, but with grace.

02 / THE SYMBOLS

Promises drawn

Read the signs in their scriptural and historical context.

THE END IS A FACE, NOT A FEAR
01

REVELATION 22:1

The river of the water of life

Ezekiel saw a river flow from the temple and bring life wherever it went (Ezek 47:1–12). Jesus promised rivers of living water, the Spirit (John 7:37–39). Its source is the throne of God and of the Lamb.

Read Ezekiel 47
02

REVELATION 22:2

The tree of life

Guarded by cherubim after the Fall (Gen 3:24), now open to all, with leaves to heal the nations. The Fathers saw the Cross as the new tree of life, whose fruit is Christ himself in the Eucharist.

Read Genesis 2
03

REVELATION 22:4

They will see his face

The deepest longing of the Psalms, “your face, LORD, do I seek” (Ps 27:8), is fulfilled. Seeing God face to face (1 Cor 13:12; 1 John 3:2) is what the Church calls the beatific vision (CCC 1023, 1028).

Read Psalm 27
04

REVELATION 22:16

The bright morning star

The morning star announces the dawn after the night. Balaam foresaw a star rising from Jacob (Num 24:17), and Christ promised the morning star to the faithful in Thyatira (2:28). The Exsultet at the Easter Vigil sings of Christ as the Morning Star who never sets.

Read Numbers 24
05

REVELATION 22:20

Come, Lord Jesus

One of the oldest Christian prayers, preserved in Aramaic as Marana tha (1 Cor 16:22; Didache 10). The Church prays it in every Mass as she awaits the Lord’s return (CCC 671, 2817).

Read 1 Corinthians 16
READ WITH CARE

“I am coming soon” has led some to set dates for Christ’s return and others to conclude that Revelation was simply mistaken, since nearly two thousand years have passed. The Church reads “soon” in the light of 2 Peter 3:8–9: with the Lord a thousand years are like a day, and his patience is for our salvation. Since the Ascension, the Catechism teaches, Christ’s coming in glory is “imminent,” though “it is not for you to know times or seasons” (CCC 673). The warning of verses 18–19 is about tampering with this prophecy, not a ruling on which books belong in the Bible; the canon was discerned by the Church over time.

01 / 03

FACE & LONGING

Do I long to see God’s face?

The end of the whole story is not a place but a face. When I pray, am I seeking gifts from God, or God himself? Can I spend five minutes this week simply in his presence, before the Blessed Sacrament if I can?

REVELATION 22:4

WASHED & FORGIVEN

When did I last wash my robe?

Those who wash their robes in the Lamb’s blood enter the city. Is it time for me to return to the sacrament of Reconciliation?

REVELATION 22:14

COME & WITNESS

Whom can I invite to come?

The Spirit and the bride say “Come,” and all who hear are to say it too. Who in my life is thirsty, and how can I invite them to the water of life?

WHERE IT FITS

From witness to reign.

Chapter 22 completes the vision of the new Jerusalem begun in 21:9 and closes the whole book, and the whole Bible. It returns to the beginning of Revelation: the angel who brought the message (1:1), the blessing on those who keep its words (1:3), the Alpha and the Omega (1:8), the testimony for the churches (chapters 2–3). It returns, too, to the first pages of Genesis: the river, the tree of life, the lifting of the curse. From the Lamb who was slain (chapter 5) to the Lamb on the throne, the message is one: the Lamb’s victory. The study ends where the Church begins each day, with a prayer: Come, Lord Jesus.
