Revelation 7:3; 13:16–17; 14:1, 9–11; 22:4

Two Marks on the Forehead

Revelation knows two marks, and in the end everyone bears one of them. Set side by side, each explains the other.

The texts
"Do not damage the land or the sea or the trees until we put the seal on the foreheads of the servants of our God" (7:3). "It forced all the people, small and great, rich and poor, free and slave, to be given a stamped image on their right hands or their foreheads, so that no one could buy or sell except one who had the stamped image of the beast's name or the number that stood for its name" (13:16–17). "With him a hundred and forty-four thousand who had his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads" (14:1). "They will look upon his face, and his name will be on their foreheads" (22:4).
In one sentence
Both marks are a name, and each says to whom a person belongs; the beast's is an imitation of God's, taken by an act of worship, and it cannot be received by accident.
Authority
Scripture

The seal came first

A reader who begins at chapter 13 meets the mark of the beast as something new and frightening. A reader who begins at chapter 7 knows better. God had already sealed his servants on the forehead (The seal of the living God). The beast's mark is the counterfeit of something that existed before him.

This is how the dragon works throughout these chapters. He does not create; he copies (The dragon on the shore). The beast has a healed wound to imitate the Lamb who was slain (13:3; 5:6). The world asks of him, "Who can compare with the beast?" in imitation of Michael's name (13:4). And he marks his own, because God has marked his.

Two words

John uses two different Greek words, and never confuses them.

  • The seal is sphragis: the impression of a signet ring, a sign of ownership and protection. It is used only of what belongs to God.
  • The mark is charagma: something cut, stamped or branded. The New American Bible translates it "a stamped image." It was the word for the emperor's stamp on documents and his image on coins. It is used only of the beast.

What the table shows

Both are a name. This is the most important thing to see. The seal is the name of the Lamb and of his Father. The mark is the name of the beast, or its number. In Scripture, to bear someone's name is to belong to him. The question the two marks ask is not what is on a person's skin. It is whose he is.

Both are joined to worship, every time. The mark is never mentioned apart from the worship of the beast: "Anyone who worships the beast or its image, or accepts its mark" (14:9); those who "had not worshiped the beast or its image nor had accepted its mark" (20:4). The partner site states the consequence: the mark is the outward sign of an inward act. See The First Beast: buying and selling.

One is given; the other is imposed. The seal is placed on those who are already God's servants. The mark is forced "on all," by threat of death (13:15) and by shutting people out of trade (13:17). God seals; the beast compels.

The mark offers something now. It lets a person buy and sell. The seal offers no advantage in the market. The choice between them is a choice between a present good and a promised one.

The forehead and the hand. The mark may be on either. Many commentators, ancient and modern, read the forehead as what a person believes and confesses, and the hand as what he does. The beast is content with either: with conviction, or with mere compliance. God's seal is on the forehead only. A theologian

The Old Testament behind both

Israel was commanded to wear God's word in exactly these two places: "Bind them on your arm as a sign and let them be as a pendant on your forehead" (Deuteronomy 6:8). The words to be bound there begin, "Hear, O Israel! The LORD is our God, the LORD alone! Therefore, you shall love the LORD, your God, with your whole heart" (6:4–5). It was the confession of the one God, worn on the hand and between the eyes.

The beast's mark, on the hand or the forehead, is a parody of this. It puts another name where the name of the one God belongs. That is why the first of the three angels answers it with the first commandment: "Worship him who made heaven and earth" (14:7). See Three angels. Scripture

For John's first readers

The first-century reading gives the mark a setting that explains every detail. In the cities of Asia, to buy and sell meant handling coins stamped with the emperor's image and divine titles, belonging to trade guilds that feasted in honor of pagan gods, and at times producing proof of having sacrificed. The notes of the New American Bible: "Christians had either to worship the emperor and his image or to suffer martyrdom" (note on 13:11–18). To refuse was to be shut out of ordinary economic life. A theologian

This shows what kind of thing the mark is. It was not a mystery to the Christians of Smyrna or Pergamum. It was the price of taking part in a society organized around a false worship.

What the Church teaches

The Church has not defined what form the mark will take at the end. She has taught clearly about the two things it is made of.

  • The seal. Christians are sealed in Baptism and Confirmation, and that seal is "the promise of divine protection in the great eschatological trial" (CCC 1296).
  • Idolatry. "Man commits idolatry whenever he honors and reveres a creature in place of God, whether this be gods or demons… power, pleasure, race, ancestors, the state, money… Many martyrs died for not adoring 'the Beast,' refusing even to simulate such worship" (CCC 2113).

Church teaching

"Refusing even to simulate." The martyrs were often offered an easy way out: a pinch of incense, a certificate, an outward gesture with no inward belief. The hand without the forehead. They refused that too.

No one takes the mark by accident

This follows from everything above, and it needs to be said plainly because many Christians have been frightened by this passage.

The mark is the name of the beast, taken in an act of worshiping him. A person cannot worship without knowing it. No object, number, document or device is the mark in itself; a thing could become its instrument only if it were made the sign of that worship. God does not allow his children to be damned by a trick. The third angel's warning is given "in a loud voice," to everyone, in advance (14:9), so that the choice, when it comes, will be a real one.

And those who bear God's seal are not left to face that choice by their own strength. They were sealed first. A theologian

Read with the Woman

The Woman bears no mark in chapter 12. She is clothed with the sun, and that is enough: she is wrapped in what belongs to God. Her children are those "who keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus" (12:17), the two things the mark exists to prevent. The seal is the name of her Son on their foreheads.

Where readers differ

  • Literal or symbolic. Whether the mark at the end will be a visible, physical sign, or is a figure for open allegiance. The text allows both, and the Church has not said. Disputed
  • Past, present or future. Rome's coins and certificates of sacrifice; every regime that makes livelihood depend on a false worship; a last and universal instance. The pattern recurs.
  • Modern candidates. In every generation some technology or system has been proposed as the mark. This study names none. By the test of the text, nothing is the mark unless it is joined to the worship of the beast.

What it asks of us

To stop being afraid of the wrong thing. The danger in Revelation 13 is not that a Christian might be marked without knowing it. It is that a Christian might know exactly what is being asked, and consent, because the cost of refusing is his livelihood.

That choice is not only for the last days. It is made in small ways whenever a person is asked to say what he does not believe, or to honor what he should not honor, as the price of getting on. The seal was given for those moments. "The Lord knows those who are his" (2 Timothy 2:19).

And to look to the end of the book. The last word on the forehead is not the beast's. "They will look upon his face, and his name will be on their foreheads" (22:4).
