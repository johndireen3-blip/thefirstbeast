Revelation 12:17

The Rest of Her Offspring

The dragon cannot reach the Son and cannot destroy the Woman, so he goes to war against her other children. The verse tells us who they are, and so tells us who she is to us.

The text
"Then the dragon became angry with the woman and went off to wage war against the rest of her offspring, those who keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus" (Revelation 12:17).
In one sentence
The Woman who bore the Messiah has other children, the faithful; they are the brothers and sisters of Christ, known by obedience and witness, and they are the ones the dragon now fights.
Authority
Scripture

The words

  • Her offspring. The Greek is sperma, "seed." It is the only time the word appears in Revelation, and it is the word of the Greek Old Testament in Genesis 3:15: "I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers." The New American Bible's cross-reference for this verse is Genesis 3:15. John closes the chapter by naming the promise it has been showing.
  • The rest. She has had one child already, the male child of verse 5. "The rest" are those who come after him and belong with him. St. Paul calls Christ "the firstborn among many brothers" (Romans 8:29).
  • To wage war. The same phrase is used of the beast against the two witnesses (11:7) and against the holy ones (13:7). The war announced here is carried out in the next chapter.
  • Keep… and bear witness. Two marks, one of life and one of speech. They return in 14:12 as the description of the saints: "the holy ones who keep God's commandments and their faith in Jesus."

Scripture

Who they are

The verse defines them itself. They are not a nation, a class or an elite. They are "those who keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus."

John had used the second phrase of himself at the beginning of the book: he was on Patmos "because I proclaimed God's word and gave testimony to Jesus," and he writes as "your brother, who share with you the distress, the kingdom, and the endurance we have in Jesus" (1:9). At the end an angel uses it of all the faithful: "I am a fellow servant of yours and of your brothers who bear witness to Jesus" (19:10).

So the rest of the Woman's offspring are the Christians: everyone who, in any age, lives by God's commandments and confesses his Son. The Lord described the same family: "Stretching out his hand toward his disciples, he said, 'Here are my mother and my brothers. For whoever does the will of my heavenly Father is my brother, and sister, and mother'" (Matthew 12:49–50). And the Letter to the Hebrews: "He is not ashamed to call them 'brothers'" (Hebrews 2:11). Scripture

Whose children

If Christ's brothers and sisters are the Woman's offspring, then she is their mother. The Church has received this in both senses of the Woman.

Mary, Mother of the Church

"Standing by the cross of Jesus were his mother" and the disciple whom he loved. "He said to his mother, 'Woman, behold, your son.' Then he said to the disciple, 'Behold, your mother'" (John 19:25–27). He calls her "Woman," the word of Genesis 3:15 and of Revelation 12, and gives her a son who is not the child of her body.

The Church teaches:

  • She is "clearly the mother of the members of Christ… since she has by her charity joined in bringing about the birth of believers in the Church, who are members of its head" (CCC 963, quoting St. Augustine through Lumen Gentium 53).
  • "In a wholly singular way she cooperated by her obedience, faith, hope, and burning charity in the Savior's work of restoring supernatural life to souls. For this reason she is a mother to us in the order of grace" (CCC 968).
  • "This motherhood of Mary in the order of grace continues uninterruptedly from the consent which she loyally gave at the Annunciation and which she sustained without wavering beneath the cross, until the eternal fulfilment of all the elect" (CCC 969).

Pope St. Paul VI proclaimed her Mother of the Church at the Second Vatican Council in 1964, and he opened his letter on the Woman of Revelation 12 by calling her "the mother of all men by the grace of Christ the Redeemer" (Signum Magnum). Since 2018 the whole Latin Church keeps the memorial of Mary, Mother of the Church, on the Monday after Pentecost. Church teaching

This motherhood takes nothing from her Son: it "in no way obscures or diminishes this unique mediation of Christ, but rather shows its power" (CCC 970). Church teaching

The Church, our mother

St. Paul had already written, "the Jerusalem above is freeborn, and she is our mother" (Galatians 4:26). The Catechism: "The Church is the mother of all believers. 'No one can have God as Father who does not have the Church as Mother'" (CCC 181, quoting St. Cyprian). She is "called 'that Jerusalem which is above' and 'our mother'" (CCC 757). By preaching and Baptism "she brings forth sons, who are conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of God" (CCC 507). Church teaching

One motherhood

The two are not in competition. Mary is mother of the members of Christ because she is mother of the Head; the Church is mother because she does in every age, by word and sacrament, what was done once in Mary. This is the point at which the chapter's readings of the Woman come to rest: verse 5 speaks most clearly of Mary, verse 6 of the Church, and verse 17 of both together, the mother of one Son and of many.

Why the dragon turns on them

The verse gives the order of his defeats. He could not devour the child (12:5). He was thrown out of heaven (12:9). He could not sweep away the Woman (12:16). "Then the dragon became angry with the woman and went off to wage war against the rest of her offspring."

Two things follow, and both are stated by the notes of the New American Bible: "Although the church is protected by God's special providence (Rev 12:16), the individual Christian is to expect persecution and suffering" (note on 12:17). A theologian

  • The Woman is safe; her children are attacked. The Church as a whole cannot be destroyed (Matthew 16:18). That promise is not made to each Christian's comfort, safety or life. "If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you" (John 15:20). "All who want to live religiously in Christ Jesus will be persecuted" (2 Timothy 3:12).
  • He attacks what marks them. He goes after those who keep and bear witness. The war of chapter 13 is aimed at exactly these two things: a deception that would have them break the first commandment by worshiping the beast (13:8, 15), and a mark that would silence their witness (13:16–17).

Where readers differ

  • Who "the rest" are in contrast to. Most read: Christ the firstborn, and then all Christians. Some early readers, taking the Woman as Israel, read the male child as Christ and "the rest" as the Gentile believers; St. Methodius, taking the child as the baptized, has to read "the rest" otherwise. The Church has not ruled, and the plain sense is the first. Disputed
  • Whether the Woman and her offspring are two groups. If the Woman is the Church, how are her children different from her? The usual answer is that John shows the Church twice: as a whole, which God preserves, and in her members, who suffer. The Marian reading has no difficulty here, since a mother and her children are plainly distinct.
  • When. The war is the Church's whole history, with a last and worst chapter still to come (CCC 675).

What it asks of us

The verse gives every Christian two things to do and one thing to know. Keep the commandments. Bear witness to Jesus. And know that this, and nothing else, is what draws the dragon's anger. Chapter 14 repeats the two marks and adds a word: "Here is what sustains the holy ones who keep God's commandments and their faith in Jesus" (14:12).

It also gives us a mother. The disciple at the Cross "took her into his home" (John 19:27). A child of the Woman who is at war does well to stay near her.
