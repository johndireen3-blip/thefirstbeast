1 JOHN 2:18; 2 THESSALONIANS 2; REVELATION 13

What Does the Catholic Church Teach About the Antichrist?

What does the Catholic Church teach about the Antichrist? She teaches that before Christ returns the Church will pass through a final trial, and that its heart is a religious deception: man putting himself in the place of God. She names no one, and she sets no date.

The word is not in Revelation

The word “antichrist” appears in the Bible only in the letters of St. John (1 John 2:18, 22; 4:3; 2 John 7), where it describes those who deny that Jesus is the Christ come in the flesh, and where John says that many antichrists have already come. Revelation speaks instead of a beast and a false prophet, and St. Paul of “the lawless one” (2 Thessalonians 2:8). Christian tradition has read these together.

What the Catechism says

“Before Christ’s second coming the Church must pass through a final trial that will shake the faith of many believers. The persecution that accompanies her pilgrimage on earth will unveil the ‘mystery of iniquity’ in the form of a religious deception offering men an apparent solution to their problems at the price of apostasy from the truth. The supreme religious deception is that of the Antichrist, a pseudo-messianism by which man glorifies himself in place of God and of his Messiah come in the flesh” (CCC 675).

A person, a pattern, or both?

The Catechism speaks of “the Antichrist,” and also says that his deception “already begins to take shape in the world every time the claim is made to realize within history that messianic hope which can only be realized beyond history” (CCC 676). So there is a pattern that recurs in every age, and the tradition expects it to come to a head. Many of the Fathers expected a single man at the end. The Church has defined neither his identity nor his time.

How it ends

St. Paul says the Lord will destroy the lawless one “with the breath of his mouth and render powerless by the manifestation of his coming” (2 Thessalonians 2:8). In Revelation the beast and the false prophet are simply “caught” (19:20). No battle is described. The Catechism: the kingdom comes “only by God’s victory over the final unleashing of evil” (CCC 677).

What is left to the reader

This study does not name anyone as the Antichrist or compute when he will come; the Lord’s word stands: “it is not for you to know times or seasons” (CCC 673, quoting Acts 1:7). But the same Lord told his disciples to watch, and he rebuked those who could read the sky and not the signs of the times. St. John wrote of antichrists so that his readers would recognize them. The Catechism gives the mark to look for. Applying it is the work of every Christian’s conscience.

For reflection today

The Catechism’s test is simple: any promise of salvation that asks me to give up the truth about Christ. Where am I offered “an apparent solution” at that price?

Keep watch

Use the Catechism’s test on what you hear and read. Is someone promising to solve humanity’s problems at the price of the truth about Christ? Is a person, a party or a movement asking for the trust that belongs to God? The Church names no one, and she leaves you free, and responsible, to recognize the pattern when you meet it. Test what you conclude, and state it with no more confidence than your evidence allows. See How to Test a Reading of Revelation.
