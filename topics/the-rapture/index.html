Is the Rapture in Revelation? A Catholic Answer

01 / INTRODUCTION

Where the idea comes from.

Is the rapture in the Catholic Bible? The word is not, but the event Paul describes is: when Christ returns in glory, the faithful who are alive will be 'caught up' to meet him (1 Thessalonians 4:17). What the Catholic Bible does not teach is a secret rapture of believers before a great tribulation.

The word ‘rapture’ comes from the Latin rapiemur, ‘we shall be caught up,’ in 1 Thessalonians 4:17: ‘we who are alive, who are left, will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air.’ All Christians believe this will happen. The question is when, and how.

The idea of a separate, secret rapture of believers before a seven-year tribulation was developed in the 1830s by the Anglo-Irish preacher John Nelson Darby. It spread through the Scofield Reference Bible (1909) and, more recently, the Left Behind novels. Earlier Christian writers do not describe a two-stage return of Christ.

The Catholic Church professes in the Creed that Christ ‘will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead.’ His return will be one event, public and visible: ‘every eye will see him’ (Rev 1:7). Before it the Church will pass through a final trial (CCC 675), following her Lord through his death and resurrection (CCC 677). Believers are not removed from history before the trial; they are kept faithful through it.

02 / THE SYMBOLS

The key passages.

ONE RETURN
01

1 THESSALONIANS 4:16–17

CAUGHT UP TO MEET THE LORD

Paul describes a loud command, an archangel’s voice and God’s trumpet: hardly a secret event. The Greek word for ‘meet’ was used for citizens going out to welcome a visiting king and escort him back into their city. Believers rise to greet Christ as he comes, not to leave with him.

Read 1 Thessalonians 4
02

REVELATION 4:1

‘COME UP HERE’

Some read John’s call into heaven as a picture of the Church raptured before the tribulation. In the text, only John is called up, to receive a vision. The Church remains on earth throughout Revelation, as the sealed servants, the two witnesses and the Woman in the desert.

Chapter 4
03

MATTHEW 24:39–41

ONE TAKEN, ONE LEFT

In context, Jesus compares his coming to the flood, which ‘carried them all away.’ Those ‘taken’ are swept away by judgment, not rescued; the call is to be ready. The parable is about watchfulness, not a secret disappearance.

Read Matthew 24
04

CCC 675–677

THE FINAL TRIAL

The Catechism teaches that before Christ’s second coming the Church ‘must pass through a final trial that will shake the faith of many believers’ (CCC 675). Revelation’s saints conquer ‘by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony’ (12:11), not by escape.

Chapter 13
05

REVELATION 1:7

EVERY EYE WILL SEE HIM

Revelation opens with Christ coming on the clouds, seen by all, as Jesus foretold (Matt 24:27, 30). His return is as public as lightning across the sky.

Chapter 1
06

REVELATION 22:20

COME, LORD JESUS

The Christian hope is not to escape the world but to welcome the Lord. The Church prays Marana tha at every Mass, ‘as we await the blessed hope and the coming of our Savior, Jesus Christ.’

Chapter 22
READ WITH CARE

Belief in a pre-tribulation rapture is held sincerely by many evangelical and Pentecostal Christians, and Catholics should discuss it with charity: it comes from a real longing for Christ and from taking his promises seriously. The Church does not teach it, and it is not found in Revelation itself. Catholic hope rests on the return of Christ in glory, the resurrection of the body and the life of the world to come, without timelines or date-setting (Matt 24:36).
