REVELATION 2:01–07

Faithful work, forgotten love

Faithful work, forgotten love.

01To the angel of the church in Ephesus: the one who holds the seven stars and walks among the seven golden lampstands says this.

02I know your works, your labor and your endurance. You cannot bear the wicked, and you tested those who called themselves apostles and found them false.

03You have endured and suffered for my name, and you have not grown weary.

04Yet I hold this against you: you have lost the love you had at first.

05Remember how far you have fallen, repent, and do the works you did at first. Otherwise I will come and remove your lampstand from its place.

06But you have this in your favor: you hate the works of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate.

07Whoever has ears ought to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. To the victor I will give the right to eat from the tree of life in the paradise of God.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The letters are addressed to real churches in seven cities of Roman Asia, and the USCCB notes read each against its city’s setting. Every letter follows the same shape: an address to the church’s angel; a title of Christ taken from the vision in chapter 1; praise and/or blame (“I know your works”); a call to repent; and a closing exhortation with a promise “to the victor.” Ephesus was the leading city of the province and the regional center of the Church. Its Christians are commended for discernment, but they have let their first love cool.

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 2:08–11

Poor, yet rich

Poor, yet rich.

08To the angel of the church in Smyrna: the first and the last, who died and came to life, says this.

09I know your tribulation and your poverty, though you are rich. I know the slander of those who oppose you.

10Do not be afraid of what you are about to suffer. Some of you will be thrown into prison to be tested, and you will face tribulation for ten days. Remain faithful until death, and I will give you the crown of life.

11Whoever has ears ought to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. The victor will not be harmed by the second death.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Smyrna, a prosperous city loyal to Rome, had a church that was poor and slandered. It receives no rebuke at all. Verse 9 speaks of slander from “those who claim to be Jews and are not.” The USCCB notes describe a local conflict in which some members of Smyrna’s Jewish community accused Christians before Roman authorities. The Church reads such passages in their first-century setting and firmly rejects any use of them against the Jewish people (Vatican II, Nostra Aetate, no. 4). This guide follows that teaching.

ORIGINAL VERSE SUMMARIES
REVELATION 2:12–17

Faithful where Satan’s throne is

Faithful where Satan’s throne is.

12To the angel of the church in Pergamum: the one with the sharp two-edged sword says this.

13I know you live where Satan’s throne is, yet you hold fast to my name. You did not deny your faith even when Antipas, my faithful witness, was put to death among you.

14Yet some among you hold to the teaching of Balaam, who led Israel to eat food sacrificed to idols and to commit immorality.

15Some likewise hold to the teaching of the Nicolaitans.

16So repent. Otherwise I will come quickly and fight against them with the sword of my mouth.

17Whoever has ears ought to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. To the victor I will give some of the hidden manna, and a white stone with a new name written on it, known only to the one who receives it.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Pergamum was a center of pagan worship. The notes suggest “Satan’s throne” refers to emperor worship and pagan cults, possibly the great altar of Zeus. Antipas, otherwise unknown, had already died for the faith there. Three letters warn against teachers named “Nicolaitans,” “Balaam” and “Jezebel.” The USCCB notes read them as advocates of accommodation with pagan society: eating food sacrificed to idols at trade-guild banquets and loosening Christian morals.

REVELATION 2:18–29

Growing love, dangerous tolerance

Growing love, dangerous tolerance.

18To the angel of the church in Thyatira: the Son of God, whose eyes are like a flame of fire and whose feet are like polished bronze, says this.

19I know your works, your love, faith, service and endurance, and that your last works are greater than your first.

20Yet I hold this against you: you tolerate the woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophet and leads my servants into immorality and eating food sacrificed to idols.

21I gave her time to repent, but she refuses.

22I will cast her onto a sickbed, and plunge those who commit adultery with her into great distress, unless they repent of her works.

23I will strike her children dead. Then all the churches will know that I search hearts and minds, and I will give each of you what your works deserve.

24To the rest of you in Thyatira who do not hold this teaching and have not learned the so-called deep secrets of Satan, I place no further burden on you,

25except to hold fast to what you have until I come.

26To the victor who keeps my works to the end, I will give authority over the nations.

27He will rule them with an iron rod, shattering them like clay pots,

28just as I received authority from my Father. And I will give him the morning star.

29Whoever has ears ought to hear what the Spirit says to the churches.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Thyatira, a smaller trade city, is praised for growing in love and service but rebuked for tolerating a false prophet. Balaam and Jezebel are Old Testament names for those who led Israel into idolatry (Num 25:1–3; 31:16; 1 Kgs 16:31). The danger in these churches is less persecution than quiet assimilation.

The crown of life

Smyrna’s faithfulness “until death” earns the victor’s wreath. The second death, final separation from God (20:14), cannot touch those who share in Christ’s first death and resurrection.

REVELATION 2:17

Hidden manna and the white stone

The manna of the Exodus, the bread from heaven (Ps 78:24–25), is the food of eternal life. The white stone and new name signify victory and the Christian’s new identity in Christ, known fully to God alone.

The morning star

At the end of the book Christ calls himself “the bright morning star” (22:16). To receive the morning star is to share in his resurrection, the dawn that follows every night of trial.

The letters address real first-century churches. Some interpreters have treated the seven churches as a timeline of seven ages of Church history, but the text and the USCCB notes give no warrant for that. Their warnings apply to every Christian community in every age.

WHERE IT FITS

The letters to the churches.

Each letter opens with a title from the vision of chapter 1: the one who holds the seven stars (2:1), the first and the last (2:8), the sword of his mouth (2:12), the eyes like fire (2:18). Chapter 3 completes the seven letters. The promises “to the victor” look ahead to the book’s ending, where the tree of life, the new name and the morning star reappear in the new Jerusalem (chapters 21–22).

04 / GOING DEEPER

What the Fathers said.

The sections above introduce the chapter. This one is for further study: how the Fathers of the Church and the early commentators read it, verse by verse. Read it now, or go on to the next chapter and come back to it.

The four letters were read from early on as letters to the whole Church. The readings below come from Origen, Tertullian, Clement of Alexandria, St. Cyprian, St. Methodius and St. Jerome, and from the commentators on the whole book: St. Victorinus, Tyconius, Apringius of Beja, Primasius, St. Caesarius of Arles, Oecumenius, St. Andrew of Caesarea and St. Bede.

01

REVELATION 2:1–7

Ephesus: the first love

“To the angel of the church.”

  • Origen: one who has the Holy Spirit may speak even to angels. Here a man writes to angels and charges them, so “angels too can be taught by human voices” (Homilies on Luke 23.7).
  • Oecumenius reads it otherwise: “the angel of the church” is a way of naming the church itself. A guardian angel has not sinned and needs no call to repent, and each letter ends with what the Spirit says to the churches, not to their angels (Commentary on the Apocalypse 2.1–7).
  • St. Bede: each church is praised in part and blamed in part, and what is written to one is said to all. Ephesus was not the only church that could lose its place, nor Pergamum the only seat of Satan. He takes the name Ephesus to mean “great fall” or “my will in it” (Explanation of the Apocalypse 2.1).

Praise, and the love that was lost. St. Victorinus: the praise is real and not small: toil, patience, the testing of false apostles. Yet even such people must be warned, so that they are not robbed of what is theirs. One falls from a height; and works of love must be practised to the very end, for this is “the principal commandment” (Commentary on the Apocalypse 2.1). Oecumenius: the Ephesians had obeyed the command to test the spirits (1 John 4:1); he thinks the false apostles were followers of Cerinthus, who lived in John’s day.

“Repent.”

  • Tertullian: you have sinned, and you can still be reconciled. The Spirit charges Ephesus with abandoned charity, Thyatira with fornication and idol-food, Sardis with unfinished works, Pergamum with false teaching, Laodicea with trust in riches, and warns them all to repent. He would not threaten the impenitent if he did not pardon the penitent (On Penitence 7–8).
  • St. Cyprian uses the same verse against those who would refuse penance to Christians who fell under persecution. The Lord exhorts the fallen to rise again through works, for almsgiving delivers from death (Tobit 4:10): not the death that Baptism has already put out, but the death that creeps in afterward through sins. He gave even “Jezebel” time to repent (2:21). He would not call to repentance unless he promised pardon (Letter 55.22).
  • Apringius of Beja: remember your falls so as not to fall again; wash away your sins with tears, as the sinful woman, a figure of the Church, washed the feet of Jesus (Luke 7:44). The “first works” are those done in the earnestness of your first conversion (Tractate on the Apocalypse 2.5).

The lampstand removed. Oecumenius: “I will come” does not mean that God moves, since he fills all things; it means a change from patience to punishment. St. Caesarius of Arles: he says “move,” not “take away.” The lampstand is the one Christian people; in the same Church the evil are moved and the good made firm, and what is taken from the one is added to the other (Matthew 25:29) (Exposition on the Apocalypse, Homily 2).

The Nicolaitans.

  • Victorinus, our only witness for these details, says they were quarrelsome ministers who made a heresy under the name of Nicolaus: food offered to idols could be exorcised and eaten, and one who had committed fornication could be received back to peace on the eighth day (2.1).
  • Clement of Alexandria defends Nicolaus himself. His followers twisted a saying of his about being hard on the flesh. He meant that pleasures and desires are to be curbed; they took it as licence (Stromateis 2.118.3–5). Bede follows Clement and repeats his story: Nicolaus, reproached for jealousy over his beautiful wife, answered that anyone who wished might marry her, and the faithless turned this into a teaching of promiscuity. Bede adds that they held pagan fables about the beginning of the world and did not keep their food apart from what had been offered to idols. St. Irenaeus, by contrast, traces the sect to the deacon Nicolaus (Against Heresies 1.26.3).
  • Apringius takes the name to mean “a pouring out”: heretics are poured out of the vessel of truth, and they say “Peace, peace” when there is no peace (Jeremiah 8:11) (2.6).

“Whoever has ears.” Oecumenius: to have an ear is to obey. “The Spirit” is named because the visions are given through the Spirit, or because Christ, who is God, is spirit (John 4:24). St. Andrew of Caesarea: everyone has a bodily ear; only the spiritual person has the ear that was given to Isaiah (Isaiah 50:5) (Commentary on the Apocalypse 2.7).

The tree of life.

  • Tertullian: persecution is a contest, and the one who gives the crown sets its terms. The prizes are announced here, in the promises to the victor (On Flight in Time of Persecution 1.5).
  • St. Methodius: the tree that paradise once bore the Church now bears for all, and its fruit is faith. The tree is Wisdom (Proverbs 3:18). The law and the prophets bore good fruit; the fruit of the Gospel is better (Banquet of the Ten Virgins 9.3).
  • Tyconius: the fruit of the tree of life is the fruit of the cross. The Church is the paradise, and there Christ, the Wisdom of God who hung on the tree, gives the faithful the food of life, “the sacrament of the celestial bread” (Commentary on the Apocalypse 2.7).
  • Bede: the tree of life is Christ, by the sight of whom holy souls are fed in the heavenly paradise and in the body of the Church now.
  • Apringius and Andrew: the victor freely eats of the tree from which Adam was barred; it is eternal life, and it is Christ, who is “true God and eternal life” (1 John 5:20).
02

REVELATION 2:8–11

Smyrna: poor, and rich

The name and the title. Bede: Smyrna means “myrrh,” the mortification of the flesh, and the letter is about bearing persecution (2.8). Oecumenius: he is “first” in his divinity and “last” in his incarnation; he entered death and by death destroyed death (2.8–11).

“I know your poverty, but you are rich.” Victorinus: for such people there are riches hidden with him (2.2). Apringius: he names the grace of poverty; the Church despises present things to gain what is to come, and is rich in faith (2.9). Oecumenius: you have Christ as a wealthy patron, who for us became poor and took the form of a slave (Philippians 2:7). Andrew: in the things of the Spirit you are rich, with the treasure hidden in the field of your heart (Matthew 13:44) (2.9).

“Those who claim to be Jews and are not.” Tyconius says the verse is addressed to every church and reads it of false Christians inside the Church, who have religion outwardly only; for the true circumcision is of the heart (Romans 2:29; Philippians 3:3). He adds that we are not to abandon them: the Lord bore with Judas to the end (2.9). Apringius reads those who claim to confess God and do not. Oecumenius notes that Judah means “confession,” so that the name belongs to the one who confesses Christ. The Church does not read this verse as a charge against the Jewish people (Nostra Aetate 4).

“Ten days.”

  • Apringius: set beside the blessedness that does not end, present evils are as short as ten days (Romans 8:18). He expects more suffering when the Antichrist comes, and notes that the Church suffers even now (2.10).
  • Primasius: the ten days are the whole of this life, in which the devil never stops making war on the Church. The law is summed up in ten commandments. The five bodily senses, counted for both sexes, make ten, and victory comes when by God’s grace they are subdued. And the seven days of time, with the threefold temptation, make ten. The Lord, the second Adam, met those three temptations: bread, vainglory on the pinnacle of the temple, and the kingdoms of the world (Luke 4:1–13). So the enmity between the woman and the serpent (Genesis 3:15) is the Church’s own battle (Commentary on the Apocalypse 2.10).
  • Bede reports that some took the ten days for the ten persecutions from Nero to Diocletian.

“Remain faithful until death.” St. Cyprian asks that care be taken for the bodies of those who die in prison without having been tortured. They are no less than the martyrs: each offered himself to the torments, and “the torments failed him,” not he them (Letter 12.1). Primasius: the words are spoken to the whole Church. He reads St. Paul’s saying about the woman who “will be saved” if she continues in faith, love and holiness (1 Timothy 2:15) as a figure of the Church.

The second death.

  • Victorinus: the punishment of hell.
  • Oecumenius: everyone, just and sinner alike, dies the first death, the parting of soul and body (Genesis 3:19). The second is the death that sin brings.
  • St. Fulgentius of Ruspe: there is a first resurrection here, when grace turns the heart from unbelief to faith and from evil to a holy life, and a second in the body, when that change is completed in glory. Whoever does not want the second death should hasten to share in the first resurrection (Revelation 20:6) (On the Forgiveness of Sins 2.12.3–4).
  • Primasius: Adam brought death by eating from the forbidden tree. Christ watered the tree of the cross with the water and blood from his side, and said, “I have conquered the world” (John 16:33). Whoever conquers, conquers in him (2.11).
03

REVELATION 2:12–17

Pergamum: where Satan’s throne is

The name and the sword. Bede takes Pergamum to mean “dividing their horns”: by his judgment Christ separates the strength of the faithful from the deceit of the Nicolaitans (2.12). Andrew: the city was given to idolatry, and those who stayed faithful are praised. The sword is the word of the Gospel, which cuts the heart and divides believer from unbeliever, or it is judgment (2.12–13). Victorinus: these are Christians strong in faith and unafraid of persecution; the sword is aimed at the few among them who lean toward forbidden indulgence (2.3).

Satan’s throne. Tyconius: this is said to every church, since the tempter is everywhere and “the whole world is under the power of the evil one” (1 John 5:19). His throne is the people he possesses through wickedness (2.13). Caesarius says the same: Satan dwells everywhere in his own body, the proud and the evil.

Antipas. Andrew: Antipas was a most courageous martyr of Pergamum. Andrew had read the account of his martyrdom, which is now lost (2.13). Tyconius turns the verse on the reader: Christ is “killed” among many, by those who do not believe that he rose and by those who deny him through their sins.

The teaching of Balaam.

  • Victorinus: Balaam advised the king of Moab how to make Israel stumble (Numbers 25:1–2). Some do the same now, and corrupt others “under the pretext of mercy.”
  • Apringius: Balaam means “without a people.” He is a figure of the adversary, who gathers no one for salvation and is glad only when he destroys. Balak he takes to mean “throwing down” or “devouring”: Israel was thrown down before the idol and devoured by pleasure (2.14).
  • Andrew: the city had two evils: many pagans, and among the believers the Nicolaitans, sown like weeds among wheat (Matthew 13:24–30). The devil is the Balaam of the mind, working through the Balaam of history (2.14–15).
  • Tyconius: eating and fornication are the two things hypocrites strive to enjoy. Idolatry is fornication of the spirit (Psalm 73:27) (2.14).

“Repent.” St. Jerome argues from this letter that those who return from heresy need repentance and not a second Baptism. The Lord rebukes Ephesus, Pergamum and Thyatira and calls them to repent; he does not say, let those baptized among the Nicolaitans be baptized again (Dialogue Against the Luciferians 24). The Church teaches that Baptism cannot be repeated (Catechism of the Catholic Church 1272). Primasius: the sword of his mouth is the commandments of both Testaments, by which the contemptuous are judged (Romans 2:12) (2.16).

The hidden manna.

  • Tyconius: the manna is the invisible Bread that came down from heaven and was made man, so that we might eat the bread of angels (Psalm 78:25). The manna in the desert was its figure. Those who ate it spiritually, as Moses did, received the same life (1 Corinthians 10:3). So now the Lord’s body does not profit those who receive it unworthily, who eat and drink judgment on themselves (1 Corinthians 11:29) (2.17).
  • Andrew: the Bread of Life who came down from heaven and became our food; and also the good things still to come down from heaven, with the new Jerusalem (2.17).
  • Victorinus: immortality.

The white stone and the new name.

  • Victorinus: the white stone is adoption as a child of God; the new name is “Christian.”
  • Primasius: the stone is a precious gem, like the pearl for which the merchant sold all he had (Matthew 13:45–46). The new name is “Christian” (Isaiah 62:2), given in Baptism and sealed in martyrdom. Hypocrites bear it outwardly and cannot enter into what it means (2 Timothy 3:5; 1 John 2:4) (2.17).
  • Caesarius: the stone is the body made white by Baptism. The name written on it is the knowledge of the Son of man, which no one has except by revelation (Homily 2).
  • Oecumenius: a stone of victory, bright with glory. What it holds cannot yet be told: “What eye has not seen, and ear has not heard” (1 Corinthians 2:9) (2.12–17).
04

REVELATION 2:18–29

Thyatira: the one who searches hearts

The name and the church. Bede takes Thyatira to mean “for sacrifice,” since the saints offer their bodies as a living sacrifice (Romans 12:1) (2.18). Victorinus: these are the faithful who do good works every day and greater ones as time goes on. Yet some among them too easily grant an unlawful peace and listen to new prophecies (2.4).

Eyes like fire, feet like bronze. Apringius: his gaze discerns all things, and his flesh is without stain, bright as bronze in the fire (2.18). Oecumenius: the eyes are a warning to sinners; the feet are the steadfastness of faith in him. Andrew: the eyes give light to the just and punishment to sinners. The feet are the fragrance of Christ among those being saved (2 Corinthians 2:15), and the union of his divinity and humanity, undivided and unmixed (2.18).

“I know your works.” Oecumenius: he says “I know” where we expect “I praise,” in the sense in which God is said to know Moses, his intimate friend (Exodus 33:12). “Service” is help given to those in need, and the later works surpass the first because they grew in obedience (2.18–29). Apringius looks to the end: when the man of sin comes, countless saints will be consecrated in their own blood (2.19).

“That woman Jezebel.”

  • Caesarius: the rebuke falls on leaders of the churches who fail to apply the Church’s discipline to those who do evil. It may also mean heretics, for many heresies flatter themselves with the name of Christian (Homily 2).
  • Oecumenius: a woman of that time, described on the pattern of Ahab’s wife. Her “fornication” is either bodily or the apostasy from God that the prophets call by that name (Psalm 106:39; Jeremiah 3:9). The Lord does not will the death of a sinner; he gave her time to repent.
  • Andrew: the Nicolaitan heresy, called Jezebel as a figure for its impiety and licence. It draws the simple back toward the idolatry they had fled (2.19–20). St. Epiphanius thought of the Montanist prophetesses.

The sickbed. Primasius: the bed is the false security of sinners who have done wrong and suffered nothing. They put off penance until ruin comes on them suddenly: “the smugness of fools destroys them” (Proverbs 1:32) (2.22). Apringius: the bed is the pleasure of this world, and her “children” are her disciples. He takes the name Jezebel to mean “dung heap” or “flow of blood,” a picture of sin itself (2.22). Andrew: union with heresy is compared to union with a harlot, and the threat stands unless they turn back through repentance.

“I am the searcher of hearts and minds.”

  • St. Cyprian: no one escapes God as judge. He sees what is hidden, and he will judge not only our deeds but our words and thoughts, the will conceived in a heart still closed (1 Samuel 16:7; Jeremiah 23:23–24) (The Lapsed 27).
  • Bede: others can know our works and words; only the Lord knows what we intend by them and what we take pleasure in. Why is the one who punishes open sins called the knower of secrets, unless fornication and idolatry are found even in the smallest sins? “Children, be on your guard against idols” (1 John 5:21) (2.23).
  • Tyconius: the churches know this by faith, not only after the event. The death he threatens is spiritual. As David’s victory showed all Israel that the Lord saves (1 Samuel 17:46–47), so the Church sees by faith what becomes of those who will not be subject to the truth (2.23).

“Hold fast to what you have.” Tyconius: he speaks to those who kept their minds from the devil’s teaching. They were taught to follow no man, only the truth of the faith. The just do not “know” the teaching of Satan, even if they hear it; and God does not “know” evildoers (Matthew 7:23) (2.24). Andrew: to the simple he says, you are no match for clever talkers and do not know “the deep secrets of Satan”; I ask of you no war of words, only that you keep the teaching you received (2.24–25). Victorinus: the “further burden” he will not lay on them is the observances of the law.

Authority over the nations. Tyconius: the Church has this power in Christ. Whoever holds fast to his body is co-heir with him and receives what he received, for God has given us all things with him (Romans 8:32) (2.26–27). Andrew: it is the authority over five cities or ten that the Lord promises in the Gospel (Luke 19:17), or the judgment in which believers condemn the unbelieving, as the Ninevites will (Matthew 12:41). “As I received from my Father” is said of his human nature. Apringius: the saints will judge the fallen angels.

The morning star.

  • Victorinus, Tyconius and Apringius: the first resurrection, and Christ himself. The morning star drives off the night and announces the day; Christ is the light that evening never overtakes.
  • Oecumenius: the fallen morning star of Isaiah (Isaiah 14:12), that is, Satan, whom the Lord puts under the feet of his servants (Romans 16:20; Psalm 91:13).
  • Andrew allows both. It may be the one who fell and will be crushed under the saints’ feet, or the morning star that rises in the hearts of the faithful (2 Peter 1:19), the light of Christ. Scripture uses one image both ways, as the lion of Judah is Christ and the lion from Bashan the Antichrist. John the Baptist and Elijah are both light-bearers, the forerunners of the first and of the second rising of the Sun of righteousness (2.28–29).
