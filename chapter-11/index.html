REVELATION 11:01–06

Protected to proclaim.

Protected to proclaim.

01John is given a measuring rod like a staff and told, “Get up and measure the temple of God and the altar, and count those who worship there.

02But leave out the outer court; do not measure it, for it has been given over to the nations, who will trample the holy city for forty-two months.

03I will give my two witnesses power to prophesy for twelve hundred and sixty days, dressed in sackcloth.”

04They are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth.

05If anyone tries to harm them, fire comes from their mouths and consumes their enemies; whoever tries to harm them will be destroyed.

06They have power to shut the sky so that no rain falls while they prophesy, and power to turn the waters into blood and to strike the earth with every kind of plague as often as they wish.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

Chapter 11 completes the interlude that began in chapter 10 and then sounds the seventh trumpet. The USCCB notes describe the second scene: the measuring of the temple and the two witnesses, “whose martyrdom means that the kingdom of God is about to be inaugurated.” The temple and altar, in the notes, symbolize the new Israel, and the worshipers are Christians. Measuring them (compare Ezek 40; Zech 2:5–6) means that God will preserve his faithful people, even while the outer court is trampled. The forty-two months, the same as 1,260 days or three and a half years, recall the persecution of the Jews under Antiochus IV (Dan 7:25; 12:7) and became a symbol of every period of trial for God’s people.

REVELATION 11:07–14

Three and a half days.

Three and a half days.

07When they have finished their testimony, the beast that comes up from the abyss will make war on them, conquer them and kill them.

08Their bodies will lie in the street of the great city, which is called symbolically “Sodom” and “Egypt,” where their Lord was crucified.

09For three and a half days people from every nation, tribe, language and people gaze at their bodies and refuse to let them be buried.

10Those who dwell on the earth gloat over them, celebrate and exchange gifts, because these two prophets had tormented them.

11But after the three and a half days, the breath of life from God enters them, and they stand on their feet. Great fear falls on those who see them.

12They hear a loud voice from heaven say, “Come up here,” and they go up to heaven in a cloud while their enemies watch.

13At that moment there is a great earthquake. A tenth of the city falls and seven thousand people are killed; the rest are terrified and give glory to the God of heaven.

14The second woe has passed; the third is coming soon.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The USCCB notes admit the two witnesses “cannot readily be identified.” Some have seen Moses and Elijah, whose miracles they echo (Exod 7:17–20; 1 Kgs 17:1), or Peter and Paul. The notes conclude that “most probably they refer to the universal church, especially the Christian martyrs,” two because the Law required two witnesses (Deut 19:15). Like their Lord, they are killed in “the great city,” lie dead for a short time, and are raised and taken up in a cloud. The Church’s witness may be silenced, but never for long.

REVELATION 11:15–19

He will reign forever.

He will reign forever.

15The seventh angel blows his trumpet, and loud voices in heaven say, “The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Anointed, and he will reign forever and ever.”

16The twenty-four elders seated on their thrones before God fall on their faces and worship God,

17saying: “We thank you, Lord God almighty, who are and who were, for you have taken your great power and begun to reign.

18The nations raged, but your wrath has come, and the time to judge the dead, to reward your servants the prophets, the holy ones and those who fear your name, small and great alike, and to destroy those who destroy the earth.”

19Then God’s temple in heaven is opened, and the ark of his covenant is seen in the temple. There are flashes of lightning, rumblings, thunder, an earthquake and heavy hail.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT / USCCB NOTES

The seventh trumpet brings no new plague, only a proclamation: “The kingdom of the world now belongs to our Lord and to his Anointed, and he will reign forever and ever” (11:15). Then the heavenly temple opens and the ark of the covenant appears, lost since the Babylonian conquest. In the next verse John sees “a woman clothed with the sun” (12:1). The Church reads these verses together on the Solemnity of the Assumption, and Catholic tradition has long called Mary the Ark of the Covenant: she carried within her the Word made flesh. The Catechism calls her “the ark of the covenant, the place where the glory of the Lord dwells” (CCC 2676).

02 / THE SYMBOLS

Read the signs in their scriptural and historical context.

SILENCED ≠ DEFEATED
01

REVELATION 11:1–2

The measuring rod

Measuring marks what God claims and protects (Ezek 40; Zech 2:5–6). The Church may be trampled from outside, but her worship and her people are kept safe in God’s hands.

Read Zechariah 2
02

REVELATION 11:2–3

Forty-two months, 1,260 days

Three and a half years, half of seven: a limited time of trial, modeled on the persecution under Antiochus (Dan 7:25). Real suffering, but with an end God has set.

Read our page on the forty-two months
03

REVELATION 11:4

Two olive trees, two lampstands

Drawn from Zechariah 4, where two anointed ones stand beside the Lord. The lampstands recall 1:20, where they are the churches: the witnesses are the Church shining before the world.

Read Zechariah 4
04

REVELATION 11:9–12

Three and a half days

The witnesses lie dead for a short, symbolic time, then rise and ascend like Elijah (2 Kgs 2:11) and like their Lord. The world’s victory over God’s witnesses is always brief.

Read Ezekiel 37
05

REVELATION 11:19

The ark of the covenant

The ark held the tablets of the Law and was where God’s glory dwelt. It appears in heaven just before the Woman clothed with the sun. Catholic tradition sees Mary as the new Ark, who carried the Word made flesh (CCC 2676).

Read Luke 1
READ WITH CARE

The two witnesses are often identified with two specific people expected to appear in Jerusalem at the end of time. The USCCB notes say they “cannot readily be identified” and most probably represent the whole Church, especially the martyrs. “The great city” is not simply the earthly Jerusalem; the notes read it symbolically as Rome, the city that rejects God’s witnesses.

WHERE IT FITS

From witness to reign.

Chapter 11 ends the first half of Revelation’s visions. The seventh trumpet announces the reign of God, and the third woe it brings unfolds in the bowls of chapters 15–16. The ark seen in the heavenly temple (11:19) opens directly onto the great sign of the Woman clothed with the sun (12:1), where the book begins again from a new angle: the cosmic battle between the Dragon and the Woman’s child. The forty-two months return in chapters 12 and 13, and the beast from the abyss reappears in chapter 13.
